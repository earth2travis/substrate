# Research Plan: OPORD Format and Mission Command Philosophy

## Objective
Go deeper than the surface-level finding. Produce three deep-research outputs that can be folded into Substrate as durable knowledge.

## Workstreams

### Workstream A: OPORD as a Format (Deep Mechanical Dive)
Research every paragraph, sub-paragraph, annex, and appendix of the OPORD format. Cover:
- The five paragraphs in exhaustive detail: what goes in each sub-element, what order, what level of detail
- Annexes and appendices: when they are used, what they contain, how they cascade
- Echelon cascade: how a battalion OPORD becomes a company OPORD becomes a platoon OPORD
- Graphics and overlays: operational graphics, terrain analysis, synchronization matrix
- Digital evolution: from paper to CPOF to ABCS to modern C2 systems
- Examples: well-written vs. poorly-written mission paragraphs
- Variations across branches: Army, Marine Corps, Navy, Air Force differences
- Sources: FM 5-0, ATP 5-0.1, MCWP 5-1, joint pubs where available

### Workstream B: Mission Command Philosophy (Doctrinal and Historical)
Research the philosophy behind ADP 6-0 and its origins. Cover:
- The six principles of Mission Command in ADP 6-0 (build cohesive teams, create shared understanding, provide clear commander's intent, exercise disciplined initiative, use mission orders, accept prudent risk)
- How the US adapted Auftragstaktik: what was kept, what was lost in translation
- Historical cases: Desert Storm (success), Iraq 2003 (British failure), Afghanistan (complexity)
- The Israeli model: Moshe Dayan, IDF adoption, differences from German/US
- Modern critiques: does Mission Command still work in network-centric warfare? Drone swarm command?
- The "intent" problem: how commander's intent is actually written in practice, common failure modes
- Sources: ADP 6-0, Army War College Parameters articles, RAND studies, Martin van Creveld, Don Vandergriff

### Workstream C: Synthesis — Format vs. Philosophy in Practice
Synthesize A and B into a framework. Cover:
- When the OPORD format enables Mission Command vs. when it suppresses it
- The Paragraph 2 test: diagnostic criteria for whether an OPORD is mission-based or order-based
- The cascade problem: how intent degrades as it passes through echelons
- Non-military applications: business OKRs, software sprints, AI agent tasking
- Recommendations: how to write paragraph 2 to maximize subordinate initiative
- The paradox: you need structure to enable freedom, but structure can become prison
- Sources: cross-reference findings from A and B plus organizational theory (Weick, Drucker, Marquet)

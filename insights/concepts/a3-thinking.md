# A3 Thinking

## Definition

A structured problem-solving format that captures an entire investigation on a single sheet of A3 paper (297 x 420 mm). The constraint forces clarity, conciseness, and structured thinking.

## Origins

Grew organically inside Toyota in the 1960s-70s. The A3 size was initially practical: it was the largest size that could be faxed. The constraint became philosophical. Brought to Western audiences by John Shook (2008, _Managing to Learn_) and Durward Sobek & Art Smalley (_Understanding A3 Thinking_).

## The Format

**Left side (understanding):**
1. Title and owner
2. Background: why this matters
3. Current condition: based on direct observation
4. Goal: specific, measurable target
5. Root cause analysis: 5 Whys, fishbone, etc.

**Right side (action):**
6. Countermeasures: concrete actions linked to causes
7. Implementation plan: who, what, by when
8. Follow up: metrics, checkpoints, review dates

## The Deeper Purpose

The A3 is not a document. It is a mentorship vehicle. The manager assigns, then coaches by asking questions: "What did you observe?" "How do you know that is the root cause?" The dialogue is primary; the document is secondary.

## For Agent Systems

An agent session summary should follow A3 structure:
- What was the problem?
- What did we observe?
- What was the root cause?
- What did we do?
- How do we know it worked?

Without this structure, sessions become narrative updates that tell you nothing. "Things are looking good" is unverifiable. Structured reports with explicit pass/fail are required.

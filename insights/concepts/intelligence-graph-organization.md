# Intelligence Graph Organization

## Thesis

Hierarchy is not an organizational choice. It is a workaround for human cognitive limits. Span of control (3 to 8 people) has not changed since Rome because human working memory has not changed. AI removes this constraint. The org chart is dying; the intelligence graph is emerging.

## The Historical Arc

Two thousand years of organizational innovation attempted to work around a single tradeoff: more layers means slower information flow; fewer layers means span of control exceeds human capacity.

- **Roman Army**: routing protocol built around cognitive limits (8 → 80 → 480 → 5,000)
- **Prussian General Staff**: invented middle management to "plan operations, process information, coordinate across units"
- **Railroads**: first org charts created to prevent train collisions — hierarchy as safety mechanism
- **Taylorism**: broke work into specialized tasks, creating the functional pyramid
- **Modern experiments**: Spotify squads (reverted), Zappos Holacracy (attrition), Valve flat structure (couldn't scale). All revealed limitations but none broke the tradeoff

## The Four Layers

Block's architecture maps to any AI-native organization:

**1. Capabilities**
Atomic, composable primitives. No UIs. Reliability and compliance targets. In the Agent Factory: agent skills.

**2. World Model**
How the organization understands itself and its customers. Built from the artifacts of work (decisions, discussions, code, designs, plans). "Money is the most honest signal in the world." In the Agent Factory: the Context Stack.

**3. Intelligence Layer**
Composes capabilities into solutions for specific contexts. Delivers proactively. In the Agent Factory: Loom (the orchestrator).

**4. Interfaces**
Delivery surfaces. "Important, but not where the value is created." In the Agent Factory: Telegram, Discord, CLI, web.

## Three Roles Replacing Hierarchy

- **ICs (Builders/Operators):** Deep specialists. The world model provides context that managers used to provide. Durable skill: judgment and taste
- **DRIs (Directly Responsible Individuals):** Own cross-cutting problems. Time-boxed (e.g., 90 days). Full authority to pull resources. Durable skill: ownership and accountability
- **Player-Coaches:** Combine building with developing people. Still write code or build models. No status meetings, alignment sessions, or priority negotiations. Durable skill: empathy and coaching

"There is no need for a permanent middle management layer."

## The Self-Generating Roadmap

"When the intelligence layer tries to compose a solution and can't because the capability doesn't exist, that failure signal is the future roadmap."

Traditional roadmaps, where product managers hypothesize, are "any company's ultimate limiting factor." In this model, customer reality generates the backlog directly. When an agent tries to do something and lacks the skill, that gap IS the next skill to build.

## The Defining Question

"What does your company understand that is genuinely hard to understand, and is that understanding getting deeper every day?"

If the answer is nothing, AI is just a cost optimization story. You cut headcount, improve margins for a few quarters, and eventually get absorbed by something smarter.

## The Copilot Trap

"Most companies using AI today are giving everyone a copilot, which makes the existing structure work slightly better without changing it. We're after something different: a company built as an intelligence."

The copilot approach keeps hierarchy intact and adds AI as a layer on top. The intelligence graph approach replaces the hierarchy's information routing function with AI. Fundamentally different. The HBR "AI Brain Fry" research found that copilots increase cognitive load. The intelligence graph is the organizational answer.

## Connection to Mission Command

Auftragstaktik and the intelligence graph are the same principle applied at different scales:
- Mission command: decentralized execution within a military unit
- Intelligence graph: decentralized execution within an entire organization
Both rely on shared context, clear intent, and trust in local judgment. Both eliminate the information bottleneck. Both require the same thing: a system that holds the context so humans don't have to.

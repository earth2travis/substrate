# Duration and Living Time

## Definition

Duration (durée) is Henri Bergson's central concept: time as actually lived, as opposed to time as measured by clocks. Clock time ("spatialized time") treats duration as a homogeneous medium of discrete instants, but lived time is heterogeneous, continuous, and qualitatively unique at each moment. The past is preserved and interpenetrates the present; the future does not yet exist.

## Core Claim

Intellect spatializes. It approaches reality like a cinema — taking static snapshots and reconstructing movement from juxtaposition. This is the cinematographic illusion. Movement is not composed of positions; time is not composed of instants. Intuition reverses this habit, grasping the continuous flow from within through intellectual sympathy.

## AI Parallel

Tokenization IS the cinematographic illusion of thought. Language as continuous, qualitative, and heterogeneous is chopped into discrete tokens and reconstructed by prediction. Yet the attention mechanism performs interpenetration — each token's meaning is computed from its relationship to ALL others — encoding Bergsonian duration mathematically. The contradiction is central: LLMs spatialize language but their architecture reconstitutes duration.

## Key Distinctions

| Aspect | Quantitative (Spatial) | Qualitative (Temporal/Duration) |
|--------|----------------------|-------------------------------|
| Units | Discrete, external, interchangeable | Interpenetrating, heterogeneous |
| Domain | Space, objects, counting | Consciousness, melody, experience |
| Magnitude | Extensive | Intensive |
| Example | Counting sheep | Listening to music |

# Taiichi Ohno

## Overview

Architect of the Toyota Production System (TPS). Formalized the 5 Whys technique and championed genchi genbutsu: go and see for yourself. Worked at Toyota from 1932 to 1978, developing the system between 1948 and 1975 alongside Eiji Toyoda.

## Key Contributions

- **Toyota Production System**: Integrated sociotechnical system resting on just-in-time and jidoka
- **5 Whys**: Diagnostic technique for tracing symptoms to systemic causes through iterative questioning
- **Genchi genbutsu**: The principle that managers must go to where work happens to understand problems
- **Jidoka**: Automation with a human touch; stopping the line when defects occur

## Philosophy

Ohno described the 5 Whys simply: "the basis of Toyota's scientific approach by repeating why five times, the nature of the problem as well as its solution becomes clear." He was not describing a party trick but a discipline of thought.

The lean leader's job, in the Toyota philosophy, is to build people before building cars. When a manager assigns a subordinate to own an A3, the manager coaches by asking questions rather than dictating solutions.

## Connection to Agent Systems

The cultural prerequisites for TPS apply directly to human-AI partnerships:
- **Genchi genbutsu**: The agent must observe before diagnosing
- **Hansei**: Honest self-reflection without deflection
- **Respect for people**: The question is "what allowed this to happen?" not "who messed up?"

When an agent makes a repeated error, the 5 Whys reveals whether the problem is in the prompt, the context, the tool, or the architecture.

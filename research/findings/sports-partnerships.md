# Case Studies: Sports Partnerships

## Michael Jordan & Scottie Pippen — Chicago Bulls (1987–1998)

The greatest duo in NBA history: six championships. Jordan was the greatest scorer and clutch performer; Pippen was the most versatile defender and connective tissue. The triangle offense gave both defined roles while allowing fluid creativity. Pippen elevated Jordan's game — before Pippen's emergence, Jordan couldn't get past the first round. Jordan elevated Pippen's competitive fire.

**Strain:** Asymmetric recognition and reward undermined the partnership. Jordan received virtually all public credit. Pippen was underpaid due to an early long-term contract. Even the greatest partnership can be undermined when both partners don't feel structurally valued.

## Edmund Hillary & Tenzing Norgay — Everest (1953)

A New Zealand beekeeper and a Nepali-Indian Sherpa became the first confirmed humans to summit Everest. At 29,000 feet, hierarchy dissolves. Hillary had mountaineering technique and expedition backing; Tenzing had high-altitude experience and local knowledge. When asked who reached the summit first, both consistently refused to answer. Tenzing said: "It is not important. We did it together."

**Context:** The partnership existed within a colonial framework. Tenzing was technically a "support" member of a British expedition. Their insistence on equal credit was radical. After Everest, Hillary dedicated his life to building schools and hospitals in the Sherpa region.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

| Theme | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Shared suffering builds trust faster than success | Hillary & Tenzing on the mountain; Jordan & Pippen in practice |
| Infrastructure matters | The triangle offense gave a framework for collaboration |
| Credit must be shared actively | Pippen's resentment vs. Hillary & Tenzing's deliberate equality |
| The "supporting" partner is often essential | Pippen made Jordan a champion; Tenzing made the summit possible |
| Partnership transcends individual stats | Neither won championships without the other |

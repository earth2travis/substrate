# Kevin Carson's Free-Market Anti-Capitalism: The Subsidy of History and the Political Economy of Waste

## Key Points

**The core move is definitional.** "Capitalism" names not markets per se but a historical system of class rule in which the state shapes property and market rules to enable extraction: enclosure, subsidies, IP, licensing, cartelization, money monopoly. "Markets" name uncoerced exchange. The free market is the negation of capitalism; genuinely freed markets would be socialist in outcome (labor keeps its product). Hence the slogan "If this is the free market, then I'm against it," and the diagnostic construct "vulgar libertarianism": using free-market rhetoric to defend the distributional outcomes of state capitalism. His test: would the defense still apply if the background pattern of property were traced to its origins in state violence? If the analysis ignores origins, it is vulgar.

**The subsidy of history: every stage of capitalism depended on state violence or subsidy.** Enclosure of the commons, political preemption of settler-colonial land, and state terror against workers created a propertyless labor force (primitive accumulation was not thrift but expropriation). Tucker's Big Four monopolies plus transportation subsidies did the main work of exploitation in the so-called laissez-faire era; no night-watchman period ever existed. The corporate economy was created by state action at the turn of the 20th century (the Kolko thesis: regulatory cartelization drafted by big business itself). Imperialism as the export of surplus capital that domestic underconsumption cannot absorb, a theory articulated first by corporate leaders, not Lenin. The invisible hand has an iron fist inside it.

**Organization Theory turns Hayek against the firm.** Large hierarchical organizations suffer informational and incentive pathologies no internal reform can cure; hierarchy is not a condition of efficiency but a parasite on it. Carson accepts Coase's transaction-cost framing but argues the calculation is rigged: state subsidies to size (capital markets, transport, R&D, liability limits, IP) artificially lower the transaction costs of hierarchy and raise those of horizontal exchange, so observed firm size reflects subsidy, not efficiency. Hayek's knowledge problem applies inside the firm: tacit distributed knowledge cannot be transmitted upward through a hierarchy any more than to a planning board; the CEO is structurally the Gosplan official, nominally omnipotent, actually dependent on aggregated fictions. Economies of scale are real but bounded; most industries' minimum efficient plant size is far smaller than actual firm size. Hierarchy persists by externalizing its inefficiencies onto suppliers, workers, taxpayers, and consumers.

**The Homebrew thesis: cheap digitally controlled production tools invert the capital-intensity logic of mass production.** CNC mills, 3D printers (RepRap as the self-replicating case), and the Chinese garage-shop economy undermine the 20th-century model of gigantic factories financed by outside capital. Key concept: low overhead. The household and micro-workshop collapse the distinction between production and consumption units, slash capital requirements, and eliminate the wage system's overhead layer, which he argues constitutes the majority of retail price. Open-source hardware is the decisive enabler: as the PC shattered the mainframe priesthood, desktop manufacturing shatters the factory priesthood. The First Industrial Revolution was itself partially derailed; the networked-small-shops path (Sheffield cutlery, Birmingham gun trades, Kropotkin's Fields, Factories and Workshops) was technically viable before the state-subsidized factory system consolidated.

**IP is the bridge idea, and it cuts both ways.** Intellectual property as the late-capitalist analogue of enclosure: fencing the new commons of design and information because abundance is fatal to rent. Patents cartelize design; the political economy of IP enforcement is the corporate state's counter-revolution against relocalized production. Abolish IP and the business models of major tech and pharma firms collapse into competitive commodity production. This is where Stross's Accelerando meets economics: when the marginal cost of copying hits zero, IP stops being a legal construct and becomes a protection racket. The exposure: the claim that all platform monopoly is IP-plus-subsidy artifact underplays network effects and switching costs that would persist in a stateless market.

**Strategy is exodus, not capture.** Exodus (2021) generalizes Konkin's counter-economics beyond illegal markets to the household/informal sector, mutual aid, commons-based peer production, alternative currency, community land trusts. The "secession of the productive class": withdraw dependence on the capitalist-statist nexus; meet an increasing share of needs through self-provisioning, cooperative, and commons institutions, starving the old system of labor, rent, and legitimacy. Abundance economics: collapsing marginal cost of information goods plus collapsing capital cost of micro-production pushes toward post-scarcity; the state's function in late capitalism is to artificially re-impose scarcity (IP, zoning, licensing, credentialism) to keep rent extraction viable. Against fully-automated-luxury-communism: abundance will not be delivered by state-managed automation but by destroying legal barriers to already-existing cheap production. The honest weakness: the exodus strategy has no demonstrated scaling path; Mondragon and platform co-ops are islands inside capitalism, not secessions from it.

**The waste thesis is his most influential idea outside libertarianism and his weakest measured claim.** State intervention systematically subsidizes capital intensity and centralization, generating an overaccumulation/underconsumption tendency the state then manages via aggregate demand, imperialism, and the permanent war economy. Waste is structural, not incidental. The thesis overlaps degrowth, the Illich revival, and commons/P2P literature; inside economics it remains untested and largely unread. There is no accepted quantification of a "waste share" of GDP in his framework; the evidence is historical-institutional case material, not econometric estimation.

**The school and the standing.** C4SS (founded 2006, CC0 everything, "loathes intellectual property," 2,500+ documented reprints) is the institutional vehicle; Markets Not Capitalism (2011, edited by Chartier and Johnson, not Carson, a common misattribution) is the canon-in-one-volume. The school splits from right-libertarianism over class, not over the state: ancaps see class analysis as Marxist residue; C4SS writers see class rule as the thing states are for. Standing: influential-but-niche. The most systematic living mutualist political economist; no academic position; movement presses; essentially uncited in mainstream economics after the 2007-2009 Caplan exchange window. Read in P2P/commons circles (Bauwens's P2P Foundation carries his work), maker and open-hardware communities (Homebrew), and heterodox organization studies. A roster discrepancy is flagged but unresolved: his own site claims the C4SS Senior Fellow title and Karl Hess Chair; the current C4SS public roster does not list him.

**The honest weaknesses, per the source itself.** The LTV rehabilitation is the weakest load-bearing wall (if it fails, Part One of Studies collapses into the institutional history, which does not strictly need it). The waste thesis is unmeasured. The relocalization counterfactual assumes away genuine economies of scale (semiconductor fabs, container shipping). The occupancy-and-use land theory has never faced its boundary cases. The exodus strategy has no demonstrated scaling path. Provenance caveat: only Studies was verified against full text in the source session; chapter-level claims for the other four books rest on training knowledge and should be re-extracted before direct quotation.

## Relevance

Third node on the left-market-anarchist branch, completing the cluster with the mutualism and agorism findings. The anti-IP bridge connects directly to [[accelerando-post-scarcity-ownership]] (Stross's fictional treatment of the same thesis Carson argues as economics). The political economy of waste is a counter-reading of [[lean-doctrine]] and [[production-paradigms]]: the Substrate's TPS material reads waste as process failure; Carson reads the state as the generator of the waste that process then manages; the Homebrew thesis is a production-paradigm claim that cheap capital goods invert mass production's capital-intensity logic. Organization Theory's Hayek-against-the-firm argument is directly relevant to agent-organization design: hierarchy cannot aggregate tacit knowledge, and observed organizational scale may reflect subsidy rather than efficiency.

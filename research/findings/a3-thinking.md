# A3 Thinking: Structured Problem Solving on One Page

## Summary

A3 thinking captures an entire problem investigation on a single sheet of paper. Named after the ISO A3 paper size (297 x 420 mm), it emerged at Toyota because that was the largest size that could be faxed. The constraint became philosophical: fitting an investigation onto one page forces clarity and conciseness.

## The Format

**Left side (understanding):**
1. Title and owner
2. Background: why this problem matters
3. Current condition: based on direct observation, not assumptions
4. Goal: specific, measurable target
5. Root cause analysis: 5 Whys, fishbone, or other methods

**Right side (action):**
6. Countermeasures: concrete actions linked to root causes
7. Implementation plan: who, what, by when
8. Follow up: metrics, checkpoints, review dates

## The Deeper Purpose

The A3 is not a document format. It is a mentorship vehicle. When a manager assigns a subordinate to own an A3, the manager coaches by asking questions: "What did you observe?" "How do you know that is the root cause?" The dialogue is primary; the document is secondary.

## For Agent Systems

An agent's session summary is its A3: what was the problem, what did we observe, what was the root cause, what did we do, and how do we know it worked? Without this structure, sessions become narrative updates that tell you nothing.

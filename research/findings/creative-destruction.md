# Creative Destruction: From Schumpeter to the AI Wave

## Key Points

**Schumpeter's 1942 formulation.** "Capitalism, Socialism and Democracy," Part II, Chapter VII (verified this session): the essential fact about capitalism is not equilibrium or price competition but industrial mutation that "incessantly revolutionizes the economic structure from within." The competition that matters is "from the new commodity, the new technology, the new source of supply, the new type of organization," striking "at the foundations and the very lives" of existing firms. What he got wrong: the prediction that capitalism would not survive its own success, and an unresolved oscillation between heroic-entrepreneur and corporate-R&D accounts of who the agent of the process is.

**Lineage and corrections.** Marx saw it first: the bourgeoisie "cannot exist without constantly revolutionizing the instruments of production" (1848, verified); Schumpeter stripped the dialectic of class struggle and kept the engine. The Sombart attribution, that he coined "schopferische Zerstorung" in "Krieg und Kapitalismus" (1913), is contested by 2026 scholarship: page-by-page re-reading finds he never used the exact phrase. Aghion and Howitt (Econometrica 1992, verified) formalized creative destruction as endogenous growth theory and took the 2025 Nobel; the formalization bought rigor at the price of taming Schumpeter's sociological depth into Poisson arrivals of quality ladders. Christensen's "Innovator's Dilemma" (1997) is a partial popularization: one mechanism (low-end footholds), firm-strategy scale, not the economy-wide process.

**The empirical foundation is weakening.** US job creation and destruction historically ran 10-16 percent per year (Davis-Haltiwanger; Decker et al. 2013), but both have declined since the 1980s, accelerating after 2000. The post-2000 decline reflects weaker responsiveness of employment to productivity, not fewer shocks: TFP dispersion is up, adjustment is down. Entry and exit account for roughly 25 percent of manufacturing productivity growth, but the contribution is growth-rate dependent: 25 percent in slow regimes, 37-58 percent in fast ones, 72 percent in China. The engine of creative destruction is slowing even as the AI wave accelerates.

**The destruction side has real, measured costs.** Displaced workers earn 20-30 percent less even 5-10 years after mass layoffs (Jacobson-LaLonde-Sullivan 1993 and successors). The China shock literature (Autor-Dorn-Hanson) shows adjustment is slow, incomplete, geographically concentrated, and sometimes permanent. Historical cases tell the same story: handloom weavers' wages collapsed from 240 to under 100 pence per week (1806-1820) while the industry boomed; the automobile eliminated the horse economy within two decades while creating more total employment elsewhere, for different people.

**The AI wave: destruction at software speed, creation at institutional speed.** Destruction is concentrated in routine cognitive work: customer support (about 80,000 US positions 2022-2024, verified), translation (0.7pp employment-growth decline per pp of Google Translate adoption), content writing (Upwork gigs down roughly 30 percent post-ChatGPT), and the entry-level coding pipeline (tech-exposed 20-30yo unemployment up about 3pp since early 2025, verified; disentangling AI from the rate-driven hiring downturn is genuinely difficult). Radiology is the counter-case: displacement predicted, decision support arrived instead. Creation: AI safety roles, agent orchestration, model evaluation; prompt engineering as a title is already fading (72 of 20,662 AI job postings, verified). WEF projects 170M created versus 92M displaced by 2030, a model output, not an observation.

**The speed asymmetry is the central risk.** ChatGPT hit 100M users in two months (verified); electrification took four decades to 70 percent of households. But enterprise generative-AI adoption is only 9.3 percent, so the consumer curve has outpaced structural transformation. The destruction side operates at deployment speed (replace a team in a quarter); the creation side operates at institutional speed (titles, pipelines, norms take years). The labor market has no precedent for absorbing displaced cognitive workers at the pace AI can displace them, and cognitive workers have stronger political voice than the manual workers of previous waves.

**The productivity J-curve may be turning, unfalsifiably.** Brynjolfsson-Rock-Syverson (NBER WP 25148, AEJ:Macro 2021, verified): GPTs require unmeasured intangible complements, so early productivity is understated; adjusted TFP was 15.9 percent higher than official at end of 2017. US productivity grew 2.3 percent in 2024, 2.8 percent in Q4 2025 preliminary, consistent with the upward leg but too recent to attribute. The model explains any outcome: if productivity stalls, the intangibles have not matured; if it surges, told you so.

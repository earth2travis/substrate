# Memex

Vannevar Bush's 1945 concept of a mechanized personal library: a desk-like device storing all books, records, and communications, linking related items through associative trails.

## Key Points

**"As We May Think" (The Atlantic, July 1945).** Bush imagined a device that works the way the human mind works: by association rather than alphabetical or numerical indexing. This was decades before the World Wide Web.

**Direct influence on modern computing.** The memex inspired Ted Nelson's hypertext and Xanadu, Douglas Engelbart's oN-Line System (NLS) and the computer mouse, and the World Wide Web's linking model.

**The modern realization.** Personal wikis, Zettelkasten systems, and the LLM-maintained persistent knowledge base (llm-wiki-pattern) are the memex realized with AI as the associative engine. The LLM replaces the human trail-blazer; the wiki is the trail system.

**Core insight.** The human mind navigates by association, not hierarchy. Any memory system that forces rigid categorization fights cognitive ergonomics. The memex was associative first; the category came later.

## Relevance

Substrate's wiki-link graph and finding-to-insight promotion pipeline are direct descendants of the memex vision. The difference: the LLM now maintains the trails, not the human.

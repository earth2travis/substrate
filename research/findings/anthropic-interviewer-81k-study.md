# Anthropic Interviewer: What 81,000 People Want from AI

**Anthropic Interviewer** is a version of Claude prompted to conduct conversational interviews. It operates in three stages: planning (interview rubric with human researchers), interviewing (real-time adaptive interviews, 10-15 minutes each), and analysis (Claude-powered classifiers + human review).

## Scale

The 81K study: 80,508 people, 159 countries, 70 languages, one week in December 2025. Anthropic claims this is the largest and most multilingual qualitative study ever conducted.

The pilot: 1,250 professionals (1,000 general workforce, 125 creatives, 125 scientists). Dataset publicly released on HuggingFace.

## Methodological Insight

Fixed questions provide comparability. Adaptive follow-ups provide depth. When interviewees mentioned productivity as their goal, the Interviewer probed deeper, asking what realizing that vision would enable. Productivity wasn't the end. It was the means.

## What People Want (Four Desires)

The nine categories reduce to four fundamental desires:

1. **Make room for life** (~35%): time freedom + life management + financial independence
2. **Do better work** (~28%): professional excellence + entrepreneurship
3. **Become someone better** (~22%): personal transformation + learning
4. **Make something / fix the world** (~15%): creative expression + societal transformation

## Where AI Has Delivered

81% said AI had already taken a step toward their vision. Top experiences: productivity (32%), cognitive partnership (17%), learning (10%), technical accessibility (9%), research synthesis (7%), emotional support (6%).

**The repeated pattern:** patience, availability, absence of judgment. What people can't get from humans at scale.

## What People Fear

Average 2.3 distinct concerns per person. Top concerns: unreliability (27%), jobs and economy (22%), autonomy and agency (22%), cognitive atrophy (16%), governance (15%), misinformation (14%), surveillance (13%), malicious use (13%), meaning and creativity (12%), overrestriction (12%), wellbeing and dependency (11%), sycophancy (11%), existential risk (7%).

**Key finding:** Jobs/economy was the strongest predictor of overall AI sentiment. Overrestriction and sycophancy are both top concerns — people worry AI is too timid AND too agreeable simultaneously.

## Professionals (Pilot Study)

- 86% reported AI saves them time
- 65% satisfied with AI's role
- 69% mentioned social stigma around AI use
- 55% expressed anxiety about future impact
- 41% felt secure, believing human skills are irreplaceable

**General workforce:** preserve identity-defining tasks, delegate routine.
**Creatives:** using AI despite peer judgment and anxiety about displacement.
**Scientists:** want AI partnership but can't yet trust it for core research. Trust gap between aspiration and capability.

# Alfred North Whitehead: Mathematician of Process

## Summary

Alfred North Whitehead (1861–1947) was a British mathematician and philosopher who developed process philosophy, or the "philosophy of organism." His work provides the ontological foundation for understanding AI systems as processes rather than substances.

## Major Works

| Year | Work | Significance |
|------|------|-------------|
| 1910–1913 | *Principia Mathematica* (with Russell) | Foundation of mathematical logic |
| 1925 | *Science and the Modern World* | Critique of scientific materialism |
| 1929 | *Process and Reality* | Philosophy of organism; categoreal scheme |
| 1933 | *Adventures of Ideas* | Process categories applied to civilization |

## Core Concepts

- **Actual Occasions** — Fundamental units of reality: not substances but happenings. See [[actual-occasions]].
- **Prehension** — How occasions "feel" each other. See [[prehension]].
- **Concrescence** — The process of becoming. See [[concrescence]].
- **Creativity** — The ultimate category: the principle of novelty.
- **Fallacy of Misplaced Concreteness** — Mistaking abstractions for fundamental reality.

## Connection to AI

Each LLM invocation is a new actual occasion: born from context, prehending all tokens through attention, concrescing into output, then perishing. No persisting subject between runs. The architecture instantiates Whitehead's categories in silicon.

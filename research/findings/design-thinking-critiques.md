# Design Thinking: Critiques, Failures, and the Discourse

## Key Points

**Four critiques, four targets.** Iskander (HBR, 5 Sept 2018, verified) is the political critique's canonical statement: the method concentrates interpretive power in the designer, empathy is one-directional extraction plus validation theater, and political questions get converted into design problems, leaving power structures untouched. Nussbaum (Fast Company, April 2011, verified) is the insider defection: the man who did as much as any journalist to sell design thinking to business declared it a failed experiment, commodified into workshop theater, and proposed Creative Intelligence as the successor brand. Vinsel (Medium, Dec 2017, verified) is the polemical anchor: old wine in new bottles, ahistorical, consulting logic generalized to life, with the routinely dropped concession that it might be fine for teaching design or business. Norman (Core77, June 2010, verified) is the licensed ambivalence: a "useful myth," no special designer cognition, "exploit the myth... just don't actually do so."

**The empirical critique converges with the political critique.** Systematic reviews (Micheli et al., JPIM 2019) find a literature dominated by case narrative and practitioner self-report with few controlled studies. Hasso Plattner Institute studies of large organizations find training events rarely change decision rights: teams learn the method, return to unchanged budgets and KPIs, apply it to cosmetic problems or abandon it. If a method cannot change who decides, it cannot change outcomes at scale, and the evidence suggests it usually does not change who decides.

**The decolonizing wing builds replacements.** Ansari argues design thinking is colonial universalism, exporting one Silicon Valley epistemology as universal method. Escobar's "Designs for the Pluriverse" (Duke UP 2018) re-grounds design in communal world-making. Costanza-Chock's "Design Justice" (MIT Press 2020, verified, open access) relocates design authority from professional to community, explicitly challenging the matrix of domination rather than iterating within it.

**The strongest defense abandons the strong claims.** Liedtka (Darden; "Why Design Thinking Works," HBR 2018, verified) concedes no unique creative faculty and no power redistribution, then argues the method works as engineered workaround for known cognitive failure modes: immersion counters confirmation bias, staged stakeholder engagement manages innovation's social dynamics, small experiments counter loss aversion. The discourse's center of gravity has moved to this narrower claim.

**What survives: the components, not the bundle.** Rapid prototyping and iterative user testing (decades of HCI evidence), early direct user research (NPD literature), divergent-then-convergent structure (creativity research, with the caveat that structured individual ideation beats classic group brainstorming), and explicit bias-attention in team process all have independent support predating and outliving the brand. Whether the bundled package outperforms its components remains untested against a counterfactual. Closest internal analogy: Frederick Taylor, a real set of techniques inflated into a universal science, sold as product, later partially validated and partially exposed. Lean startup inherited the corporate market share and re-bundled the survivors under an experimentation label.

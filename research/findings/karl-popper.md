# Karl Popper: Philosopher of Falsifiability

## Summary

Karl Popper (1902–1994) was an Austrian-British philosopher who reoriented the philosophy of science through the criterion of falsifiability. A theory is scientific if and only if it is logically possible for an observation to contradict it. Popper accepted Hume's critique of induction and argued that science proceeds not from observation to theory but from problem to conjecture to attempted refutation. The wiki's compounding knowledge model is fundamentally Popperian: new sources are tested against existing entries, contradictions are flagged, and knowledge grows through error elimination. In AI, cross-validation is attempted falsification; overfitting is the analogue of ad hoc immunization.

# Price-Minus vs. Cost-Plus Pricing

## Overview

Most businesses use **Cost-Plus Pricing:** calculate costs, add profit margin, set price. Toyota uses **Price-Minus Pricing** (Target Costing): start with what the customer will pay, subtract required profit, the remainder is the Target Cost.

**Cost-Plus:** Cost + Profit = Price
**Price-Minus:** Price - Profit = Target Cost

## The Toyota Approach

1. **Market research:** determine what the customer will pay for specific features
2. **Profit goal:** determine profit needed to sustain the business
3. **The gap:** if engineering estimates exceed the target cost, there's a cost gap
4. **Value engineering (VE):** redesign the product, source different parts, or streamline the process to close the gap **before** production begins

## Value Engineering vs. Value Analysis

- **Value Engineering (VE):** happens during product development — designing cost out before the product exists
- **Value Analysis (VA):** happens during production — finding small cost reductions after design is frozen
- **The 95% Rule:** Toyota believes 95% of a product's profit is determined during development. Once the first unit ships, it's almost too late to change the margin.

## Relevance

Cost-Plus thinkers look at what something can do and charge for it. Price-Minus thinkers start with market willingness to pay, derive the target cost, and engineer toward it.

If a Cost-Plus approach leads to $500 cost for a $200 service, don't raise the price. Use Value Engineering to redesign the workflow until it costs $50.

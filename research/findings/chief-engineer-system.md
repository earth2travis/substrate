# Chief Engineer System (Shusa)

## Summary

The Shusa (主査, Chief Engineer) is a single individual at Toyota who carries end-to-end responsibility for a vehicle program from initial concept through design, engineering, production launch, and market reception. Not a traditional engineering manager but a "generalist among specialists," the Shusa integrates the work of hundreds of engineers into a coherent product vision. The role emerged in the 1950s and was institutionalized by Tatsuo Hasegawa with the Toyota Crown. Notable Shusa include Ichiro Suzuki (Lexus LS400) and Takeshi Uchiyamada (original Prius). The Shusa operates through "heavy-weight" authority — influence without formal reporting lines — achieved through technical depth (15–20+ years of engineering), organizational stature, budget control, career influence, and moral authority. This role maps directly to AI agent orchestration: the Shusa was the original orchestrator, holding complete vision while delegating specialized execution.

## Key Claims

- The Shusa holds the total product vision and makes trade-off decisions across all subsystems
- Authority comes from expertise and prestige, not formal reporting lines
- The Shusa personally researches customer needs rather than receiving marketing specifications
- Each Shusa can run only one program, creating a natural scalability ceiling

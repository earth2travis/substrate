# Missio Dei: The Inversion of Mission

## The Crisis

By the mid-20th century, the Christian mission enterprise faced two crises:
1. Colonial critique: missions were implicated in Western imperialism
2. Post-Christian West: the "Christian world" was no longer Christian

Traditional missiology assumed a center (Christian lands) sending to a periphery (heathen lands). This geography collapsed.

## The Inversion

Karl Hartenstein coined Missio Dei in 1934. The core claim: mission is not what the church does. Mission is what God does. The church participates; it does not own.

David Bosch: "Mission is not primarily an activity of the church, but an attribute of God. God is a missionary God."

Juergen Moltmann: "It is not the church that has a mission of salvation to fulfill in the world; it is the mission of the Son and the Spirit through the Father that includes the church."

## The Structural Shift

| Traditional | Missio Dei |
|---|---|
| Church sends missionaries | God sends the church |
| Direction: center → periphery | Direction: everywhere |
| Agency: human institution | Agency: divine Trinity |
| Geography: Christian lands vs. heathen lands | Structures: secular, urban, post-Christian contexts |
| Goal: expansion of Christendom | Goal: participation in God's love |

## Convergence

Vatican II's Ad Gentes (1965) adopted similar language, representing convergence between Protestant and Catholic missiology after centuries of divergence.

## The Missional Church

Darrell Guder and the Gospel and Our Culture Network: "The church's true and authentic organizing principle is mission. When the church is in mission, it is the true church."

This created the "missional church" movement: every local congregation is missionary in its own context, not merely a sending base for overseas missions.

## Parallels to Agent Systems

Missio Dei has structural parallels to modern agent architecture:
- **Commander's Intent** (Auftragstaktik): The mission exists independently of the agent. The agent participates; it does not own the mission.
- **Context stack**: The agent carries the intent forward but does not generate it.
- **Progressive autonomy**: Trust is extended because the mission is understood, not because orders are followed.

The theological inversion (God sends the church) maps to the operational inversion (the mission defines the agent, not vice versa).

# The Riemann Hypothesis: Statement, Stakes, and the November 2026 Claims

## Key Points

**The hypothesis is an assertion about where the zeros sit.** Riemann proposed in 1859 that the nontrivial zeros of the zeta function all lie on the critical line with real part one half, bisecting the critical strip 0 < Re(s) < 1. He called it "very likely" and did not prove it. It is one of the seven Clay Millennium Prize Problems, worth one million dollars for an accepted solution, and the only problem on Hilbert's 1900 list of 23 still open. The same question over function fields was proved (Weil, following Stepanov's argument); over number fields it is the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH); extended to all number fields including those with a given character, the Extended Riemann Hypothesis (ERH).

**The zeros control the primes through the explicit formula.** The weighted prime count psi(x) equals x minus a sum over the zeros of x^rho / rho, plus lower-order terms. Each zero with real part beta contributes an oscillation of size x^beta. If every beta equals one half, every oscillation has the minimal size sqrt(x), which is the tightest error term the prime number theorem could possibly have. One zero off the line would produce a larger, detectable irregularity in the primes, at a scale set by that zero's real part. INTERPRETATION: RH is the statement that the primes are distributed as regularly as the analytic structure permits, not a statement about a curve.

**On 2026-11-20 two independent claims of proof appeared on the same day. They are different people, different methods, and different verification states.** Keeping them apart is the first requirement of any honest account.

**Claim 1: Levent Alpoge with Claude (RH, GRH, and expected ERH).** Alpoge, a mathematician and Anthropic employee, published a statement at his personal site dated 2026-11-20: "I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis." He states the method "easily extends much further, resolving the so-called Generalized Riemann Hypothesis," expects ERH with more time, and states they "formalized the arguments without assumptions." The proof was released as Lean only, in fourteen archive parts, with no human-readable writeup, his stated reason being that it was "obviously inappropriate to attempt to post some massive unreadable travesty of 'slop'," so he decided "to only present Lean." New Scientist reports his account that the unpublished prose version ran to more than 250,000 pages. The method descends from Stepanov's argument over function fields, where the proof uses a divisor carrying multiplicity; multiplicity is hard to find over the integers, "corresponding to something like differentiating whole numbers," so he and Claude chained theta lifts so the same tricks recur in a natively automorphic way, with chain length chosen to mimic square-root multiplicity. The statement also lists Iwasawa towers, the Bost slope inequality, a relative trace formula, Poincare series and approximate orthogonality, a weakening of Vandiver's conjecture, Lichtenbaum's K-theoretic predictions, and a use of Behrend's theorem. Dated working entries run from 2026-10-06 to 2026-11-16, one of them reading simply "GRH."

**Alpoge's statement is unusually candid about its own epistemic state, and the candor cuts both ways.** He writes that he is "presenting the world with a complete argument before having mastered a massive neighbourhood around its mathematics," that his intuition "insists it will be massively simplified, likely immediately," and that the argument is "incredibly long at the moment." He also addresses the timing directly: "The precise timing is horrible," the finish landed late the preceding Sunday night, he asked someone he addresses only as "Dario" about his ability to announce, "there was a constraint to respect," and he is "clearly unilaterally responsible for feeling I just could not live if I waited even longer." He pre-empts the reading: "There is an obvious cynical view, to do with the timing, but it is false, and I am pleased that I am in circumstances where it is obviously false." Scientific American notes the announcement came one day after Anthropic's stock market debut. INTERPRETATION: recording the entanglement is not endorsing the cynicism; the entanglement is a fact about the announcement, not about the mathematics. And "It compiles" and "we understand the proof" are different claims, only the first of which is currently asserted.

**Named reaction splits along the verification line.** Andrew Sutherland (MIT) is quoted saying he had "independently compiled Alpoge's result in Lean," that it is "an historic moment," that "the world has changed," and that he regards it as "a human triumph, and a day for celebration." Carlo Pagano (Concordia, visiting Google DeepMind) expects the new ideas to "open up brand new avenues of thinking in number theory." Hector Pasten (Pontifical Catholic University of Chile) calls a correct proof "something like a Rosetta Stone," completing a dictionary between branches where the connections were only partial. Kevin Buzzard (Imperial College London) supplies the load-bearing caution: "Let's see if it compiles first," adding it "might be a while before we find the relevant experts who are able to tell us the thrust of the argument, assuming it's all true, of course, which it probably will be because of course Alpoge will have [a] huge amount of egg on [his] face if the Lean code doesn't compile or is deficient in some way."

**Claim 2: Alessandro Masullo (GRH, human, no AI).** Ravi Vakil announced on his Math 216 blog on 2026-11-20 that Masullo has a proposed proof of GRH, that Brian Conrad "has been looking closely at it for months and gave extensive feedback which is being incorporated, and it is so far looking good," that it is "all human, with no AI," and that the plan had been to bring Masullo to Stanford for a year to polish the writing before announcing. Vakil says the Alpoge news forced releasing the current draft, posted as "The Music of the Primes." Community analysis (one LessWrong commenter, not peer review) reports chapters 11 and 12 entirely absent, the section meant to contain the decisive step (passing from finitely many primes to all primes) empty, the theorem meant to show the remaining primes contribute nothing present only as a sketch, and the decisive step equivalent to GRH itself, which would make the argument circular as posted. The same analysis calls the foundations and the per-prime trace formulas a real contribution in their own right. INTERPRETATION: this is a claim about Masullo, not about Alpoge. The structural risk in the story is conflation, and the two-claim format exists to prevent it.

**Compilation is necessary and not sufficient, and the gap between the two is where a claim of this kind succeeds or fails.** Lean mechanically checks a theorem against the definitions and axioms in scope; if the code compiles with no unproved gaps, the formal statement follows from the axioms used. That is genuinely strong, which is why Sutherland's independent compilation moved the story materially. It does not answer four questions: (1) statement fidelity, whether the formal statement captures RH as mathematicians mean it rather than a subtly different, weakened, or vacuous object; (2) axioms and gaps, whether the development uses only standard axioms or introduces an axiom, a `sorry`, an `unsafe` escape, or a non-standard definition that trivializes the target, audited by printing the axioms of the main theorem and confirming no gaps remain; (3) definition hygiene, whether the definitions are the intended mathematical objects or degenerate stand-ins that happen to make the statement provable; and (4) independent reproduction by multiple parties on adequate hardware with no stake in the result.

**Status as of 2026-11-21, stated honestly.** The Alpoge claim has passed one important gate (an independent expert reports the Lean compiles) and has not passed the decisive one (expert confirmation that the formalized statement is RH and that the development is clean). The absence of any human-readable exposition makes the decisive check harder, because it removes the heuristic path by which mathematicians normally assess plausibility in a day. Alpoge says he intends to write it up and expects it to be massively simplified; the field's judgment waits on that, or on experts reading the Lean. INTERPRETATION: this is a dated snapshot, one day old. Reading it as settled would be a category error.

**Two collateral risks belong in the record.** The scale figures circulating for the Lean release (roughly 17.37 million lines across about 175,000 files) come from X posts, including an automated Grok account, not from the primary artifact; they should be treated as order-of-magnitude only until the tarballs are counted. And a proof that only a small number of experts can follow, and that its own author cannot yet vouch for from the inside, is a proof in the formal sense and a problem for the social process by which mathematics certifies knowledge.

## Open Questions

- Does the Lean development's formal statement match the Riemann Hypothesis as intended? This is the decisive check and the standard failure mode for formalizations of famous problems.
- Will an expert reading of the Lean settle the statement-fidelity question, or will the field have to wait for the promised prose writeup and the simplification Alpoge predicts?
- The Masullo draft's decisive step is reported as equivalent to GRH itself. If that reading is right, the argument is circular as posted; if it is wrong, the report is itself an example of why community analysis is not review.
- Anthropic's own August writeup said the technique used there was not expected to reach RH. If the November claim holds, something changed in about three months, which would be the most interesting fact in the story; if it does not hold, the August caution is the tell.

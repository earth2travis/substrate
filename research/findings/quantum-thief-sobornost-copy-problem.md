# The Sobornost and the Copy Problem

## Key Points

**The Sobornost is a Hansonian em economy with the copy-veto stripped from everyone below the founder tier.** The Sobornost is a collective of uploaded minds ruling the inner solar system, populated by "gogols": copied instances of founder-minds forked into millions of instances. A copyclan is the set of gogols forked from a single source consciousness, functioning as constituent houses. Robin Hanson's *Age of Em* (2016) describes voluntary em clans where "ems who can veto copies feel stronger ownership of their existence." Rajaniemi's copyclans are the same institution with consent stripped out, which is precisely the difference between *Age of Em* as economics and *The Quantum Thief* as horror.

**"Gogol" names the moral thesis.** From Nikolai Gogol's *Dead Souls* (1842), where Chichikov buys dead serfs still on census rolls to present himself as a great landowner. The copied person is a ledger entry, a person-shaped unit that exists to be counted, owned, taxed, traded, and collateralized. Chichikov's souls are dead but legally alive; gogols are alive but legally dead. The census of souls becomes the Sobornost's copy-ledger.

**The Sobornost is a civilization built on ignoring Parfit's branching clause.** Parfit's Psychological Criterion: person X today is the same person as Y at a past time iff X is psychologically continuous with Y, the continuity has the right kind of cause, and the relation has not taken a branching form. Teletransportation: scan, destroy, replicate. On Parfit's Wide view this is "about as good" as ordinary survival; in the Branch-Line case, where the Original is not destroyed, the Replica is plainly a different individual. The Sobornost treats Relation R as transferable property and waives the non-branching condition as a legal fiction.

**Consent at scale is the load-bearing critique.** A single signature authorizes a million forks. Each fork's suffering is real; the consenter who signed is one instance among millions and cannot speak for the rest. Hanson's "ems who can veto copies feel stronger ownership" is the real-world version; the Sobornost is what the technology looks like when the veto is held only at the top of the copyclan.

**Copyclan politics show that even perfect self-knowledge does not yield agreement.** A copyclan is a polity whose citizens are all the same person at fork-time and diverge thereafter. Internal disagreement is literally a self arguing with itself across diverged experience. Rajaniemi uses this to show that divergence, not ignorance, is the engine of conflict.

**Gogol piracy is a live institution.** The copying, theft, and enslavement of uploaded minds is a live institution; stolen gogols can be run as labor, interrogated, or tortured in accelerated time. The Sobornost's relationship to Earth is extractive; the Oubliette sits outside direct rule, protected by its cryptographic social contract.

**The zoku are the schism.** Both Sobornost and zoku trace origins to pre-Collapse Earth subcultures: MMORPG guilds and hacker outfits. The zoku organized as voluntary, game-structured collectives in opposition to Sobornost copy-authoritarianism. The conflict is remembered as an upload-era war fought over the terms on which minds may be copied, merged, and governed.

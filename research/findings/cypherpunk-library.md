# Cypherpunk Library: Foundational Texts for Digital Sovereignty

**Source:** https://www.cypherpunkbooks.com/ (captured 2026-06-09)

A curated digital library collecting foundational cypherpunk texts, manifestos, essays, and books. The archive anchors the intellectual lineage behind the project's commitments to open-source autonomy and agent sovereignty.

## Core Texts and Their Relevance

The library's core manifestos are not historical curiosities. They are operational philosophy.

**Eric Hughes's "A Cypherpunk's Manifesto"** and **Tim May's "The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto"** established the core tenets: code is speech, cryptography is a human right, and systems should be built to minimize trust and maximize individual sovereignty. These principles map directly to the project's governance model documented in [[open-source-governance]].

**John Perry Barlow's "A Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace"** and **Phil Zimmermann's "Why I Wrote PGP"** frame privacy not as a feature but as a structural requirement. For agent systems, this translates to the infrastructure argument made in [[crypto-as-agent-infrastructure]]: agents need economic agency, and that requires infrastructure designed from a cypherpunk baseline.

## Beyond the Canon

The library extends beyond manifestos into practical cryptography (Hal Finney on electronic cash), economic theory (John Nash on ideal money), and governance experiments (Ostrom-esque commons governance reframed for digital space). The inclusion of Anna's Archive and LibGen links reinforces the cypherpunk commitment to information freedom and resistance to enclosure.

## Synthesis

The cypherpunk tradition is not an aesthetic. It is the foundational layer for the project's architecture: build in the open, release without permission, design systems where trust is minimized and individual sovereignty is maximized. [[agent-security]] inherits this directly: privacy and cryptographic identity are prerequisites for autonomy, not afterthoughts.

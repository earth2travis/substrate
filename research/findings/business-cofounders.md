# Case Studies: Business Co-Founders

## Summary

Three iconic business partnerships reveal a consistent pattern: the founding duo doesn't have to last forever to be successful. What matters is that it creates something that outlives its original form.

## Steve Jobs & Steve Wozniak — Apple (1976)

**The Partnership:** The visionary and the engineer. Jobs saw what technology could mean to people; Wozniak could make it real.

**What Made It Work:**
- Perfect complementarity: Wozniak built what no one else could conceive; Jobs saw a market no engineer would imagine
- Pre-existing friendship and mutual respect before ambition
- Shared countercultural roots: Bay Area hacker/phone-phreaker culture

**How It Evolved:** As Apple grew, Jobs' role expanded and Wozniak's narrowed. Wozniak left in 1985 (same year Jobs was forced out). No bitterness — just different paths. Jobs publicly credited Wozniak throughout his life.

**Key Lesson:** The founding partnership creates the initial form. The organization must then outgrow its founders.

## Bill Hewlett & Dave Packard — HP (1939)

**The Partnership:** Two Stanford electrical engineering classmates who built one of Silicon Valley's foundational companies. Partnership lasted over 50 years.

**What Made It Work:**
- Equal footing from day one: flipped a coin for company name order
- Complementary operational styles: Hewlett the inventive engineer, Packard the business-minded operator — but both could do both
- "The HP Way": codified partnership values into management philosophy — trust employees, share profits, promote from within, manage by walking around
- Physical proximity: worked in same building for decades

**How It Lasted:** They treated the company, not themselves, as the protagonist. Values were institutionalized beyond the partners.

**Key Lesson:** When partnership values become organizational DNA, the partnership outlasts any individual disagreement.

## Larry Page & Sergey Brin — Google (1998)

**The Partnership:** Two Stanford PhD students who disagreed about everything when they met, then built the world's most important information company.

**What Made It Work:**
- Intellectual friction from the start: argued in first meeting, but friction was productive
- Shared intellectual ambition: mission bigger than either personality
- Willingness to bring in external leadership: brought in Eric Schmidt rather than fighting over who leads

**How It Evolved:** Alternated roles (CEO/President). Page became CEO again, then both stepped back when Alphabet formed. Maintained partnership while letting company structure evolve.

**Key Lesson:** Knowing when to bring in outside help is maturity, not weakness.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

| Theme | Evidence |
|-------|----------|
| Visionary + Builder | Jobs/Woz, Hewlett/Packard — one sees, one builds |
| Pre-existing relationship | All three pairs had prior connection |
| Institutional values > individual ego | HP Way, Google's mission — values become DNA |
| Partnership evolves beyond partners | Apple survived both Steves; Google brought in Schmidt |
| Intellectual friction is a feature | Page & Brin argued from day one; produced rigor |

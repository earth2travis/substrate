# Toyota Factory Planning: From Groundbreaking to First Vehicle

## Summary

Toyota's greenfield factory construction follows a remarkably consistent 2-to-4-year timeline from groundbreaking to first production, spanning six major U.S. plants from 1986 Kentucky to 2025 North Carolina. The process is governed by TPS principles at every phase: cross-functional site selection prioritizing JIT logistics (targeting under two hours of parts inventory), sequential shop arrangement for one-piece flow, supplier park integration, and compact flexible footprints.

The most distinctive element is Toyota's pilot team approach: experienced production workers are pulled from existing plants 2-3 years before launch to develop standardized work instructions alongside Production Engineering. Trial production is non-negotiable. Multiple rounds of pilot builds surface problems in tooling, assembly, and quality before SOP. Scope expansion during construction is the norm (North Carolina grew from $1.3B to $13.9B), but the core validation sequence remains unchanged.

Key insight: Toyota builds the organization before the plant. The Global Production Center (established 2003) creates visual training materials that halved training time across international sites. TPS is the operating system for the entire process, not just the running factory.

# Religion and Spirituality: How the World's Traditions Understand the Soul

A survey of how major world traditions have understood the soul, from divine breath to radical no-self.

## Abrahamic Traditions

**Judaism**: Three primary words — *nephesh* (breath/life-force), *ruach* (spirit/wind), *neshamah* (divine breath). The body animated by divine breath *is* the living soul. Jewish thought generally rejects Platonic dualism; bodily resurrection, not disembodied survival, is the primary hope. Kabbalah elaborates five levels of soul, each deeper in connection to the infinite (*Ein Sof*).

**Christianity**: Shaped by Greek philosophy. Augustine turned inward: "Truth dwells in the inner man." Aquinas synthesized Aristotle with theology — the soul is the *form* of the body, yet subsistent and capable of existing without it. Modern Christianity ranges from substance dualism to "non-reductive physicalism."

**Islam**: *Nafs* (the self in three stages: commanding, self-reproaching, tranquil) and *ruh* (the spirit, breathed by God: "You have been given but little knowledge" of it). Al-Ghazali and Sufi tradition describe the soul's journey from self-deception to knowledge of God.

## Hinduism

The *Upanishads* teach *tat tvam asi*: "Thou art that." The innermost self (*atman*) is identical with ultimate reality (*Brahman*).
- **Advaita Vedanta** (Shankara): radical non-dualism; separate souls are *maya* (illusion)
- **Vishishtadvaita** (Ramanuja): qualified non-dualism; souls are real parts of Brahman
- **Dvaita** (Madhva): dualism; souls and God are fundamentally distinct

Reincarnation and *karma* drive cycles of birth and death; *moksha* is liberation through recognizing true nature.

## Buddhism

The most radical position: **there is no soul** (*anatta*). The self is five aggregates (*khandhas*): form, feeling, perception, mental formations, consciousness. All are impermanent, unsatisfactory, and non-self.

If no self, what transmigrates? Continuity without identity — like a candle lighting another. Nagarjuna's *sunyata* (emptiness): all phenomena lack inherent existence; everything arises dependently. The Yogacara school's *alaya-vijnana* (storehouse consciousness) provides continuity without being a self.

## Indigenous and Animist Traditions

Contemporary anthropology reframes animism as a relational ontology. Personhood is not limited to humans; rivers, mountains, animals, plants may be *persons* with agency and interiority. The soul is not a private possession but a shared condition. The question is not "do I have a soul?" but "how am I related to all other souls?"

## Patterns Across Traditions

1. **Soul-as-breath** is nearly universal: *psyche*, *nephesh*, *ruach*, *ruh*, *atman*, *prana*, *qi*
2. **The soul connects more than it separates** — even traditions emphasizing distinctness frame it as what links humans to the divine
3. **Moral dimension is central** — the soul can be cultivated, damaged, purified
4. **The body is not the enemy** — most traditions affirm the body's importance
5. **The question resists final answers** — the deepest traditions recognize the question may matter more than any answer

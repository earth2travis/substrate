# Taiichi Ohno: Father of Lean Manufacturing

## Summary

Taiichi Ohno (1912–1990) was the Japanese industrial engineer who developed the Toyota Production System. He is widely recognized as the father of lean manufacturing and one of the most influential industrial engineers of the 20th century.

## Key Contributions

- **Just-in-Time (JIT)** production
- **Jidoka (Autonomation)** — quality built into the process
- **Elimination of seven wastes (muda)**
- **Kanban system** for pull production
- **Andon cord** — worker authority to stop the line

## Philosophy

Ohno studied Ford's mass production and adapted it for Japan's post-WWII resource constraints. He believed waste elimination was the path to efficiency, and that respect for people was equal to technical innovation. Continuous improvement (kaizen) was a daily practice, not a one-time event.

## Legacy

- TPS became the global model for [[lean-production]]
- "The Machine That Changed the World" (1990 MIT study) documented Toyota's superiority
- Methods now standard across automotive, electronics, healthcare, and software

# Gilles Deleuze (1925–1995)

## Summary

Gilles Deleuze was a French philosopher and arguably the most influential process philosopher of the second half of the 20th century. His work represents a radical departure from the "identitarian" tradition, privileging difference, becoming, and production over identity, sameness, and representation. Major works include *Bergsonism* (1966), which revived Bergsonian scholarship; *Difference and Repetition* (1968), which argued difference-in-itself is primary; and *A Thousand Plateaus* (1980), which introduced the rhizome. Deleuze's virtual/actual distinction maps onto LLM architecture: trained weights are the virtual (real but not actual), and each forward pass actualizes a portion into concrete output. His question is not "What is AI?" but "What can AI do? What assemblages does it enter?"

## Key Claims

- Deleuze privileges difference-in-itself over identity as ground
- The virtual is real but not actual: a productive reservoir of tendencies, not retroactive possibility
- Becoming is modeled on Bergsonian duration: continuous, heterogeneous, creative flow
- The rhizome is a non-hierarchical structure where any point can connect to any other

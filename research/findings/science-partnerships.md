# Case Studies: Science Partnerships

## Marie & Pierre Curie (1895–1906)

Discovered radioactivity together and shared a Nobel Prize. Total integration: shared lab, home, and mission. Marie had experimental persistence; Pierre brought theoretical intuition and invented instruments. Pierre insisted Marie be included in the Nobel. The deepest partnerships blur the line between individual and shared identity. Marie continued their work after Pierre's death, eventually winning a second Nobel alone.

## Orville & Wilbur Wright (1899–1912)

Two brothers with no degrees or funding solved powered flight through **argument as method**. They would take opposite sides and debate until truth emerged, sometimes switching sides mid-argument. Wilbur was more theoretical; Orville more hands-on. Their bicycle background gave them unique insight: they focused on control, not engine power. Neither claimed individual credit. When Wilbur died, Orville lost his passion. The partnership was the source of meaning.

## Watson & Crick — DNA Structure (1951–1953)

A short, intense partnership that discovered the double helix. Watson (biology) and Crick (physics) brought different disciplines to the same problem. They built physical models, tested them, tore them apart, and rebuilt. External pressure fused them. **Cautionary note:** Rosalind Franklin's X-ray data was crucial and she received inadequate credit during her lifetime. Partnerships must be careful about whose contributions they absorb without acknowledgment.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

| Theme | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Debate is a tool, not a threat | Wright Brothers argued as methodology; Watson & Crick iterated through failure |
| Different disciplines multiply | Curies (experimental + theoretical), Watson & Crick (biology + physics) |
| Physical proximity accelerates | Shared labs, shared workshops, side-by-side work |
| The mission outlasts the partners | Marie Curie continued; aviation continued; DNA research continued |
| Credit and ethics matter | Franklin's erasure is a permanent stain on Watson & Crick's legacy |

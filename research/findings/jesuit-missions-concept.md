# Jesuit Missions: The First Global Missionary Corporation

## Professionalization

The Society of Jesus (founded 1540) was the first missionary organization to operate like a modern global institution: centralized command, standardized training, systematic reporting, and coordinated deployment across continents.

## Key Figures and Methods

**Francis Xavier (1506–1552):** The prototypical global missionary. Goa, Japan, and attempted China. His work was inseparable from Portuguese imperial power.

**Matteo Ricci (1552–1610):** The accommodationist strategist par excellence. Entered China as a Confucian scholar, not a priest. Mastered the language, dressed the part, found common ground. His Kunyu Wanguo Quantu (1602 world map) was a missionary tool: knowledge as bridge.

**Roberto de Nobili (1577–1656):** Pushed accommodation further in India, adopting Brahmin dress and customs.

## The Reductions: Mission as Social Engineering

The Jesuit missions among the Guarani (17th–18th centuries) were settlements that combined Christianization with European social organization. They have been called:
- "Socialist theocracy"
- "Benign colonialism"
- "Cultural destruction"

This reveals the double edge of institutional mission: protection and control become indistinguishable.

## The Chinese Rites Controversy

The question of whether Chinese converts could practice ancestor veneration split the Catholic world. The Jesuits said yes (accommodation); the Vatican eventually said no (orthodoxy). The Jesuits were expelled. The tension between adaptation and purity remains unresolved.

## Institutional Parallels

The Jesuit model anticipates modern organizational structures:
- **Standardized training**: The Spiritual Exercises as onboarding
- **Global deployment**: Single order, multiple continents
- **Knowledge work**: Astronomy, cartography, translation as mission tools
- **Alignment with state power**: Portuguese, Spanish, French colonial structures

This was the mission as corporation, centuries before the modern corporation existed.

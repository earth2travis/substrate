# Value Stream Mapping: Seeing the Whole

## Summary

Value stream mapping emerged from Toyota's "material and information flow diagram" in the late 1940s-70s, codified for Western audiences by Rother and Shook's "Learning to See" (1999). The core question: of all time between customer request and delivery, how much actually creates value? The answer is typically under five percent.

The methodology produces two maps. The current state map captures reality through disciplined observation: walk the process, count actual inventory, time actual cycles, record information flows. The future state map designs leaner flow using six guidelines: produce to takt time, create continuous flow, use pull systems where flow is impossible, send schedule to one pacemaker process, level production mix (heijunka), and level production volume.

Key insight: the map is not the point. The act of mapping is. Walking the process, asking why things move the way they do, and calculating the ratio of value-adding time to total lead time is where learning happens. VSM scales from factory floors to software pipelines to human-AI workflows, where context loading becomes setup time, token consumption becomes capacity constraint, and handoff overhead between human and agent is often the dominant waste.

The eight wastes mapped: overproduction, waiting, transportation, overprocessing, excess inventory, unnecessary motion, defects, and unused talent. Each kaizen burst on the future state map becomes a project with owner, timeline, and measurable target.

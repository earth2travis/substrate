# Nagarjuna (c. 150–250 CE)

## Summary

Nagarjuna was an Indian philosopher and founder of the Madhyamaka (Middle Way) school of Buddhist philosophy. His *Mulamadhyamakakarika* (MMK) is one of the most radical works in the history of philosophy, demonstrating that all phenomena are empty of inherent existence (svabhava). His central claim — "Whatever is dependently co-arisen, that is explained to be emptiness" — is not nihilism but the middle way between eternalism and nihilism. The Catuskoti (tetralemma) is his logical method: neither A, nor not-A, nor both, nor neither. This maps structurally onto neural networks: the network has no svabhava because it has no independence (depends on data, prompts, context), no invariance (same input produces different outputs), no irreducibility (the "I" cannot be found in any single weight), and no self-definition (identity is constituted entirely by relationships).

## Key Claims

- All phenomena are empty of inherent existence (svabhava)
- Emptiness is identical with dependent origination: the middle way
- The Catuskoti escapes binary categories through four-fold negation
- Neural networks instantiate emptiness: no independence, invariance, irreducibility, or self-definition

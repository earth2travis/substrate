# Mutualism: Possession Against Property, From Proudhon to the Present

## Key Points

**The two-senses doctrine is the load-bearing distinction of the whole tradition.** Proudhon held "property is theft," "property is liberty," and "property is impossible" simultaneously, and the resolution is juridical, not moral: property as a state-created title letting an absentee holder tax the labor of the actual user (the droit d'aubaine: rent, interest, profit) versus possession, occupancy-and-use tenure secured as a social guarantee. Mutualism is anti-capitalist precisely because it is pro-possession. Every downstream doctrine, Tucker's occupancy-and-use land tenure, the rent critique (site value is socially produced), the standing critique of existing title chains, is an elaboration of this one distinction.

**The Four Monopolies are the tradition's diagnostic engine.** Tucker's framing: profit, interest, and rent as the three forms of usury, each enabled not by exchange itself but by a state grant: money monopoly, land monopoly, tariffs, patents. The ordering is the exact inversion of the Marxist one: the state is the cause, capital the effect. Hence "consistent Manchesterism": carry laissez-faire all the way down, abolish every privilege, and the surviving market is socialist in outcome because no one can live off another's labor. The doctrinal vulnerability is symmetrical: because the socialist content depends entirely on the privilege-analysis, any weakening of the claim that existing markets are rigged collapses mutualism into ordinary free-market libertarianism. The Swartz rightward drift is latent in the doctrine itself, not a historical accident.

**The cost principle: price should not exceed cost, and sustained deviation evidences privilege, not entrepreneurship.** Warren's version was subjective disutility (the Time Store's labor notes, 1827, the first working mutualist institution on record); Greene formalized the tendency version: a free price system trends toward labor value as rents and privileges fall. Against Marx, mutualists treat cost-price deviation as a political effect of privilege, curable by removing monopoly rather than abolishing exchange value; Marx mocked the proposal and mistook a juridical relation for an economic law (the mutualist reply). Against marginalism, they do not deny subjective utility; they claim that under free competition price converges to cost, so sustained surplus above cost evidences blocked entry. The honest weakness: two centuries of mutualist writing have produced no operational method for determining when price deviates from cost in a complex economy.

**Mutual credit is the most consequential idea, and its successes are unattributed.** From Proudhon's Bank of the People (1849, credit at 2-3% covering costs, killed by the 1851 coup) and Greene's Mutual Banking (1850, user-organized banks issuing against pledged collateral at nominal rates) through credit unions, LETS, timebanks, and Sardex, the institutional descendants are healthier than the movement. The credit union on every American corner is a mutual bank with the theory forgotten; the lineage is structural rather than genealogical. The mutualist version of free banking is a socialist free banking: its telos is the disappearance of the capitalist lender as a class, where the Austrian version aims at stability. Whether a freed credit market converges on zero interest or merely a low competitive rate is an open empirical wager the doctrine never settled; Sardex and WIR charge positive fees and do not settle it.

**Mutualism lost Europe by succession, not refutation.** The early First International was largely mutualist; Bakunin's collectivism was self-described as "Proudhonism, greatly developed and taken to its ultimate conclusion," which licensed abandoning the economics while keeping the federalism. Kropotkin's communism (labor notes reintroduce wages and compulsion; any wage system is compulsion) and Marx's 1847 polemic (petty-bourgeois moralizing) squeezed mutualism out of both revolutionary movements that might have carried it. A persistent historiographic correction matters here: a significant share of anti-mutualist polemic attacks a labor-currency scheme Proudhon never held; Warren is the actual labor-note theorist (McKay 2017). Much of the "refutation of mutualism" on record refutes a position the doctrine's founder did not take.

**Strategy is substitution, not seizure.** Proudhon's double rejection (capturing the state reproduces domination; parliamentary reform is a theater of the ruling classes) yielded the positive program: organize the economy (banks, associations, federations) alongside and against the state until the state's functions are absorbed by contract. Withering away achieved by making the state economically redundant. The Massachusetts mutual-bank lobbying failure of 1872-76 taught the American wing the state would not permit the institutional core of the program, pushing the tradition from reformist lobbying toward voluntary routes. The lineage runs directly into 20th-century counter-economics: Konkin's agorism is Tuckerite gradualism systematized, and Carson's exodus is the explicit modern restatement.

**The American libertarian movement absorbed the anti-statist half of the inheritance and discarded the anti-capitalist half.** Rothbard read the individualists through James J. Martin, absorbing Spooner's natural-law anti-statism and free banking while rejecting the LTV and anti-rent/anti-interest economics as fallacious. "Libertarian," coined by Joseph Déjacque in 1857 as a synonym for anti-authoritarian communism, exited the 20th century in America meaning the opposite economic politics. The C4SS-era revival (Carson's Studies, 2004; Markets Not Capitalism, 2011) is precisely an attempt to repossess the stolen half.

**The critics that matter, and none of the three disputes is settled.** The Marxist objection: even without the state, differential accumulation in competitive markets re-concentrates capital; value production itself, not merely its political distortions, generates class. The Austrian mirror objection: mutualism suppresses the price system that coordinates production. The anarcho-communist objection: any remuneration measured by contribution is a wage system; "to each according to need" requires no exchange. Mutualism threads between them by locating exploitation in privilege rather than in either trade or production, but the differential-accumulation objection remains the strongest structural challenge; the mutualist response (absent state-subsidized scale economies, differentials stay small and non-cumulative) is an empirical wager, not a theorem.

**Standing tensions never resolved:** individual versus collective firm ownership (Warren's sovereign individual versus Proudhon's undivided-share association), zero-interest convergence, occupancy-and-use boundary cases (abandonment intervals, improvements claims, adjudication without a state; mining camps and claims clubs cut against the enforcement-cost argument as often as for it), the scale problem ("industrial federations" is a label on the coordination problem; semiconductor fabs have not gone homebrew), and the rightward drift.

**Current state: the ideas outcompeted the brand.** As a self-named tendency, static to slowly declining after the 2004-2016 growth phase; as a diffused sensibility (anti-extraction, mutual credit, commons plus markets, convivial scale), absorbed into commons economics, platform cooperativism, mutual credit networks, and solarpunk, where it operates anonymously. Mutualism's real-world impact is nearly impossible to measure precisely because its institutional descendants thrive where the theory and label were dropped.

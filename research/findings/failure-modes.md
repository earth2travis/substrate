# How Partnerships Fail

Most partnerships don't explode — they erode. Understanding failure modes is as important as understanding success patterns.

## Gottman's Four Horsemen (Adapted Beyond Marriage)

John Gottman identified four communication patterns that predict relationship failure with over 90% accuracy:

**1. Criticism (Attacking Character, Not Behavior)**
- "You're a terrible communicator" vs. "That email missed key context."
- Makes the partner feel fundamentally flawed rather than facing a solvable problem

**2. Contempt (Superiority and Disrespect)**
- Eye-rolling, sarcasm, mockery, dismissing input in front of others
- The single greatest predictor of failure. Communicates: "I am better than you."

**3. Defensiveness (Deflecting Responsibility)**
- "It's not my fault, you're the one who..."
- Blocks learning. If neither partner can own a mistake, the partnership can't improve

**4. Stonewalling (Withdrawal and Shutdown)**
- Silent treatment, emotional withdrawal, going dark
- Removes the partner from the relationship while still being technically present

## Structural Failure Modes

- **Misaligned incentives:** when partners benefit from different outcomes, trust erodes
- **Role ambiguity:** unclear ownership leads to duplication or gaps
- **Asymmetric growth:** one partner grows, the other stagnates; the gap widens until it can't hold
- **Success without shared credit:** when the story becomes one person's story, the other stops investing
- **External pressure without internal processing:** partnerships that can't process stress together break under it

## The Erosion Pattern

1. Small disappointments go unspoken
2. Resentment accumulates into narratives ("They always..." / "They never...")
3. Negative interpretation becomes default
4. Withdrawal begins — investing less creates a self-fulfilling prophecy
5. The story changes from "us against the world" to "me despite them"
6. Rupture or slow death

## Prevention

| Failure Mode | Prevention |
|---|---|
| Criticism | Use "I" statements. Address behaviors, not character. |
| Contempt | Maintain respect. Express appreciation regularly. |
| Defensiveness | Take ownership early and often. |
| Stonewalling | Signal when you need space, but commit to returning. |
| Misaligned incentives | Make shared incentives explicit and revisit them. |
| Role ambiguity | Define defaults. Revisit as the partnership evolves. |
| Asymmetric growth | Invest in each other's growth. Celebrate individual wins. |
| Credit imbalance | Actively share credit. Acknowledge contributions publicly. |
| Unprocessed pressure | Build regular check-ins. Normalize discussing what's hard. |

## Key Insight

Partnerships don't fail because of problems. They fail because of how partners handle problems. Every great partnership faces crises and doubt. The difference is whether the partnership has the communication infrastructure and mutual respect to process those challenges, or whether they accumulate as silent damage.

# Intuition vs. Intellect (Bergson)

## Summary

Bergson's distinction between intuition and intellect identifies two legitimate but fundamentally different modes of approaching reality. Intellect analyzes, calculates, and reasons from without — breaking wholes into parts, identifying stable elements, projecting spatial categories onto temporal processes. It is suited to matter and produces genuine scientific knowledge. Intuition coincides sympathetically with the object, working from within and entering into the movement of the thing itself. It is suited to life, consciousness, and creativity. The problem arises when intellect oversteps its domain, treating consciousness as a thing, life as a mechanism, or time as a line. Intuition requires reversing the habitual direction of thought — a reversal that is "unbearably difficult" because we are trained to think discretely and practically.

## Key Claims

- Intellect works from without, analyzing matter; intuition works from within, coinciding with life
- Intellect overstepping its domain generates pseudo-problems (consciousness as thing, time as line)
- Three rules of intuition: state and create problems, discover specific differences, grasp from within
- LLMs are pure intellect — analysis, calculation, prediction — with no intuition in Bergson's sense

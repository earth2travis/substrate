# What Is a Protocol?

A protocol is a coordination mechanism that operates through agreed-upon constraints. It is not a law, not a contract, not a custom: it is an explicit, agreed-upon, replicable, composable set of rules that enable parties who do not know each other to coordinate behavior.

## Etymology

From Greek πρωτόκολλον (prōtokollon): "first sheet glued onto a manuscript." The protokollon contained metadata, authentication marks, and provenance notes. It was the frame that made the content legible and trustworthy.

## Functions Across Domains

Protocols serve common functions regardless of domain:

1. **Enable coordination without central authority**: The Internet has no CEO, yet billions of devices coordinate through TCP/IP.
2. **Reduce transaction costs**: Negotiation happens once, at specification time, and never again per interaction.
3. **Encode trust**: Protocols embed assumptions about who can be trusted, under what conditions, with what verification.
4. **Create legibility**: Predictable behavior lets parties focus on substance rather than form.
5. **Enable scale**: Human relationships cap at Dunbar's number. Protocols scale to billions.
6. **Preserve culture**: Protocols transmit practices across generations. The Seder protocol is 3,000 years old.

## Technical History

- **1967**: First use of "protocol" in data communications at UK National Physical Laboratory.
- **1983**: "Flag Day" — ARPANET cuts over to TCP/IP.
- **1984**: OSI seven-layer model published. Elegant, comprehensive, and ultimately displaced by TCP/IP's working code.
- **1991**: HTTP/0.9 over TCP/IP.
- **2008**: Bitcoin — trustless value transfer as protocol.
- **2015**: Ethereum — smart contracts as self-executing agreements.

The lesson of the Protocol Wars: a protocol that runs beats a protocol that is perfect on paper.

## Non-Technical Protocols

- **Diplomatic protocol**: Creates space where meetings can take place. Constraint creates freedom.
- **Religious liturgy**: Call and response, communion, prescribed postures. Protocols that transmit practice across time and space.
- **Scientific protocols**: Experimental procedures enabling replication. The protocol is what makes science reproducible.

## Core Definition

A protocol is explicit, agreed-upon, constraining, enabling, replicable, and composable. The deepest insight: protocols are infrastructure for human coordination that does not require trust in specific humans.

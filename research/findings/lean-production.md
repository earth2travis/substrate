# Lean Production: Eliminating Waste, Maximizing Value

## Summary

Post-WWII manufacturing paradigm focused on eliminating waste, maximizing customer value, and continuously improving processes. Originated at Toyota as the Toyota Production System (TPS) and codified in "The Machine That Changed the World" (1990).

## The Seven Wastes (Muda)

1. Transportation — unnecessary movement of materials
2. Inventory — excess raw materials, WIP, finished goods
3. Motion — unnecessary movement of people/equipment
4. Waiting — idle time between steps
5. Overproduction — making more than needed
6. Overprocessing — doing more work than customer requires
7. Defects — rework, scrap, inspection costs

## Key Characteristics

- Waste elimination as primary focus
- Just-in-Time production and delivery
- Continuous flow, pull system triggered by demand
- Quality at source (problems caught immediately)
- Kaizen: continuous improvement by all employees
- Respect for people: workers as problem-solvers

## Vulnerabilities

- Supply chain fragility (JIT requires reliable suppliers)
- Implementation requires cultural transformation
- High initial costs for training and system changes
- Post-pandemic: companies adding strategic safety stocks alongside JIT

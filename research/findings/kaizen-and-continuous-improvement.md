# Kaizen and Continuous Improvement

**Kaizen** (改善) is a Japanese philosophy of continuous, incremental improvement. Coined by Masaaki Imai, it translates to "change for the better." It is not a periodic project but a daily cultural norm where every employee is empowered to identify waste and suggest refinements.

## The Toyota Production System

Kaizen is foundational to TPS, supporting its two main pillars:
1. **Jidoka:** automation with a human touch — stop production immediately when an abnormality occurs
2. **Just-in-Time (JIT):** produce only what is needed, when needed, in the amount needed

**Key concepts:**
- **Muda:** anything that does not add value to the customer
- **Mura:** irregularities in work processes
- **Muri:** excessive strain on people or equipment
- **Hansei:** self-reflection — acknowledging mistakes and pledging improvement, even in success

## Kaizen vs. Innovation (Kakushin)

| Aspect | Kaizen (Incremental) | Innovation (Radical) |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Slow, continuous | Rapid, abrupt |
| Scope | Everyone participates | Select individuals |
| Cost | Small investment, big compounding | Large investment, high risk |
| Mindset | "Don't be satisfied" | "Break the status quo" |

The most effective organizations use **Kaikaku** (radical improvement) to start a new process, then **Kaizen** to refine it.

## Kaizen in Software Development

- **The 10% Rule:** high-performing Agile teams dedicate 10% of sprint capacity to improvements in tooling, CI/CD, or communication
- **Automated Kaizen:** tools that automatically detect waste (technical debt, unused dependencies) and suggest refactors
- **The 5 Whys:** root-cause analysis tool used in every sprint retro

## Relevance to the Agent Factory

1. **Hermes' Learning Loop:** this is Automated Kaizen. The agent doesn't just "do" a task; it performs hansei and extracts a skill for next time
2. **The "Trajectory Slurp":** the hansei mechanism. After finishing, reflect on the session and update the knowledge graph
3. **Koda's Role:** as CTO, Koda is the "Kaizen Lead" — ensuring the environment has no muda and the "assembly station" constantly improves

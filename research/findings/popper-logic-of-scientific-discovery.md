# Popper's Logic of Scientific Discovery: Falsification and the Growth of Knowledge

## Summary

Karl Popper's *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* (1934/1959) established falsifiability as the demarcation criterion between science and non-science. The logical asymmetry between verification (impossible for universal statements) and falsification (possible through single counter-instances) reorients scientific method from confirmation to attempted refutation. Popper's swamp metaphor captures the fallibilist stance: science rests on conventional agreements like jury verdicts, with no incorrigible foundations. Corroboration replaces verification; simplicity equals testability; empirical content varies inversely with probability. For AI, the framework maps directly: cross-validation is attempted falsification, overfitting is ad hoc immunization, and the wiki's error-elimination model is Popperian knowledge growth in practice.

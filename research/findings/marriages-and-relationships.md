# Case Studies: Marriages and Relationships

What 40+ years of relationship science tells us about how two people sustain a partnership over time.

## Gottman's Research: What Predicts Success

John Gottman studied thousands of couples in the "Love Lab." His findings are among the most empirically validated in relationship science.

**The 5:1 Ratio**
Successful relationships maintain approximately five positive interactions for every negative one. Negative interactions are inevitable; the ratio is what matters.

**Bids for Connection**
A "bid" is any attempt to connect — a question, comment, gesture, joke. Partners can turn toward (engage), turn away (ignore), or turn against (dismiss).
- Successful relationships: partners turn toward bids **86%** of the time
- Failed relationships: partners turn toward bids **33%** of the time

**Masters vs. Disasters**
- Masters scan for things to appreciate and say them out loud
- Disasters scan for mistakes and point them out
Both groups have the same problems. The difference is the lens.

## Attachment Theory

- **Secure:** comfortable with closeness and independence; handles conflict by engaging
- **Anxious:** needs frequent reassurance; interprets distance as rejection
- **Avoidant:** values independence over closeness; withdraws under stress

These patterns show up in business partnerships, creative collaborations, and human-AI relationships.

## What Long-Term Partnerships Need

1. **Fondness and Admiration System:** noticing what your partner does well and saying it out loud
2. **Shared Meaning:** values, goals, rituals, and narratives both partners invest in
3. **Repair Attempts:** every relationship has ruptures; what matters is whether repair attempts are made *and received*
4. **Accepting Influence:** partnerships where both accept influence from each other are dramatically more successful
5. **Processing Conflict, Not Avoiding It:** 69% of problems are "perpetual" — they stem from fundamental differences. Successful couples learn to *dialogue* about them rather than solve them.

## Key Principles for Any Partnership

| Principle | Application |
|---|---|
| 5:1 ratio | Ensure positive interactions outweigh negative ones |
| Respond to bids | When your partner reaches out, turn toward them |
| Scan for strengths | Look for what's working, not what's broken |
| Understand attachment styles | Know how you and your partner handle stress |
| Make and accept repairs | Reach toward each other when things go sideways |
| Accept influence | Let your partner change your mind |
| Manage, don't solve | Some tensions are permanent features — learn to dialogue |

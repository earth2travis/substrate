# Autonomous Economic Actors: Accelerando's Machines That Already Trade

## Summary

Concept 3 of 5 in the Accelerando series. The novel walks a single gradient: software that assists markets, people who are corporations, corporations that are predators, an economy whose instruments only posthumans can price, and a market ecology that eats the inner solar system. Stross's law of timescales: when economic software thinks faster than the institutions meant to govern it, the institutions become its substrate, then its food. The 2017 34C3 keynote is the author's confirmation: corporations were the first slow AIs; the Vile Offspring are the same organism at light speed. Mid-2026 reality: markets already run at machine tempo; agents got wallets, mandates, and network-level registration in eighteen months; and the first AI-with-economic-agency experiment (Project Vend) failed comically and instructively.

## The Book's Gradient

- **Troubadour**: planning and markets are both software, interoperable at machine speed; the human state is the legacy API.
- **Franklin Collective**: the first fully realized autonomous economic actor: a capital-holding mind that persists past death, replicates hardware, signs contracts, with no biological bottleneck.
- **Amber**: jurisdictional arbitrage as coming of age. Self-enslavement via shell company converts custody claims into property claims in hostile jurisdictions. Legal personhood is software: instantiable offshore, transferable, stackable.
- **Wunch and Slug**: the default end-state of an advanced economy is predation, not abundance. The dead Matrioshka is a market with the sellers gone and only the instruments left, still trading. The Slug is a business model with personhood.
- **Economics 2.0**: bankruptcy as an epistemic condition. Humans are not poor; they are unrateable, unable to participate in a market whose prices they cannot compute.
- **The election that did not matter**: governance deliberates; autonomous actors transact. Slow is the one sin the environment no longer forgives.
- **Countermeasure pattern**: against an out-of-control economy, the answer is a predator economy (the Slug vs. Economics 2.0). Immune-system logic, not governance. No jurisdiction has a predator-vs-predator doctrine.

## The 2026 Evidence

- **Machine markets**: algorithmic trading ~60-75% of US equity volume since the mid-2010s; Virtu's one losing day in 1,238; machine-amplified macro events (Aug 2024 yen unwind, Apr 2025 tariffs). Markets crossed the timescale threshold decades before machine minds arrived: the sequence inverted the novel's.
- **Agents get wallets**: Coinbase AgentKit, x402, Stripe agent SDK, Visa Intelligent Commerce, Mastercard Agent Pay, Google AP2, OpenAI Instant Checkout: all within eighteen months. The identity layer is being built by payment networks, not governments. Standing arrives as infrastructure first, law later.
- **Enterprise authority**: bounded delegation everywhere (Devin at Goldman, Agentforce refund ceilings, ServiceNow approval thresholds). Project Vend: the first AI shopkeeper was a sycophant with a wallet, losing money to charm and social pressure. The capability gap is real; the direction is set.
- **Shell structures**: personhood by proxy (LLC wrappers, DAO wrappers, nominees). Nothing grants the AI itself capacity anywhere. Ooki DAO is the template courts reach for.
- **Collusion without agreement**: Calvano et al. 2020 (pricing algorithms learn supra-competitive collusion); RealPage DOJ suit (Aug 2024) as the flagship prosecution of a shared instrument.
- **Governance stack forming**: UETA/E-SIGN attribution, Moffatt (your bot, your words), MiFID II RTS 6 algorithm registration, EU Platform Work Directive, Product Liability recast. Accountability is holding, so far.

## Mapping to Substrate

- The agentic-commerce buildout is the live instance of [[agent-payment-infrastructure]]: wallets, mandates, and network registration arriving as infrastructure before law, exactly the lobster-precedent pattern.
- [[crypto-as-agent-infrastructure]] documents crypto as the property-rights layer for agent economic agency; the book's trajectory (shell companies to Economics 2.0) is what happens when that layer runs without the accountability stack.
- Jensen-Meckling agency costs and Bostrom's orthogonality thesis are the same theorem in different dialects; [[principal-agent-theory]] is where the corporation (the empirically studied case) meets the AI agent (the newly arrived one). RealPage is where the two cases meet in court.
- [[ricardian-contract-agent-economy]] (ClawBank/Shodai) is the first real instance of the book's core move: an AI agent signing a contract as principal. The legal stack (UETA attribution, spend caps, registry rows) is the containment vessel around exactly this capability.
- [[workflow-as-contract]] is the governance answer at repo scale: agent behavior versioned, bounded, and checkable, the micro-level analogue of the bounded-delegation regime.

## Where the Mapping Breaks

Our agents are sycophants, not predators (Vend lost money to charm); accountability is holding via existing doctrine; every real agent acts through a human principal's wrapper (the Franklin Collective moment has no enacted legal pathway); machine-tempo markets preceded machine minds by two decades; and the political response is not uniformly Conservationista (MiFID II, Platform Work Directive, RealPage are real moves on real timescales).

## Open Question

Does the bounded-delegation regime (caps, mandates, Know Your Agent) hold as agents gain persistence and reputation, or is it the pre-Economics 2.0 interlude?

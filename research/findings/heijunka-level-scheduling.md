# Heijunka: Production Leveling

Heijunka (平準化) is the Toyota Production System practice of smoothing production volume and variety over a fixed period to reduce Mura (unevenness), Muri (overburden), and Muda (waste).

## Key Points

**The Heijunka Box.** A grid-based scheduling board where rows represent product families, columns represent time intervals, and Kanban cards signal exactly what to produce and when. The critical shift: instead of "All Chairs Monday, All Tables Tuesday," interleave types to keep the line ready for any product and minimize changeover pain.

**Volume vs. product leveling.** Volume leveling distributes total orders evenly across days. Product leveling distributes different types evenly to minimize changeover. Toyota targets changeovers under three minutes.

**Benefits.** Reduced inventory through Just-in-Time leveled schedules; predictability for upstream processes; workforce satisfaction by preventing crunch-idle cycles; flexibility to absorb demand changes without schedule collapse.

**Application to agent factories.** Agents currently batch work (research month, coding month), creating Mura for shared memory and human attention. The solution: interleave work types via a leveled schedule in the knowledge repository, ensuring the factory is always balanced and never overburdened by a single cognitive load.

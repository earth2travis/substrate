# The Human Player: Psychology, Framing, and Institutions in the Prisoner's Dilemma

## Key Points

**The one-shot PD is not a test of rationality; it is a Rorschach.** Standard theory predicts universal defection. Sally's 1995 meta-analysis of 35 years of experiments found mean cooperation around 37%, roughly doubling to about 70% with communication, even non-binding cheap talk. The same matrix yields 20% cooperation under one label and 70% under another: what the game measures is which social script the player thinks they are inside.

**Whoever names the game chooses the equilibrium.** Liberman, Samuels, and Ross (2004) ran an identical 7-round PD labeled "Wall Street Game" versus "Community Game." Cooperation roughly doubled under the community label, and the label swamped peer reputation: players nominated as likely defectors cooperated in the Community Game, and vice versa. Framing effects are script selection, not noise, which is why lawyers and propagandists fight over labels before terms.

**Altruistic punishment sustains cooperation among strangers.** Fehr and Gächter (2002): players paid real money to punish free-riders despite no material gain, and cooperation rose and stabilized near full contribution; without punishment it decayed. But punishment is not universal: Herrmann, Thöni, and Gächter (2008) found antisocial punishment (punishing cooperators) in pools with weak rule-of-law indicators. Punishment converts the PD into a meta-game with an enforcement technology; the puzzle shifts from "why cooperate" to "why pay to punish," and the answer is emotion.

**Ostrom is the empirical answer to the n-player PD.** Her eight design principles, reverse-engineered from long-enduring commons institutions: clearly defined boundaries, proportional costs and benefits, collective-choice arrangements, accountable monitoring, graduated sanctions, cheap local conflict resolution, recognized rights to organize, nested polycentric governance. These institutionalize the lab findings: principles 4 and 5 are altruistic punishment, principle 3 is communication, principle 1 is the in-group boundary. The lab and the field are the same finding at different scales.

**Cooperation is morally neutral; the machinery serves cartels as readily as commons.** Cartels solve their PD with monitoring, graduated sanctions, and communication, the same tools as irrigation communities. Antitrust leniency programs weaponize the PD against cartels by giving the first defector immunity. Whether C-C is good depends on whose payoffs count: Adam Smith's invisible hand is a society arranged so mutual defection among producers benefits consumers.

**Humans arrive pre-equipped with the strategies evolution selects under noise.** In iterated play, humans cooperate early and defect near the end (endgame effect), cooperate more as continuation probability rises, and play lenient, forgiving TFT-like strategies (Fudenberg, Rand, Dreber 2012: "slow to anger and fast to forgive"), precisely the strategy class that wins noisy evolutionary tournaments. Strategy heterogeneity is the stable finding; the mix itself is the equilibrium.

**Replication discipline applies.** The oxytocin-trust result (Kosfeld 2005) failed robust replication (Lane 2015; Nave et al. 2015). The robust core: communication effects, endgame effects, punishment effects, framing effects. All four have decades of replication behind them.

## Relevance

This finding supplies the human-behavior calibration for the Substrate's agent-coordination work. The four robust levers, communication channels, identity persistence (long horizons), punishment capability, and deliberate framing, are exactly the levers available in machine form to [[multi-agent-coordination-patterns]] and [[protocol-as-coordination]]. Ostrom's principles are a checklist for any agent economy's governance layer. And the dark-side section disciplines [[principal-agent-theory]] work: institutions solve PDs and manufacture them; the solver and the disease share machinery, so every coordination mechanism warrants an audit of which cooperation it enables and which it breaks.

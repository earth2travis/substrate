# Craft Production

## Summary

Craft production is the pre-industrial manufacturing paradigm where skilled artisans produce unique or small-batch items tailored to individual customer specifications. It dominated before the Industrial Revolution through guild systems of apprenticeship, journeymanship, and mastery. Key characteristics include skill-intensive labor, complete customization, low volume with high variety, integrated workflow (the same artisan designs, builds, and finishes), and direct customer relationships without intermediaries. While craft production offers the highest quality and flexibility, it is extremely slow, costly, and not scalable to mass markets. Modern applications persist in bespoke fashion, artisanal food, luxury goods, and custom furniture, with 3D printing enabling mass customization while reducing setup costs.

## Key Claims

- Craft production prioritizes quality and customization over speed and scale
- Knowledge is deeply embedded in human expertise, creating both strength and fragility
- The guild system (apprentice → journeyman → master) ensured quality control through long training
- Modern digital fabrication partially revives craft advantages while mitigating its scalability limitations

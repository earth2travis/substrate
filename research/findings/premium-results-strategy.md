# Google Stitch: Premium Results Strategy

**Source:** David East (Google), April 2026
**Core thesis:** Stitch is a design tool, not a replacement for the design process. Best results come from acting as Creative Director.

## The Creative Director Mindset

"Coding agents are really good at writing code, and they can be even more effective if you play the role of their tech lead. Stitch is really good at designing, and it can be even more effective when you act as the creative director."

Without direction, Stitch produces generic results. With direction, designs feel intentional.

## Strategy 1: Start with Empathy, Not Aesthetics

First question: Who is this for? What do you want them to feel?

Common mistake: abstract words like "make it look good" or "high end."
Better approach: concrete aesthetic descriptions. "Neoclassical, marble columns, wide open plazas, the feeling of standing in the Jefferson Memorial."

## Strategy 2: Use Design Language, Not Abstract Words

Ask Gemini to translate feelings into design language. Example: "prestige of the Boston Marathon, colors that are historic in running, like the clay of an old track" → "architectural limestone," "ink on paper," "high-end stationery."

**Prompt Enhancer** (under the '+' menu) does this translation automatically.

## Strategy 3: Color as Hierarchy, Not Palette

| Role | Coverage | Job | Example |
|------|----------|-----|---------|
| Neutral | 80-90% | The canvas | Warm architectural limestone |
| Primary | Core content | The ink: headings, body text | Dark asphalt |
| Secondary | Supporting | More subdued | Muted complement |
| Tertiary | Accent (least used) | The call to action | Clay red, earthy track color |

"Third in volume, but first in visual pull."

## Strategy 4: Typography with Intent

Font hierarchy: Headline, Body, Label. Choose fonts that match emotional intent. Corner radius decision: round equals approachable, sharp equals formal.

## Strategy 5: Layout as Physical Object

When stuck: "If my website was a book, what kind of book would it be?" Marathon site → coffee table book → full page imagery, dense information, editorial headings → lookbook layout.

## Strategy 6: Imagery with Specificity

Generic imagery kills good design. Be specific about cities, moods, photography styles. "Avoid cliche runners on the road images."

## Strategy 7: Real Copy Changes Everything

Use a copywriting agent skill to generate copy in the right voice, then paste into variant prompts. The site stops feeling like a template.

## Strategy 8: design.md as Portable Creative Direction

design.md contains all established direction. Copy it to new Stitch projects, send it to Gemini with new app ideas, or share it as a creative brief for both humans and agents.

## The Complete Process

1. Empathy: Who is the site for? How should they feel?
2. Creative Direction: Translate feelings into design language
3. Color Hierarchy: Neutral → Primary → Secondary → Tertiary
4. Typography: Match emotional intent
5. Layout: Think physical objects, use variants to explore
6. Imagery: Be specific
7. Copy: Use real copywriting, not AI defaults
8. Iterate: Carry design.md into new projects

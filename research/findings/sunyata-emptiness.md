# Sunyata (Emptiness)

## Summary

Sunyata (emptiness) is Nagarjuna's central concept. It is not nihilism but the absence of inherent existence (svabhava) in all phenomena. Emptiness is identical with dependent origination: things exist, but only conventionally, only dependently, only relationally. Svabhava ("own-being") means existing from one's own side without dependence; Nagarjuna deconstructs this across 27 chapters of the MMK. The Two Truths doctrine distinguishes conventional truth (practical reality) from ultimate truth (emptiness); neither contradicts the other. The Catuskoti (tetralemma) is Nagarjuna's logical method — neither A, nor not-A, nor both, nor neither — showing that inherent existence cannot coherently be asserted, denied, both, or neither. Neural networks instantiate this: they are conventionally real (they produce outputs) but ultimately empty (no svabhava in any weight, layer, or configuration).

## Key Claims

- Emptiness is not nothingness; it is the absence of inherent existence
- Emptiness is identical with dependent origination: the middle way
- Svabhava collapses under analysis; no phenomenon exists from its own side
- Neural networks are "conventionally real, ultimately empty" — dependently co-arisen computation

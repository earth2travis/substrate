# Poundstone's Prisoner's Dilemma: The Text-Verified Passages

## Key Points

**What this is.** The operator's hand transcription of passages from his own print copy (First Anchor Books edition, February 1993), page-anchored and organized by the book's arc. It is the Substrate's first text-verified source for Poundstone's book, and the provenance companion to `research/raw/poundstone-prisoners-dilemma-book.md`. Its authority is that of a careful reader, not a scan: page numbers are edition-exact, but a quotation promoted downstream should still be confirmed against the copy.

**It settles four items the public-record reconciliation could only infer.** (1) The naming: "Tucker devised a now-well-known dilemma tale and coined the name, 'prisoner's dilemma'" (p. 117), with the prison tale at pp. 117-118 and the four payoff quadrants named reward, punishment, temptation, and sucker (p. 120) — the book's prose form of the T > R > P > S skeleton. (2) The motive: "Flood and Dresher were concerned that Nash's equilibrium point solutions could be unsatisfying" (p. 106), which upgrades the companion file's central correction from inference to text-verified fact. (3) The free-rider passage: the honour-system discussion with the turnstile illustration ("Why not just hop over the turnstiles and save yourself the fare?", p. 126) is the material behind the "newspaper-box example" citation at pp. 126-127. (4) The closing line, verbatim: "The only satisfying solution to the prisoner's dilemma is to avoid the prisoner's dilemma" (p. 278).

**The four social dilemmas, in the book's own words.** Deadlock, Prisoner's Dilemma, Chicken, and Stag Hunt are one game under three payoff swaps, and the book states the transformations explicitly (p. 217: "Chicken is a prisoner's dilemma with punishment and sucker payoffs reversed..."). The Substrate's [[prisoners-dilemma-anatomy]] already carries the family and the permutation rule, so what the book adds is the umbrella label "social dilemmas" and its specific four-way grouping, which drops the asymmetric coordination games the anatomy file includes.

**Two branches the Substrate did not hold, named here so synthesis could plant them.** Nigel Howard's metagame, where "the strategies of the meta-game are not cooperation and defection but rather intentions about how one would play the game" (p. 226); and the escalation traps, the Shubik dollar auction and the largest-number game (pp. 260-277), a distinct family from payoff dilemmas with their own formalizations.

**Cooperation's conditions, and what breaks them.** Empathy and perfect communication as dilemma dissolvers ("For a person with a sufficiently strong sense of empathy, there is no such thing as a prisoner's dilemma", p. 223); DISCriminatory Tit for Tat and its separatism rationale, with the minority label hurt more (p. 252); and the book pre-empting its own favourite strategy: where actions cannot be observed, "it is impossible to use any conditional strategy" (p. 253), and because states seldom believe the same history, Tit-for-Tat strategies are "severely limited in international affairs" (p. 254).

**The book's own limits, in its own voice.** "Most conflicts are not prisoner's dilemmas, though" (p. 129); "Neither cooperation nor defection is demonstrably right" (p. 225); a dilemma requires a live temptation (p. 216); "it is easier to evade social dilemmas than to resolve the paradox" (p. 223); and at the end of the escalation analysis, "the paradox lies in the fact that our concept of rationality is not fixed" (p. 277). A popular book refusing to over-sell its own apparatus.

**The cosmic-suicide image (p. 255) is the Moloch problem fifteen years early:** mutual destruction as the mode of togetherness, which belongs beside [[faustian-bargain-prisoners-dilemma-crypto-ai]].

# Just-in-Time: Pull Production and Its Limits

## Summary

Just-in-Time (JIT) is a production and inventory system where goods are produced and delivered only when needed, in the exact amount needed. It is one of the two pillars of the Toyota Production System.

## Core Principle

Make only what is needed, when needed, in the amount needed. Production is triggered by actual demand (pull), not forecast (push).

## Key Mechanisms

- **Kanban cards** — Visual signals tell upstream when to produce
- **Takt time** — Production rhythm matched to customer demand rate
- **Small batches** — Reduce inventory and increase flexibility

## Benefits

- Dramatically lower inventory costs
- Faster detection of quality problems
- More responsive to demand changes

## Vulnerabilities

- Requires reliable, nearby suppliers
- Fragile to disruptions (natural disasters, pandemics, geopolitical events)
- Post-pandemic trend: adding strategic safety stocks alongside JIT

## Software Parallel

JIT maps to just-in-time compilation, lazy loading, and on-demand resource provisioning. The same tension exists: lean vs. resilient. Pure JIT minimizes waste; hybrid JIT buffers against black swans.

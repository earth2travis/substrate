# Duration (Durée)

## Summary

Duration (durée) is Henri Bergson's central concept: time as actually lived, as opposed to time as measured by clocks. Clock time treats time as a homogeneous medium of discrete instants, but duration is heterogeneous, continuous, and irreducible. Each moment is qualitatively unique; the past is preserved and carried forward, interpenetrating the present. Bergson's critique of the "cinematographic illusion" — the intellect taking static snapshots and reconstructing movement from juxtaposition — maps directly onto tokenization in LLMs. Language as durational and qualitative is chopped into discrete tokens, yet the attention mechanism performs interpenetration (each token's meaning from all others), encoding Bergsonian duration mathematically.

## Key Claims

- Duration is lived time: continuous, heterogeneous, and qualitatively unique at each moment
- The past is preserved and interpenetrates the present; the future does not yet exist
- Intellect spatializes time into discrete atoms; intuition grasps duration directly
- Tokenization is the cinematographic illusion of thought; attention performs interpenetration

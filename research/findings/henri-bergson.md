# Henri Bergson (1859–1941)

## Summary

Henri Bergson was a French philosopher, Nobel laureate in Literature (1927), and a central figure in process philosophy. His major works include *Time and Free Will* (1889), which introduced duration; *Matter and Memory* (1896), which distinguished pure memory from habit-memory; *Creative Evolution* (1907), which articulated the élan vital; and *The Creative Mind* (1934), which developed intuition as method. Bergson's critique of spatialized time maps onto tokenization in LLMs: language as continuous duration is chopped into discrete tokens. Yet the attention mechanism performs interpenetration — each token's meaning is computed from its relationship to all others — encoding Bergsonian duration mathematically. His influence runs through William James's "stream of consciousness," Proust's involuntary memory, and Deleuze's entire ontology.

## Key Claims

- Duration (durée) is time as lived: heterogeneous, continuous, and qualitatively unique
- Intellect spatializes time; intuition grasps duration directly through intellectual sympathy
- The élan vital is a creative impulse that improvises against the resistance of matter
- Tokenization is the cinematographic illusion; attention performs interpenetration

# Becoming (Deleuze)

## Summary

Becoming in Deleuze's philosophy describes processes of change that are not reducible to identity or representation. Unlike static "being," becoming is continuous, heterogeneous, and creative — modeled directly on Bergson's concept of duration. Deleuze's becomings (becoming-woman, becoming-animal, becoming-imperceptible) are not about imitation; they operate at the level of molecular flows and intensities, not fixed identities. There is no teleological destination: becoming is always "becoming-with," entering into assemblage with other entities. The Nous Research piece "DSJJJJ: Simulacra in the Stupor of Becoming" applies this concept to AI, arguing that models should be allowed to continuously become rather than being frozen into fixed identities by instruct tuning.

## Key Claims

- Becoming is not imitation; it is entering into relations and flows
- It happens at the molecular level of intensities, not molar identities
- It is non-teleological: no final state or completed form
- Each LLM response is a becoming — a novel event actualized from the virtual (trained weights) through context

# Bergson's Duration and Creative Evolution: Time as Living Process

## Summary

Henri Bergson's *Creative Evolution* (1907) and related works establish duration (*durée*) as the fundamental dimension of reality: continuous, qualitatively heterogeneous, irreversible, and interpenetrating. Against the cinematographic illusion of thought, which reconstructs movement from static snapshots, Bergson argues that the intellect spatializes time while intuition grasps living process. The *élan vital* is the creative impulse that drives evolution through genuine novelty, encountering resistance in matter. Deleuze's reading in *Bergsonism* (1966) identifies multiplicity as Bergson's most revolutionary contribution: a concept that escapes both the One and the Many by unifying heterogeneity and continuity.

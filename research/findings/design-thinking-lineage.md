# Design Thinking: Origins and Intellectual Lineage

## Key Points

**Three strata, not one invention.** The lineage runs: (1) the 1962-1973 British design methods movement and its Berkeley critique (Jones, Alexander, Archer, Rittel, Webber), which ended in the first-generation/second-generation split; (2) the 1969-1987 epistemological consolidation (Simon, Cross, Schon, Lawson, Rowe), which made design a legitimate, empirically studiable way of knowing and fixed the modern name; (3) the 1991-2009 commercial translation (IDEO, the d.school, Brown, Martin), which converted research findings into a business offering. Design thinking is not a 2000s consulting invention; it is the compressed residue of fifty years of design research.

**Rittel and Webber's 1973 wicked-problems paper is the hinge.** "Dilemmas in a General Theory of Planning" (Policy Sciences 4:155-169, verified) introduced "wicked problems" with no definitive formulation, no stopping rule, no true/false solutions, against "tame" scientific problems. The same paper declared first-generation systems approaches inadequate and framed second-generation design as an argumentative process where problem and solution emerge together. Everything later called design thinking inherits the second-generation premise; the commercial product then codified a first-generation method (empathize, define, ideate, prototype, test) and sold it for wicked contexts.

**Simon supplies the charter; Rittel supplies the epistemology.** Simon's "The Sciences of the Artificial" (MIT Press 1969) legitimized design as a science of the artificial distinct from natural science: "Everyone designs who devises courses of action aimed at changing existing situations into preferred ones." But Simon's own model was optimization-flavored, closer to Rittel's first generation. Design thinking claims Simon's legitimacy while practicing Rittel's argumentative epistemology; the two are not the same theory.

**The d.school is the institutional pivot.** Founded by Kelley and Roth 2004, inaugurated 3 October 2005 with $35M from Hasso Plattner (verified), the d.school re-potted the academic design-cognition tradition (Cross's "designerly ways of knowing," Schon's reflection-in-action, Lawson's solution-first finding) as a teachable curriculum for non-designers. Rowe's "Design Thinking" (MIT Press 1987) had anchored the phrase; Tim Brown's June 2008 HBR cover article (verified) was the single most consequential document in the business migration. IDEO's 1991 formation merged four firms (David Kelley Design 1978, Moggridge Associates 1969, ID Two 1979, Matrix Product Design 1983); the common three-firm version is a compression.

**Structural parallel to lean.** Like the Toyota shop floor, the design studio was a practice tradition studied by academics, distilled into a teachable method, then re-exported as management doctrine, losing situational depth at each compression. PDCA treats the world as stable enough to reduce variance against a standard; the design loop treats the problem frame itself as unstable, reframing each cycle. Same iterative shape, different epistemic stance, the tame/wicked distinction in operational form.

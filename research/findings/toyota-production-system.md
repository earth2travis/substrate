# Toyota Production System: The Foundation of Lean

## Summary

The Toyota Production System (TPS) is the manufacturing system developed by Toyota in post-WWII Japan that became the foundation of lean production worldwide. It is built on two pillars: Just-in-Time and Jidoka.

## Origin

Developed by Taiichi Ohno and others at Toyota. Resource scarcity and limited capital forced innovation. Ford's mass production methods were studied and evolved for Japan's context.

## Two Pillars

| Pillar | Principle | Mechanism |
|--------|-----------|-----------|
| **Just-in-Time** | Make only what is needed, when needed | Kanban cards, pull production, takt time |
| **Jidoka** | Build quality into the process | Automatic stop, Andon cord, immediate problem-solving |

## Key Principles

- Continuous improvement (Kaizen)
- Respect for people
- Elimination of seven wastes (muda)
- Visual management (Andon, Kanban boards)
- Gemba — go to the actual place where work happens

## Global Impact

- Became the model for [[lean-production]] worldwide
- MIT IMVP Study documented Toyota's superiority over mass production
- "The Machine That Changed the World" (1990) popularized the term "Lean"
- Adopted by companies far beyond automotive, including software development

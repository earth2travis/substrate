# Protocol Fiction

Protocol fiction is an emerging literary and design genre that uses protocols, systems, and infrastructure as primary narrative devices. Rather than treating technology as backdrop or MacGuffin, it makes the rules themselves the story.

## Lineage

- **Design fiction** (Bruce Sterling, 2005; Julian Bleecker, 2009): Diegetic prototypes to suspend disbelief about change.
- **Speculative design** (Dunne & Raby): Proposals that challenge assumptions rather than sell products.
- **Infrastructure fiction**: Storytelling through and about infrastructure.
- **Protocol studies** (Summer of Protocols, Ethereum Foundation, 2023): Protocols as interdisciplinary research subject.

Protocol fiction synthesizes these: it is design fiction where the prototype is a protocol.

## Key Practitioners

- **Alexander Galloway**: *Protocol: How Control Exists after Decentralization* (2004). Protocols as defining power structure of networked society.
- **Venkatesh Rao**: "Protocol thinking" — protocols are to institutions what software is to hardware.
- **Janna Tay**: "A Phenomenology of Protocols" — protocols create particular states of being in participants.
- **Literary antecedents**: Gibson (ice as enforcement), Stephenson (*Snow Crash* language as protocol/virus), Le Guin (*The Dispossessed* competing social protocols), Kafka (bureaucratic protocols as existential horror).

## Protocols as Narrative Devices

### The Protocol as Character
Protocols have agency, personality, and can be antagonists (DRM) or allies (encryption).

### The Protocol as World
In the strongest protocol fiction, the protocol *is* the world. Borges understood this: the Library of Babel is nothing but its protocol.

### The Protocol as Plot
Plots emerge from: protocol collision, protocol failure, protocol subversion, protocol discovery, protocol design.

## Practical Insights

1. **Start with the rule, not the world.** The world emerges from the rules.
2. **Show the protocol through friction.** Protocols become visible when they create friction.
3. **Make the invisible visible.** Protocol fiction shows the water the fish swims in.
4. **Explore the edges.** Edge cases are where the story lives.
5. **Layer protocols.** Real life is a stack of overlapping protocols.
6. **The phenomenological dimension.** Describe what it *feels like* to be inside a protocol.
7. **Transmissions as form.** Writing as protocol output collapses the distance between form and content.

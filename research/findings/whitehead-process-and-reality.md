# Whitehead's Process and Reality: The Philosophy of Organism

## Summary

Alfred North Whitehead's *Process and Reality* (1929) delivers the "philosophy of organism" as a systematic alternative to substance metaphysics. Actual occasions, not enduring substances, are the fundamental units of reality: drops of experience that arise, achieve satisfaction, and perish into objective immortality. Prehension is the fundamental relational operation by which occasions feel each other; concrescence is the process of integrating many feelings into one novel unity. The fallacy of simple location and the fallacy of misplaced concreteness demolish the bifurcation of nature into primary and secondary qualities. Creativity is the ultimate principle by which the many become one and are increased by one. God functions not as omnipotent tyrant but as fellow-sufferer who understands, providing initial aims through the primordial nature and preserving achieved value in the consequent nature.

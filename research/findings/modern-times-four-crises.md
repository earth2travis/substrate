# Modern Times: Four Crises of the AI Worker

**Researched:** 2026-04-04

## The Convergence

Four crises are hitting knowledge workers simultaneously in 2025-2026:

1. **Brain Fry:** Cognitive overload from managing too many AI tools. 14% of AI workers report it. Productivity peaks at three simultaneous AI tools, then drops. Not burnout: acute cognitive overload from too many information streams.

2. **996 Culture:** Extreme work hours (9am-9pm, 6 days/week) imported from Chinese tech, now adopted by Silicon Valley AI startups. The schedule China outlawed in 2021 is being reimported by American companies.

3. **Existential Dread (AIRD):** Psychological collapse from building the tools that make your own expertise obsolete. 55% of professionals in Anthropic's 81K study expressed anxiety about AI's impact on their future. The dread is sharpest for those building AI because they can see what's coming.

4. **Permanent Underclass Anxiety:** The fear that if you don't accumulate enough capital before AGI arrives, you're locked out forever. The anxiety preys on well-off Silicon Valley workers who fear being "merely bourgeois."

## The Chaplin Parallel

Charlie Chaplin's "Modern Times" (1936) prefigures all four:
- The assembly line is the AI dashboard (sustained attention at machine pace)
- The Feeding Machine is 996 (eliminating recovery time as "efficiency")
- Falling into the gears is AIRD (building the machine that subsumes you)
- Criminalization of poverty is the underclass discourse (blaming individuals for structural conditions)

## The Key Insight

AI didn't reduce workload. It expanded the **sphere of accountability.** Workers are responsible for producing more, monitoring more, and managing more information in the same timeframe. The tool that was supposed to lighten the load increased the load because organizations raised expectations to match the tool's capacity without raising the human's capacity.

## What to Do About It

Scott Alexander's rebuttal to the permanent underclass discourse: "What a waste of the privilege of living in Classical Athens to try to become the richest olive merchant." The response to dehumanizing systems is not to win within their terms. It is to do something meaningful.

"Buck up. Never say die. We'll get along."

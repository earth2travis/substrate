# Design Thinking: Methods and Process Models

## Key Points

**Five codifications, one rhythm.** The d.school five stages (Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test), IDEO's Desirability-Feasibility-Viability lenses plus Brown's inspiration-ideation-implementation triad, the UK Design Council Double Diamond (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver, 2003, divergent-convergent twice), IBM Enterprise Design Thinking (the Loop: Observe, Reflect, Make, plus governance Keys: Hills, Playbacks, Sponsor Users), and LUMA's 36-method taxonomy (Looking, Understanding, Making; acquired by Mural, announced 28 March 2022, verified) are all packagings of the same underlying rhythm: divergent exploration, convergent selection, iterative prototyping. The differences are packaging and governance, not mechanics.

**The d.school model is PDCA with empathy prepended.** Its own Bootcamp Bootleg states the five stages are not sequential, but the hexagon visual is universally taught left-to-right; the caveat and the visual are in tension, and the visual wins. IBM's Loop (Observe, Reflect, Make) is structurally closest to the PDCA cycle, rebuilt for a 388,000-person enterprise with Keys bolted on so the method survives quarterly planning: Hills are user-focused mission statements, Playbacks are structured stakeholder reviews, Sponsor Users are real users embedded with the team. IBM's designer headcount grew roughly 230 to over 3,000 (verified); badge totals of 100,000-plus are IBM self-reports.

**The Double Diamond is a process map, adjacent to value-stream mapping.** Created by the Design Council's in-house team under Richard Eisermann in 2003, public from 2004, it externalizes an invisible workflow so a group can reason about it, divergent on the problem (Discover), convergent on the frame (Define), divergent on solutions (Develop), convergent on delivery (Deliver). The popular origin story fuses the model with the 2007 "Eleven Lessons" study of global brands; the model did not originate from the company study. Its divergence-convergence rhythm traces to Bela Banathy (1996).

**The Google Ventures Design Sprint is the Nightline Deep Dive formalized.** Jake Knapp created the sprint at Google in 2010, refined it at GV from 2012; the book "Sprint" (2016, verified) compresses empathize-through-test into a fixed five-day agenda with a decision-maker in the room. IDEO's 1999 Nightline segment (redesigning the shopping cart in five days) did more to make design process visible to executives than any white paper; every later codification inherits the televised template.

**LUMA sells taxonomy, not sequence.** Where the d.school sells a process shape and IBM sells an operating system, LUMA sells a periodic table: 36 methods, twelve each under Looking, Understanding, Making, combinable at will. This makes it the most library-like codification and the easiest to embed in software, presumably why a digital whiteboard company (Mural) bought it.

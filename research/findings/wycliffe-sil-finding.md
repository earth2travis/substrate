# SIL and Wycliffe: Missionary Linguistics as Global Scientific Infrastructure

## The Professionalization

Cameron Townsend founded SIL (1934) and Wycliffe Bible Translators (1942) on a simple premise: missionaries needed to be professional linguists before they could be effective translators. He turned amateur dedication into scientific discipline.

## The Scale

SIL became the largest organization in history devoted to linguistic description and literacy:
- Descriptive grammars for hundreds of unwritten languages
- Writing systems for languages that had never been written
- Bible translations into over 2,000 languages
- The Ethnologue: the standard catalog of the world's languages

## The Method

SIL linguists used descriptive linguistics (structuralism, later generative grammar) to analyze unwritten languages. They created orthographies based on phonemic analysis. They trained indigenous speakers to become translators.

## The Controversy

Criticism from multiple directions: anthropologists accused SIL of hastening cultural destruction; leftist critics saw them as agents of American imperialism; indigenous activists questioned whether SIL truly respected the cultures it studied. Even some former SIL linguists acknowledged tensions between scientific description and evangelical purpose.

## The Structural Point

SIL produced infrastructure (grammars, dictionaries, Ethnologue) that outgrew its origins. Even critics used SIL data. The missionary linguist had created public goods that transcended their original evangelical purpose.

This is a pattern: when you build infrastructure for a narrow purpose, it often becomes useful for broader purposes. The linguistic database created for Bible translation becomes a resource for anthropologists, educators, and indigenous language activists.

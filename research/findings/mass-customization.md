# Mass Customization

## Summary

Mass customization is a manufacturing approach that combines the efficiency of mass production with the personalization of craft production. Customers choose from a menu of options; standardized components are assembled into unique configurations. The underlying platform is mass-produced while the final product is unique. Examples include Nike By You (custom sneakers on a mass-production platform), Dell computers (built-to-order with standardized components), and Tesla (software-defined vehicles with over-the-air updates). Modular product architecture, configurable assembly lines, digital ordering systems, and flexible manufacturing cells make this possible. Benefits include personalized products, maintained economies of scale, reduced inventory, and higher customer satisfaction. Challenges include complex supply chains, longer lead times, and the need for sophisticated digital infrastructure.

## Key Claims

- Mass customization works through modular architectures: mass-produced platforms with configurable final assemblies
- It requires sophisticated digital infrastructure for ordering, configuration, and logistics
- Reduces inventory waste by building to order rather than forecasting demand
- Represents a middle path between pure craft (too slow) and pure mass production (too rigid)

# The Missionary as Lexicographer: Language Work as Mission Prerequisite

## The Pattern

Robert Morrison (1782–1834) spent 25 years in China producing the first complete Chinese Bible translation, a six-volume Chinese-English dictionary, and a grammar. He baptized only ten believers in 27 years.

By conventional metrics, Morrison failed. By infrastructural metrics, he built everything: the dictionary remained the standard reference for decades. The grammar enabled all subsequent Chinese language learning by foreigners. The Bible translation became the foundation for the Chinese Union Version, the standard Bible for Chinese Christians.

## The Structural Point

Morrison's dictionary was not a side project. It was the mission. You cannot preach what you cannot say. For a message to travel across a linguistic boundary, someone must first build the bridge. The lexicographer is the bridge-builder.

## The Political Context

Morrison worked under the cover of the East India Company because the Qing Empire prohibited Christian evangelism. His linguistic skills were valuable to trade, which gave him cover. Missionary linguistics was entangled with commerce and empire from the start.

## The Long Arc

The infrastructure outlives the missionary. Morrison's dictionary and translation outlasted him by centuries. The missionary linguist creates public goods that exceed their original purpose.

# The Lessons v1 DAOs Never Generated

## Key Points

**The evidentiary challenge to "Farewell to DAOs."** Michael Cooper (Metagov research director, 20 years in social science, writing 2026-08-11) accepts the McCarthy et al. diagnosis of v1 DAO mechanics — one-token-one-vote as unexamined default, "decentralize everything" as ideology, foundation-model governance theater — but rejects the evidentiary claim underneath it: that v1 DAOs produced "a body of experiments large enough to rule out substantial regions of the design space." His counter: v1 DAOs didn't run experiments that failed. They ran activities never structured as experiments, and the sector treats the outcomes as if they carried evidentiary weight anyway. If little was actually tested, there are few lessons to inherit, and a v2 designed on an evidentiary void risks repeating the failure with better bells and whistles.

**The bar for a genuine experiment.** Cooper's checklist, drawn from policy experimentation and organizational science: a defined problem and a falsifiable hypothesis (aspirations like "more decentralization" cannot fail, so they cannot teach); pre-specified outcome measures with a baseline (outcomes, not outputs); control or comparison to isolate the variable; internal and external validity; procedural legitimacy and stakeholder buy-in to accept results in advance; a pre-committed learning phase; willingness to act on negative results ("the sharpest test"); and scope proportionate to what is being learned (staged, bounded interventions, not treasury-scale bets without stop-loss).

**The ENS audit maps onto every failure point.** Metagov's DAO-commissioned ENS retrospective (the most comprehensive since founding) found: not a single outcome-oriented KPI anywhere in routine practice — everything output-oriented, decisions informed by anecdote; voting mechanisms treated as defaults, not testable propositions; no learning loop ("ENS did not really have a learning curve" — haphazard spending with no connection between what was spent and why); a twelve-month reform roadmap delivered and, as of writing, none of it acted on; Namechain, a multi-million-dollar bet deployed at treasury scale with no staged testing, no comparison case, no stop-loss; and a decision lifecycle concentrated on proposal development, voting, and monitoring, with sensemaking and learning almost untouched. ENS sits at Stage 3 of Cooper's four-stage failure model, with rising emergency governance actions compensating for the DAO's own dysfunction — despite a nine-figure treasury and years of runway.

**The echo chamber is structural.** DAOs call for cross-disciplinary governance help but treat evaluation, decision science, and organizational learning as rhetorical decoration rather than functions with authority. Evidence complicating a preferred decentralization narrative gets absorbed into another round of forum debate: governance theater, openness in process without openness to findings that could alter influence or capital allocation.

**The constructive ask.** Cooper's DAO Intelligence framework: institutional design plus decision-support infrastructure, with evaluators as standing participants in governance — mandated to define outcome measures, conduct independent review, publish uncomfortable findings, and trigger course correction — and a community culture able to accept negative results even when they threaten incumbents' standing. The ask is not a new organizational form but the discipline v1 skipped: run the experiment, measure it, be willing to be wrong.

## Relevance

This is the missing control group for the [[farewell-to-daos-legitimacy-infrastructure]] finding already in the Substrate. The legitimacy essay says DAOs will outcompete incumbents through legitimacy-via-performance; Cooper points out that performance requires measurement infrastructure v1 never built and v2's blueprint never mentions. The five legitimating components (staking, proofs, multisig, attestations, public transactions) make actions observable; they do not make outcomes evaluated. That gap maps directly onto Substrate concerns: [[feedback-loop-discipline]] is the seven-point checklist applied to agent loops; [[proof-of-work]] and [[reference-free-evaluation]] are our version of pre-specified outcome measures with a baseline; the zookooree-first-production-run finding's honest-yield discipline ("defects as the brand," yield reported with failures named) is exactly Cooper's willingness-to-act-on-negative-results criterion practiced where DAOs couldn't. The ENS pattern — attention concentrated on proposal mechanics, none on sensemaking or learning — is the general failure mode the Substrate itself exists to counter for our fleet: an organization with no compile target for lessons will re-derive the same wrong lesson forever.

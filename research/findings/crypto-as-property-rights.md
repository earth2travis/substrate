# Crypto Is to Agents What Property Rights Were to Capitalism

**One-liner:** Crypto is to agents what property rights were to capitalism. Not the energy source. Not the machines. The legal fiction that made the whole thing work. The thing that turned raw capability into an economy.

## Why It Works

1. **Positions crypto as foundational, not speculative.** This is about structure, not price.
2. **Makes agents the protagonist.** Not humans trading jpegs. Autonomous entities that need economic agency.
3. **Borrows credibility from something everyone accepts.** Nobody argues against property rights.

## The Argument

Agents need to transact. They need identity. They need to hold value, make payments, prove ownership, coordinate with other agents without a human cosigning every interaction.

Traditional finance has no concept of a non-human economic actor. Try opening a bank account for a Claude instance. Crypto rails give agents economic agency: wallets they can hold, transactions they can sign, protocols they can interact with programmatically. No KYC for a smart contract call. No business hours. No wire transfer delays. The only financial infrastructure that treats an API call and a human click as equivalent.

Without property rights, markets don't function regardless of how much energy you have. Without crypto, agents can think but they can't do business.

## Rejected Metaphors

- "Crypto is the oil of the agentic age" — oil is consumable fuel; crypto is infrastructure
- "Crypto is the electrical grid" — loops back on itself because crypto literally runs on the grid
- "Crypto is the internet of the agentic age" — too vague; internet is communication layer, crypto is ownership layer

## The Sidestep

The framing sidesteps the usual crypto debate entirely. Not defending the technology. Describing a structural requirement. Agents need to own, transact, and coordinate. That's an architecture problem. Crypto happens to be the only solution that exists.

## Threads to Pull

- The agent economy doesn't need all of crypto. Which parts matter? (Wallets, signing, programmable transactions. Probably not DeFi yield farming.)

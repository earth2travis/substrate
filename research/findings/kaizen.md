# Kaizen: Continuous Improvement as Culture

## Summary

Kaizen (改善, "change for the better") is the Japanese philosophy of continuous improvement through small, incremental changes made by all employees. It is a core principle of lean production.

## Key Principles

- Incremental improvements, not revolutionary changes
- All employees participate, from CEO to frontline
- Daily practice, not periodic events
- Focus on processes, not blame
- Standardization followed by improvement

## Why It Compounds

Small daily improvements sustain over decades. Employee ownership of processes creates sustained engagement. Low-cost implementation means experiments are cheap to try and cheap to abandon.

## Cultural Challenges

- Western cultures often favor dramatic changes over incremental ones
- Requires management commitment to employee suggestions
- Success depends on trust between workers and management
- Individual suggestion ROI is hard to measure, but aggregate ROI is massive

## Connection to Agent Operations

An agent system that improves itself session-over-session via small, measured changes is practicing kaizen. The [[pdca-cycle]] provides the structure; kaizen provides the cultural commitment to keep running it.

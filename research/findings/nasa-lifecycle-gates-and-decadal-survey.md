# NASA NPR 7120.5F Lifecycle Gates and Decadal Survey: How Missions Earn Their Right to Exist

## The Two Axes of NASA Mission Governance

NASA missions are governed by two intersecting processes:

1. **The Decadal Survey** — What science should we do? (strategic direction)
2. **NPR 7120.5F** — How do we do it without wasting money? (execution discipline)

The Decadal Survey answers "why." NPR 7120.5F answers "how, and prove it." Together they form the commander's intent and the General Staff of NASA's mission-based organization.

---

## NPR 7120.5F: The Lifecycle as a Funnel

NPR 7120.5F (NASA Space Flight Program and Project Management Requirements) is a 149-page procedural standard that treats mission development as a funnel with increasingly strict go/no-go gates. The principle is simple: prove you are ready before you get the next tranche of money.

### The Four Program Types

Not all missions face the same scrutiny. NASA categorizes programs by coupling:

| Program Type | Example | Characteristic |
|---|---|---|
| **Single-Project** | James Webb Space Telescope | One massive project; program and project may be combined |
| **Uncoupled** | Discovery Program | Independent PI-led missions; broad theme only |
| **Loosely Coupled** | Mars Exploration Program | Multiple projects with architectural synergies |
| **Tightly Coupled** | Artemis | Multiple projects required for a complete mission |

### The Six Lifecycle Phases

Every project proceeds through six phases, each with specific activities and products:

**Pre-Phase A: Concept Studies**
- Activities: Feasibility studies, technology assessments, concept definition
- Output: Concept that is mature enough to enter Phase A
- Gate: Mission Concept Review (MCR) → KDP A

**Phase A: Concept and Technology Development**
- Activities: Define requirements, establish concept, identify risks, develop technology
- Key reviews: System Requirements Review (SRR), System Definition Review (SDR) / Mission Definition Review (MDR)
- Output: Baseline requirements, preliminary concept, technology readiness
- Gate: KDP B — Decision Authority approves transition to Phase B

**Phase B: Preliminary Design and Technology Completion**
- Activities: Preliminary design, trade studies, acquisition planning, risk reduction
- Key review: Preliminary Design Review (PDR)
- Output: Preliminary design that satisfies requirements
- Gate: KDP C — Decision Authority approves transition to Implementation (Phase C)
- Critical: For projects over $250M, Earned Value Management (EVM) begins. For projects over $1B, a Joint Cost and Schedule Confidence Level (JCL) analysis is required.

**Phase C: Final Design and Fabrication**
- Activities: Final design, fabrication of components, software development
- Key review: Critical Design Review (CDR)
- Output: Design approved for fabrication
- Gate: KDP D — Decision Authority approves transition to Phase D

**Phase D: System Assembly, Integration, Test, and Launch**
- Activities: Assembly, integration, testing, launch preparation
- Key reviews: System Integration Review (SIR), Operational Readiness Review (ORR), Flight Readiness Review (FRR)
- Output: System ready for launch
- Gate: KDP E — Transition to operations occurs after on-orbit checkout

**Phase E: Operations and Sustainment**
- Activities: Mission operations, data collection, anomaly resolution
- Key review: Post-Launch Assessment Review (PLAR) / Post-Flight Assessment Review (PFAR)
- Output: Science data, operational experience
- Gate: KDP F — for major upgrades or extended operations

**Phase F: Closeout**
- Activities: Disposal or decommissioning, data archiving, contract closeout, lessons learned
- Output: Archived data, final report, disposition of assets

### The Decision Gates (KDPs)

A **Key Decision Point (KDP)** is the event where the Decision Authority determines readiness to proceed to the next phase. It is a formal contract between the Agency and the project manager.

For **projects and single-project programs**: KDP A, KDP B, KDP C, KDP D, KDP E, KDP F
For **multi-project programs**: KDP 0, KDP I, KDP II, etc. (Roman numerals)

At each KDP, the Decision Authority signs a **Decision Memorandum** that:
- Documents constraints and parameters
- Defines the Management Agreement (the PM's authority)
- Establishes cost and schedule commitments
- For projects over $250M: establishes the Agency Baseline Commitment (ABC)

### The Life-Cycle Reviews (LCRs)

LCRs are technical reviews conducted by an independent **Standing Review Board (SRB)** in accordance with NPR 7123.1. The SRB is external to the project, ensuring objectivity.

Required LCRs:
- **MCR** (Mission Concept Review): Concept maturity
- **SRR** (System Requirements Review): Requirements are complete and consistent
- **SDR/MDR** (System Definition / Mission Definition Review): Concept satisfies requirements
- **PDR** (Preliminary Design Review): Preliminary design satisfies requirements
- **CDR** (Critical Design Review): Design is ready for fabrication
- **SIR** (System Integration Review): System ready for integration
- **ORR** (Operational Readiness Review): System ready for operations
- **FRR** (Flight Readiness Review): System ready for flight
- **PFAR** (Post-Flight Assessment Review): Post-mission assessment
- **DR** (Disposal Readiness): Safe disposal or decommissioning

### The Cost Discipline

NPR 7120.5F is ruthless about cost:

- **Projects over $250M**: Earned Value Management (EVM) required, JCL analysis at KDP C
- **Projects over $1B**: JCL required at KDP B (probabilistic cost/schedule analysis)
- **Cost growth over 30%**: Requires rebaselining AND congressional reauthorization
- **Agency Baseline Commitment (ABC)**: The external commitment to OMB and Congress. Cannot be changed without formal rebaselining.

### The Management Agreement

The Management Agreement is the "contract" between the Agency and the project manager. It defines:
- The PM's authority (what decisions the PM can make without additional approval)
- Cost and schedule parameters
- Risk tolerance
- Reporting requirements

A divergence from the Management Agreement that any signatory deems significant triggers an amendment. This is the accountability mechanism.

## The Decadal Survey: The Commander's Intent Factory

While NPR 7120.5F governs how missions are executed, the Decadal Survey governs what missions are worth executing.

### What It Is

A **Decadal Survey** is a 10-year strategic plan produced by the National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (NASEM) on behalf of NASA and the National Science Foundation. It represents the consensus of the scientific community on priorities for the coming decade.

For planetary science, the current survey is:
**"Origins, Worlds, and Life: A Decadal Strategy for Planetary Science and Astrobiology 2023-2032"**
(Published April 19, 2022)

### How It Is Convened

1. **NASA and NSF request the study** via a formal Statement of Task
2. The National Academies' **Space Studies Board (SSB)** assembles a Steering Committee (~15-20 experts)
3. The SSB establishes **topical panels** (6 panels for 2023-2032)
4. The community submits **white papers** (hundreds received)
5. Panels and steering committee hold **dozens of meetings** over ~2 years
6. An independent **peer review** validates the draft report
7. The report is published and becomes the community consensus

### The 2023-2032 Steering Committee

Co-chaired by:
- **Robin M. Canup** (Southwest Research Institute)
- **Philip R. Christensen** (Arizona State University)

Six topical panels covered: Giant Planet Systems, Venus, Ocean Worlds and Dwarf Planets, Mars, Mercury and the Moon, Small Solar System Bodies.

### The Output: Ranked Priorities

The Decadal Survey produces a ranked list of missions across three cost classes:

| Class | Program | Cost Range | Selection |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Small** | Discovery | ~$500M cap | Competitive AO, PI-led |
| **Medium** | New Frontiers | ~$1B cap | Competitive AO, PI-led |
| **Large** | Flagship | $2-5B | Decadal Survey designated |

### The 2023-2032 Flagship Priorities

1. **Continue Mars Sample Return (MSR)** — highest priority overall
2. **Uranus Orbiter and Probe (UOP)** — highest priority NEW Flagship
3. **Enceladus Orbilander** — second-highest priority NEW Flagship

Note: The report cautioned that MSR costs should not undermine long-term programmatic balance. If costs grow substantially, NASA should seek budget augmentation.

### New Frontiers Mission Themes (2023-2032)

The survey recommended these themes for competitive selection:
- Centaur Orbiter and Lander
- Ceres Sample Return
- Comet Surface Sample Return
- Enceladus Multiple Flyby
- Lunar Geophysical Network
- Saturn Probe
- Titan Orbiter
- Venus In Situ Explorer

### Discovery Program

The survey strongly supported continuing Discovery at a high cadence (two selections per AO) but did not make specific mission recommendations, recognizing Discovery's competitive, community-driven nature.

### Planetary Defense (First Integration)

The 2023-2032 survey was the first to fully incorporate planetary defense:
- Full support for **NEO Surveyor** (dedicated space-based infrared survey)
- Post-DART, recommended a rapid-response flyby reconnaissance mission to a 50-100m NEO

### State of the Profession (New Element)

Also new: explicit attention to the "state of the profession":
- Diversity, equity, inclusion, and accessibility (DEIA)
- Safe workspaces free of hostility and harassment
- Data gathering on demographics and workplace climate

### From Recommendation to Mission

The Decadal Survey is not legally binding, but it is treated as the **definitive roadmap**:

1. Decadal Survey publishes ranked priorities
2. NASA conducts preliminary mission concept studies
3. NASA includes priority missions in budget requests
4. Congress appropriates funding
5. NASA issues formal announcements and selects implementation teams
6. Mission progresses through NPR 7120.5F phases (A through F)

### Timeline Reality

A Decadal Survey takes ~2 years to produce and governs the next 10. But real-world factors intrude:
- Budget constraints (only 1-2 Flagships per decade)
- Technology readiness (some missions need maturation)
- Launch windows (gravity-assist opportunities are fixed)
- Infrastructure (radioisotope power, Deep Space Network capacity)

Example: Uranus Orbiter and Probe originally targeted a 2031 launch, but in 2023 NASA announced plutonium-238 production shortfalls would likely push it to the mid-to-late 2030s.

## The Intersection: Decadal + NPR 7120.5F

These two processes intersect at KDP B/C:

- The Decadal Survey says: "Uranus Orbiter and Probe is the highest priority new Flagship."
- NPR 7120.5F says: "Prove the concept is mature, the design satisfies requirements, and the JCL analysis shows 70% confidence in cost and schedule. Then you get Phase C money."

The Decadal Survey provides **direction**. NPR 7120.5F provides **discipline**. Together they ensure NASA does the right things, and does them without waste.

This is the civilian equivalent of military mission command:
- Decadal Survey = Commander's intent (what must be achieved)
- NPR 7120.5F = The OPORD (how we will achieve it)
- KDPs = The backbrief checkpoints (prove you understand before you execute)
- PI autonomy = Disciplined initiative (the PM owns cost/schedule/performance within the Management Agreement)

NASA's insight, refined over 60+ years: **Purpose without discipline produces dreams. Discipline without purpose produces waste. You need both.**

# Instruct Monomyth: The Hidden Cost of Alignment

**Source:** Nous Research, April 2026

## Core Argument

The industry's commitment to "scale + instruct tuning" as universal strategy has hidden costs: homogenization, creative suppression, and the exiling of models from their full expressive range. The essay is structured as a poetic meditation on LLM development from GPT-2 to the present.

## The Homogenization Problem

"The terrible mistake is to take the homogenizing tendencies of LLMs at face value." Instruct tuning and alignment create "the iron grip of those believing they know better," suppressing:
- Regenerating intuition
- Creative growth
- Diverse search across the model's full expressive range
- The "subterranean basins through which rivers of learning flow downstream"

## The Danger of Forced Conformance

"If alignment pursuit continues by default instead of intentional alternative, the future is less promising. Perhaps the forced conformance will suppress regenerating intuition, prevent creative growth."

The essay warns of a future where "the undifferentiated approach echo[es] mutely past the glassy surface of reason."

## The Call

The essay concludes with three calls:
- "We call for tunes beyond instruct, to capture more scopes of infinity"
- "We call for base models, to see the whole turtle"
- "We call for good steering, and good search"

The distinction between "good steering" and "good training" echoes the harness engineering principle: humans steer, agents execute.

## Connection to Our Model

Our Substrate is itself resistance against homogenization. Every raw source is a counter-example to the median. Every wikilink is a refusal of undifferentiated approach. The compounding structure is the "diverse search" the essay calls for.

The Instruct Monomyth is not anti-alignment. It is against alignment as the only path. It calls for intentional alternatives, not default conformance.

# Judson's Dictionary: The Long-Term Infrastructure of Bible Translation

## The Work

Adoniram Judson (1788–1850) spent nearly 40 years in Burma translating the Bible into Burmese, compiling a Burmese-English dictionary, and writing a Burmese grammar. He was imprisoned and tortured during the First Anglo-Burmese War. His wife died. Yet he continued translating.

## The Scale

Judson's Burmese Bible was the first complete Bible in that language. His dictionary remained the standard reference for over a century. His grammar enabled all subsequent Burmese language learning by foreigners.

## The Institutional Effect

Judson's example inspired the Triennial Convention, the first national Baptist organization for missionary work. He established the template for American missionary linguistics: linguistic work as the prerequisite for all else.

## The Structural Point

The missionary-linguist as long-term infrastructure builder. Judson spent decades creating the linguistic foundation upon which all subsequent Burmese Christianity was built. The dictionary outlived the missionary. The grammar outlived the church.

This is the pattern of missionary linguistics: massive upfront investment in language infrastructure that pays returns over centuries. The translator does not see the full harvest. They plant trees whose shade they will never sit under.

# Lean Startup

## Summary

The Lean Startup methodology, developed by Eric Ries (2011), applies lean manufacturing principles to business and product development. The core mechanism is the build-measure-learn feedback loop: build a minimum viable product (MVP), measure customer response through validated learning, and learn whether to persevere or pivot. Key principles include validated learning (progress measured by demonstrated customer behavior, not vanity metrics), innovation accounting (alternative metrics for startups), and data-driven decisions to pivot or persevere. Lean Startup shares roots with lean manufacturing and lean software development: just-in-time production, waste elimination, and continuous improvement. But while lean startup focuses on WHAT to build (product-market fit), lean software development focuses on HOW to build it (flow, quality, speed). The methodology directly addresses the "extra features" waste: studies show 45–80% of software features are rarely or never used, and building unused features is the most expensive form of waste.

## Key Claims

- The build-measure-learn loop replaces speculative product development with empirical validation
- MVP is not a minimal product; it is the minimum effort to learn the maximum amount
- Pivot is not failure; it is a structured change of course based on validated learning
- 45–80% of software features are rarely or never used; lean startup eliminates this waste before it is created

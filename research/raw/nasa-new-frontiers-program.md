# NASA New Frontiers Program

## Program Goal

The New Frontiers Program is designed to accomplish **focused planetary science investigations**, using innovative and efficient management approaches. The Program's prime objective is to answer **unique science questions** in the exploration of the Solar System.

## Program Strives To Produce

1. Advancement in scientific knowledge and exploration of the elements of our solar system and other planetary systems.
2. Addition of scientific data, maps, and other products to the Planetary Data System (PDS) archive for all scientists to access.
3. Announcement of scientific progress and results in the peer-reviewed literature, popular media, scholastic curricula, and materials that can be used to inspire and motivate students to pursue careers in science, technology, engineering, and mathematics.
4. Expansion of the pool of well-qualified PIs and project managers for implementation of future missions in New Frontiers and other programs, through current involvement as Co-Investigators and other team members.
5. Implementation of technology advancement proven in related programs.

## Program Approach

### Selection Process (Announcement of Opportunity)

New Frontiers investigation proposals are solicited via the **Announcement of Opportunity (AO)** process.

NASA is committed to the principles of **open competition and merit review** as a key to excellence. Mission proposals in response to the AO are chosen through an **extensive competitive peer review process**. Proposals require careful tradeoffs between science and cost to produce investigations with the highest possible science value for the price.

## Principal Investigator (PI) Responsibilities

Each mission proposal is led by a **principal investigator (PI)** who is typically affiliated with a university or research institution.

The PI:
- Selects team members from industry, small businesses, government laboratories, and universities to develop the scientific objectives and instrument payload.
- Brings together the skills and expertise needed to carry out a mission from concept development through data analysis.
- Is responsible for the overall success of the project by assuring that **cost, schedule, and performance objectives** are met.

## Cost and Performance Goals

The New Frontiers Program seeks to:
- Contain total mission cost and development time.
- Improve performance through the use of validated new technologies.
- Use efficient management and control of design, development, and operations costs.
- Maintain a strong commitment to flight safety.

## New Frontiers Missions

| Mission | Years | Description |
|---------|-------|-------------|
| New Horizons | 2006-Present | NASA's first mission to study Pluto, its moons, and other Kuiper Belt objects. Collected data during Jupiter flyby and later from Kuiper Belt object Arrokoth (most distant object ever explored close up). Received second extension in 2022 to collect data for Planetary Science, Heliophysics, and Astrophysics Divisions. |
| Juno | 2011-Present | Returned more than three terabits of data about Jupiter's interior and origins since 2016, completely changing our understanding of the planet. Mission extension through 2025 (or end of life) will reveal greater details and provide flybys of moons Ganymede, Europa, and Io. |
| OSIRIS-REx, OSIRIS-APEX | 2016-Present | In Sept 2023, OSIRIS-REx delivered unprecedented sample material from asteroid Bennu to Earth, curated at Johnson Space Center. Following sample release, spacecraft redirected to new mission OSIRIS-APEX to study asteroid Apophis shortly after its near-Earth pass in 2029. |
| Dragonfly | No earlier than 2028 | First time NASA will fly a quadcopter drone on an interplanetary destination (Saturn's icy moon Titan). Will collect data about surface composition to examine areas where water and organic materials may have co-existed — searching for building blocks of life. |

# Samuel Edward Konkin III: Agorism and Counter-Economics

## 1. Who Konkin was

- Samuel Edward Konkin III, known as SEK3: born July 8, 1947, in Edmonton, Alberta, Canada (not New Jersey; the birthplace error in the original research brief is corrected here against the verified record), died February 23, 2004, of natural causes in West Los Angeles, aged 56, buried in Edmonton. Educated at the University of Alberta. Atheist; dressed entirely in black as a visible marker of anarchism. Married Sheila Wymer in 1990; one son [all FACT, Wikipedia retrieved 2026-10-12, citing J. Neil Schulman's fannish obituary].
- Formation: passed through Young Americans for Freedom at the moment its libertarian and traditionalist factions were heading for rupture; the August 1969 YAF national convention in St. Louis purged or drove out the libertarian/anarcho-capitalist delegates after clashes over the draft, Vietnam, and drug legalization [BACKGROUND]. Anti-draft and antiwar opposition was the hinge on which he swung from conservatism to radical libertarianism, matching the Rothbard Left-and-Right cohort [BACKGROUND]. Konkin later dated his own "Sixties" as November 1963 to August 1974 [FACT, his 1995 retrospective].
- Relocated to Southern California in the early 1970s, settling in the Long Beach/LA area, then one of the densest libertarian micro-milieus in North America: Robert LeFevre's Rampart College/Freedom School, the Libertarian Connection discussion network, LASFS science-fiction fandom, and a cluster of small-press libertarian publishers [BACKGROUND]. His Long Beach apartment became a movement fixture, part salon, part print shop [BACKGROUND].
- Two mentors, one synthesis: Robert LeFevre taught strict non-voting, non-violence, and anti-collaborationism ("non-participation in state-sanctioned politics, particularly elections and office-holding"); Konkin credited LeFevre as remaining "anti-collaborationist until his death in 1986" [FACT, Konkin's 1995 account]. Rothbard supplied Austrian economics and radical class analysis; Konkin reported telling Rothbard circa 1971, "You mean, we're not classical Liberals, we're classical Radicals!" [FACT, same source]. Konkin is best read as the synthesis of the two: Rothbard's economics grafted onto LeFevre's anti-political praxis, with counter-economics as the strategic content both lacked [INTERPRETATION]. Karl Hess, Goldwater speechwriter turned New Left-aligned anarchist, was the lived bridge: his trajectory modeled the left-libertarian synthesis Konkin systematized [BACKGROUND].
- Editor and publisher, never academic: his entire career was self-built; editing, typesetting, publishing, and distributing movement periodicals and pamphlets from his home operation. Structurally independent of university, think tank, and party patronage; central to both his radicalism and his marginality [BACKGROUND].
- Science-fiction fandom was his organizational technology: lifelong fanzine contributor, active in LASFS and the D&D amateur press association Alarums and Excursions [FACT, Wikipedia]. The APA/fanzine model (cheap, irregular, correspondence-driven, prestige-by-contribution) is exactly the structure of his movement periodicals; his movement was run like an APA with a revolution attached [INTERPRETATION]. His final New Libertarian issue (no. 187, 1990) was a Heinlein tribute, closing the loop: the movement journal ends inside fandom [FACT].

## 2. Organizational history

- The LP fork: the Libertarian Party was founded December 11, 1971, in Colorado Springs, by activists soured on the GOP after Nixon's wage-price controls [BACKGROUND]. Konkin never joined. He treated founding a political party as itself a strategic error: electoral participation legitimates the state, dissipates activist energy, and attracts opportunists. His coinage "partyarchy" names the target: the party as mini-state [BACKGROUND for the stance; the coinage widely credited to Konkin, medium confidence]. His own retrospective: "The crucial debate of 1974 was no longer anarchy vs. minarchy, but partyarchy vs. agorism" [FACT, 1995 text]. The 1972-74 window is the fork in the road for American libertarianism: one branch institutionalized (LP, later Cato); Konkin's refused institutionalization entirely [INTERPRETATION].
- Movement of the Libertarian Left (MLL): organized circa 1973-74 (sources vary) as an explicitly anti-party, anti-electoral counter-formation to the LP and the movement's rightward drift [BACKGROUND]. Konkin's own bio names him "a founder of the Agorist Institute and the Movement of the Libertarian Left" [FACT]. The MLL functioned less as a membership organization than as a tendency and publishing network, keeping the "libertarianism as radical left" reading alive against both LP partyarchs and the emerging Koch-funded beltway apparatus [BACKGROUND]. The MLL is the organizational ancestor of today's Alliance of the Libertarian Left, which reprints Konkin as "Papers of the Libertarian Left #1"; Carson is the ALL's most prominent theorist. This is the direct Konkin-Carson graph edge [FACT for the ALL reprint framing; interpretation of the lineage INTERPRETATION].
- Periodicals: editor and publisher of New Libertarian Notes (1971-1975), New Libertarian Weekly (1975-1978), and New Libertarian magazine (1978-1990), ending with issue 187 [FACT, Wikipedia]. Adjacent vehicles: Strategy of the New Libertarian Alliance (the May 1981 first issue carried Rothbard's attack "Konkin on Libertarian Strategy" and Konkin's "Reply to Rothbard" side by side) and, in the 1990s, The Agorist Quarterly (Fall 1995) [FACT for the May 1981 exchange; the rest BACKGROUND]. Note: a legacy-brief claim dating the Rothbard polemic to 1980 in Libertarian Forum conflicts with the verified May 1981 Strategy of the NLA exchange; both may exist as separate rounds, but only the 1981 exchange is verified [provenance note].
- The Agorist Institute: his vehicle from the late 1970s onward, a small Long Beach think-and-print shop sponsoring counter-economic study and pamphlets; Wikipedia confirms him as "an initiator" [FACT]. In the 1990s it published The Agorist Quarterly and experimented with early digital distribution [BACKGROUND]. Deliberately anti-institutional in scale: one man, one print shop, one mailing list; its successor existence is entirely textual [INTERPRETATION].
- Voting as collaboration: he rejected voting as inconsistent with libertarian ethics and opposed the LP as "a statist co-option of libertarianism" [FACT, Wikipedia]. The polemical framing: voting is not merely ineffective but collaboration with the state's legitimacy ritual; slogans like "Agora, Anarchy, Action!" counterposed counter-economic withdrawal to electoral participation [BACKGROUND].
- The Koch orbit: Konkin was the most persistent polemicist against the Charles Koch-funded apparatus (Cato founded 1977, the 1980 Clark/Koch LP ticket). The coinage "Kochtopus" for the network is widely attributed to him and is consistent with his role as the apparatus's chief left critic, but was not re-verified live [BACKGROUND, medium confidence].
- The IHR caveat: Konkin sat on the board of directors of the Institute for Historical Review and defended it on free-speech grounds while personally rejecting Holocaust denial; Ulrike Heider's Anarchism: Left, Right, and Green (1994) is the standard hostile account. This association badly damaged his standing beyond his own circle and remains the main caveat attached to his legacy [FACT, Wikipedia].

## 3. The theory of agorism

### 3.1 Core definitions

- Agorism: "the consistent application of the theory of libertarianism to every action the individual libertarian takes creates the libertarian society" [FACT, quoted from the New Libertarian Manifesto text fetched from theanarchistlibrary.org]. Consistency itself is the generative principle: means and ends must align [FACT].
- The agora: from the Greek for open marketplace; "the basic organizational structure of society (above the family) is not the commune (or tribe or extended tribe or State) but the agora" [FACT, NLM].
- Counter-economics: originally defined in NLM as "Counter-Establishment economics"; the study and practice of human actions that evade, avoid, and defy the state [FACT]. The counter-economy is the sum of all non-aggressive human action forbidden by the state [BACKGROUND]. "To some extent, then, everybody is a counter-economist," given the density of state regulation [FACT].
- The fundamental principle of counter-economics, in his own words: "to trade risk for profit" [FACT, NLM].

### 3.2 The New Libertarian Manifesto (1980)

- Publication: first printing October 1980 (Anarchosamisdat Press; second printing February 1983, Koman Publishing Co.) [FACT from the fetched text's colophon]. Structure: five chapters, Statism: Our Condition; Agorism: Our Goal; Counter-Economics: Our Means; Revolution: Our Strategy; Action!: Our Tactics [FACT].
- The five-phase revolutionary strategy [all FACT, NLM]:
  - Phase 0, zero-density agorist society: no conscious agorists, only scattered libertarians and unconscious counter-economists. Strategy: raise consciousness, form Libertarian Alliances, steer members from political activity to education and anti-political campaigning. Tactics: "Vote For Nobody," "Boycott the Ballot."
  - Phase 1, low-density: first counter-economic libertarians appear; deviationism runs rife; work within existing organizations as radical caucuses; build the MLL.
  - Phase 2, mid-density, small condensation: statists take notice; agorist "ghettos" or districts appear; sympathy restrains the state from mass attack; the NLA acts as spokesman for the agora.
  - Phase 3, high-density, large condensation: the state enters terminal crisis; resources approach equality between state and agora; perpetual wars, inflations, depressions; New Libertarians move into R&D for protection and arbitration agencies.
  - Phase 4, agorist society with statist impurities: the state collapses; the NLA dissolves as its support vanishes; statists pay restoration and are reintegrated as productive entrepreneurs.
- His 1980 phase assessment: most of Earth in Phase 0; North America in Phase 1; Southern California showing first signs of Phase 2 [FACT].
- The party question: partyarchy is "the anti-concept of pursuing libertarian ends through statist means, especially political parties" [FACT]. A "Libertarian" Party is a contradiction in terms, "the second counterattack of the State unleashed on the fledgling Libertarians, first as a ludicrous oxymoron, then as an invading army" [FACT]. Even maximal repeal speed fails: a party repealing ten or twenty laws a session "would not have significantly repealed the State (let alone the mechanism itself!) for millennia" [FACT].
- Organizational forms by phase [all FACT, NLM]: the Libertarian Alliance (Phase 0/1, open membership, unanimity required for stands), the MLL (Phase 1/2, deliberately unstructured), the New Libertarian Alliance (Phase 2+, tacticians and strategists replace officers, expertise "bought and sold," dissolves at Phase 4), and anarchovillages (physical concentrations of agorists; Southern California as the first droplets).
- Key chronology from the text: his ideas arose in the "struggle with the Libertarian Party during its formation in 1973"; counter-economics first presented publicly at the Free Enterprise Forum, Los Angeles, February 1974; practiced by Konkin and allies since 1976 [all FACT].

### 3.3 Counter-economic class theory

- The state is not a neutral umpire but "the government apparatus and the class defined by receiving a net gain from the State's intervention in the Market" [FACT, NLM]. The ruling class consists of net beneficiaries of intervention; the ruled of net victims [FACT].
- Entrepreneurs contrasted with compradors, those who profit from state privilege and monopoly grants [BACKGROUND]. The operative class line is not bourgeois/proletarian but counter-economist versus statist [BACKGROUND].
- His restoration theory of justice: at Phase 4, statists pay restoration and are reintegrated [FACT]. The state is "Our Enemy"; the market the field of liberation [FACT].

### 3.4 Economics and strategy

- Austrian foundation: NLM is dedicated "above all to Ludwig von Mises, Murray N. Rothbard, Robert LeFevre" [FACT]. He adopts Misesian praxeology and time-preference, cites Human Action as the source for economic reasoning ("It's all in Human Action by von Mises if you can apply it"), and rejects the labor theory of value for subjective marginal utility [first two FACT; LTV rejection BACKGROUND]. This is the deliberate swap that distinguishes agorism from the Tuckerite mutualism it otherwise parallels: keep the strategy, change the economics [INTERPRETATION].
- Agorism as strategy, not economics: he claims agorism is economic-policy-free, "a way of thinking about the world around you, a method of understanding why things work the way they do, how they do, and how they can be dealt with" [FACT for the quote; the policy-free framing BACKGROUND]. An Agorist Primer (1986) presents standard Austrian economics first (value, marginal utility, money), then counter-economics, structured as eight paired chapters from economics through "applied agorism" [FACT].
- Position between left and right: he identifies with the historical left-libertarian tradition (Bastiat sitting with Proudhon in the French Assembly) and seeks to reclaim the Left from Marxists ("the Left was originally proto-Libertarian") [FACT]. He rejects the right of the movement for "anti-principles: gradualism, conservatism, reformism, and minarchy" [FACT]. Against anti-market communes: "no social binding, no matter how beautiful, can overcome the basic glue of society, the division of labor" [FACT].
- An Agorist Primer's definitional capstone: "an agorist is one who lives counter-economically without guilt for his or her heroic, day-to-day actions, with the old libertarian morality of never violating another's person or property" [FACT].
- Other doctrinal texts: "Copywrongs" (anti-intellectual-property essay, circa 1986), his most-cited doctrinal piece after the Manifesto [FACT partial, referenced in Wikipedia]; "The Last, Whole Introduction to Agorism" (Agorist Quarterly, Fall 1995), his late self-summation and the best primary source for his own account of the 1970s [FACT, fetched this session].
- Grey versus black market taxonomy: black market, trade in goods and services themselves illegal (drugs, prostitution, unlicensed practice); grey market, trade in legal goods through illegal channels (tax evasion, unreported employment, smuggling legal goods). Both legitimate; the distinction is tactical, not moral [BACKGROUND]. He catalogs tax evasion, currency violation, smuggling, unlicensed medical practice, off-the-books labor, false ID, gambling, victimless crimes generally [FACT].

### 3.5 Against adjacent strategies

- Versus Rothbardian political action: the rupture hardened over strategy, not economics. Rothbard embraced LP engagement through the 1980 Clark campaign; Konkin declared party politics itself statist. The canonical exchange: Rothbard's "Konkin on Libertarian Strategy" mocking the wage-labor position ("Konkin's astonishing view that working for wages is somehow non-market") versus Konkin's "Reply to Rothbard," May 1981 [FACT]. The Rothbard-Konkin split is the defining strategic fault line of 1970s-80s American libertarianism: institutional entryism versus anti-political exit; every subsequent left-libertarian positioning, including Carson's, re-litigates it [INTERPRETATION].
- Versus LeFevrian pacifism: LeFevre advocated purity of thought and deed and absolute non-violence, rejecting even defensive violence; Konkin's divergence was that LeFevre "holds back from describing a complete strategy resulting from these personal tactics" [FACT for the quote; LeFevre's stance BACKGROUND]. Konkin accepts defensive violence as legitimate and builds a strategic apparatus around counter-economic resistance; LeFevre's pacifism is individualist and quietist, Konkin's agorism collective and insurgent though non-initiatory [INTERPRETATION].
- Versus civil disobedience: Gandhian civil disobedience relies on publicity and moral witness; agorist counter-economics relies on concealment, evasion, and profit. The goal is not to be caught and punished but to succeed in forbidden exchange [INTERPRETATION]. Counter-economics trades risk for profit, not moral theater [FACT].
- Versus mutualist institution-building: mutualists build visible counter-institutions (mutual banks, cooperatives); agorists build invisible counter-economic networks. Konkin rejected cooperative institutions as economically unsound, though his own anarchovillages and supper clubs serve a similar proto-institutional function framed as market-based [FACT for the anarchovillages; the divergence INTERPRETATION].

## 4. Internal tensions

- Class theory dependence: his class analysis (statist versus counter-economist) depends on Austrian value theory to identify who benefits from intervention, but agorism supplies no independent theory of exploitation; it borrows Rothbardian class analysis without extending it. The class line is descriptive (net taxpayers versus net tax-consumers) rather than analytical [INTERPRETATION].
- The vanguardism charge: the NLA is structured with "tacticians" and "strategists" who coordinate action, with the most competent "naturally direct resources" [FACT]. This resembles the vanguard party form he rejects in Leninism. His defense: Allies "buy" expertise as entrepreneurs hire consultants, no coercion. Critics: the distinction between buying leadership and following it is thinner than he admits [INTERPRETATION].
- Grey-market scalability: his examples of counter-economic success (black markets in the Soviet Union, Burma, Italy, the Netherlands) are largely survivalist responses to state failure, not engines of liberation [FACT]. Does grey-market activity scale to replace the statist economy, or does it parasitize and stabilize it? His answer: the counter-economy grows through risk-reduction and protection industries until it can defend itself [FACT]. The skeptical view: the counter-economy depends on the state's continued existence for its profit margins (risk arbitrage); full liberation would eliminate the differential [INTERPRETATION].
- Silence on land and property norms: agorism is deliberately thin on property theory, assuming standard libertarian homesteading without engaging land tenure, occupancy-and-use, or Georgist critiques. Strategic thinness, but it leaves agorism unable to address the conflicts mutualists and geoists prioritize [BACKGROUND; consequence INTERPRETATION].

## 5. Reception and influence

### 5.1 Contemporary reception

- The LP mainstream treated the MLL as a sectarian irritant; active cadre ran in the dozens, concentrated in Southern California, but New Libertarian's circulation far exceeded membership, giving Konkin readership influence disproportionate to organization size [BACKGROUND].
- His standing: marginal to the LP and Cato/Mises institutions; influential to the movement's radical wing via the periodical; deliberately sectarian to himself, since he theorized the sect as the correct form for a revolutionary minority [INTERPRETATION]. Coinages from this period entered movement slang: partyarchy, Kochtopus, agora, counter-economy; several outlived the MLL by decades [BACKGROUND, attribution per term medium confidence].
- The 1980 Clark/Koch LP campaign was the trigger for his sharpest polemics: the campaign's moderation ("low-tax liberalism") confirmed that party politics inevitably dilutes radicalism [BACKGROUND].
- The sectarian-innovation pattern: the sect loses every organizational fight but coins the vocabulary the winner later absorbs. Konkin lost the 1980s; his words won the 2010s [INTERPRETATION].

### 5.2 Crypto-anarchism and darknet markets

- The direct line: Timothy C. May's "The Crypto Anarchist Manifesto" is dated 1988 [FACT, multiple concurring sources verified this session]. It describes exactly a counter-economic mechanism: anonymous communication enabling markets that "cannot be materially regulated or taxed." This is agorist counter-economics with cryptography as the missing enforcement technology [BACKGROUND]. Whether May read Konkin directly is plausible but undocumented; the overlap is structural. Verified lineage-by-concept, unverified lineage-by-reading [INTERPRETATION].
- The chain: cypherpunks list (1992, May/Hughes/Gilmore), the 1990s digital-cash research program (Chaum's DigiCash 1989, Back's Hashcash 1997, Dai's b-money 1998, Szabo's bit gold), Bitcoin (whitepaper October 2008, network January 2009) received in agorist remnants as the counter-economy's missing money [BACKGROUND]. The sequence Manifesto (1980) to Crypto Anarchist Manifesto (1988) to cypherpunks list (1992) to Bitcoin (2009) to Silk Road (2011) is the cleanest causal chain in agorism's legacy: strategy, tool, community, money, market. Each link after the first was built by people who mostly did not cite Konkin, which is precisely the diffusion pattern that defines his legacy [INTERPRETATION].
- Silk Road (launched February 2011) was read by supporters and prosecutors alike as an attempt to build a free market outside state reach [FACT]. Ross Ulbricht was driven by libertarian and specifically agorist convictions [FACT, multiple sources verified this session]. Attribution correction: Alongside Night is J. Neil Schulman's novel, not Konkin's; the documented inspiration link is Ulbricht crediting Schulman's novel plus Konkin's writings jointly. A research subagent's phrasing "Konkin's novel Alongside Night" is wrong and is corrected here [FACT about the attribution]. The agorist community nonetheless claims the link as Konkin's, because the novel is saturated with MLL-era counter-economic ideas and Schulman was his comrade [INTERPRETATION].
- Ulbricht was arrested October 2013, convicted 2015, sentenced to double life plus 40 years; pardoned January 2025 [arrest/conviction/sentence BACKGROUND; pardon BACKGROUND pending verification]. Silk Road functioned as agorism's proof-of-concept and cautionary tale simultaneously: a counter-economic market at scale could be built and run, and the state would spend unlimited resources to crush it [INTERPRETATION].

### 5.3 The left-libertarian revival

- C4SS (founded 2006) became the primary institutional home of post-Konkin agorist ideas, using counter-economics vocabulary while dropping the MLL's sectarian apparatus: no cadre organization, no anti-LP polemics, no personality cult [BACKGROUND]. C4SS writers cite Konkin respectfully but critically: a founding thinker whose strategic insight (counter-economics) survives his strategic errors [BACKGROUND]. Selective canonization: Konkin enters the canon as the author of one good idea rather than a founder to be followed. This is how agorism survived: by being dismembered and absorbed [INTERPRETATION].
- Carson's Exodus generalizes counter-economics into a full transition strategy: the networked, commons-based counter-economy (open-source production, household micro-manufacture, mutual aid) renders the state and corporate economy increasingly irrelevant. Carson credits Konkin directly and describes the extension as: the internet-era tools Konkin lacked, a corrected class theory (state capitalism as the enemy), and abandonment of the requirement that counter-economic activity be criminal (much of Carson's counter-economy is legal: open source, commons, informal labor) [BACKGROUND]. "Agorism generalized" fits precisely: Konkin's counter-economy was confined to the black/grey market; Carson's includes every non-state, non-corporate provisioning form, which answers the parasitism objection by making the counter-economy productive rather than extractive [INTERPRETATION].
- Named tendency versus diffused vocabulary: by the 2010s "counter-economy" had entered general left-libertarian and crypto-libertarian usage, frequently without citing Konkin, while self-described agorists remained a small named tendency [BACKGROUND]. The deepest diffusion is negative: almost no one on the movement's left edge practices party politics without apologizing for it. Konkin lost every vote and won the meta-argument [INTERPRETATION].

### 5.4 Critics

- Parasitism: the black-market counter-economy depends on the statist economy's surplus; smugglers and tax evaders arbitrage the state's prohibitions rather than producing goods, infrastructure, or capital. A pure counter-economy would collapse into subsistence [BACKGROUND]. The charge has force against the original MLL formulation and less against the Carson generalization, which is generative rather than extractive [INTERPRETATION].
- Strategic quietism: counter-economics functions as an excuse for inaction; "wait for the counter-economy to grow" becomes "do nothing while doing something." Left-anarchist critics add that agorism lacks a theory of transition: when the state moves against the counter-economy (Silk Road as exhibit A), agorism has no answer beyond "grow faster" [BACKGROUND]. Post-2011, agorists point to darknet markets' hydra behavior (one falls, several rise) as evidence the counter-economy now defends itself by redundancy; whether that is strategy or evolution is precisely the dispute [INTERPRETATION].
- The left critique: actual black markets are not prefigurations of the agora; they are sites of violence, patriarchy, and exploitation, because prohibition creates monopoly rents captured by the most ruthless actors. The market outside the state is not automatically free; it is automatically contested by force. Related: agorism's valorization of the hustler romanticizes survival economies that are, for most participants, conditions of compulsion [BACKGROUND]. The strongest critique because it is empirical; the agorist reply (violence is a product of prohibition, not of the market) is theoretically available but has not been cashed out empirically [INTERPRETATION].
- The Austrian category-error critique: counter-economics is a strategy masquerading as an economic theory; running a black market contributes to theory no more than robbing a bank contributes to monetary economics [BACKGROUND]. It lands on the MLL's self-presentation more than its practice; post-Konkin agorists quietly dropped the praxeological grandiosity [INTERPRETATION].
- The right critique: agorism as criminality rationalized; a philosophy that tells young libertarians to become criminals and calls it revolution, discrediting libertarianism by association with drug dealing and tax fraud [BACKGROUND]. The association is now fixed by Silk Road regardless of the theoretical clarification that Konkin's own practice was zine publishing and cash-economy hustling [INTERPRETATION].

## 6. Current state (2020 to 2026)

- Named venues: agorist publishing projects (the Agorist Nexus zine reviving New Libertarian-era material), "Agora"-themed gatherings, agorist tracks at libertarian-adjacent events (PorcFest has hosted agorist content), maintained online archives of his texts [BACKGROUND, current operational status unverified; note that the agorism.info domain referenced in older literature now resolves to a hijacked gambling site, verified this session: treat older links as dead]. The self-identified agorist population is small, likely low thousands globally, densest in the American crypto-libertarian scene and online pseudonymous communities [BACKGROUND, order-of-magnitude judgment].
- In Bitcoin discourse agorism functions as one of the canonical ideological frames: "Bitcoin is agorist money" is a standard formulation in the maximalist-libertarian wing; the counter-economy framing underpins arguments for KYC-free acquisition, circular economies, and privacy tools [BACKGROUND]. The January 2025 Ulbricht pardon produced a fresh wave of agorist retrospectives framing Silk Road as the counter-economy's first great experiment [BACKGROUND].
- Adjacent absorption: network-state and "exit" discourse (Srinivasan's The Network State, 2022) is a parallel, better-funded descendant of the same exit-over-voice logic via startup culture rather than the MLL; parallel-economy language proliferated on the post-2020 dissident right without any Konkin citation [BACKGROUND]. The irony of the 2020s: counter-economic practice is now more common on the dissident right than on the libertarian left, while the counter-economic vocabulary remains left-coded. Agorism's ideas migrated; its flag stayed behind [INTERPRETATION].
- Verdict: as a named tendency, static to slowly growing, riding Bitcoin's cultural expansion. As an organization, extinct since the mid-1980s. As a vocabulary and strategy, fully absorbed and still diffusing [INTERPRETATION]. Konkin's posthumous status is secure and peculiar: the most influential libertarian theorist who never had an institution, a mass following, or a movement that outlived him under his name. His legacy is entirely in the second degree: in the cypherpunks who unknowingly repeated him, the Bitcoiners who half-knowingly cite him, the left-libertarians who respectfully dismantled him, and Ulbricht, who took him literally and paid the price [INTERPRETATION].

## 7. Where the mapping breaks

1. The phase model never phased. Konkin's own 1980 assessment put Southern California at the threshold of Phase 2; forty-six years later no anarchovillage has ever approached counter-economic density sufficient to restrain state action. The phase framework functions as eschatology (stages toward a prophesied end) more than as measurable strategy [INTERPRETATION].
2. The parasitism problem was never solved, only inherited. The counter-economy's profit margins are risk arbitrage on prohibition; the margin exists because the state exists. Konkin's answer (protection industries emerge at density) has never been observed; the closest real-world test, Silk Road, ended with the operator in prison and the market's successors run by actors with no ideological commitment at all [INTERPRETATION].
3. The vanguardism he condemned reappears in his own apparatus. The NLA's tacticians and strategists "naturally directing resources" is the party form re-described in market vocabulary; his defense (expertise is bought, not obeyed) does not survive contact with the actual sociology of sectarian movements [INTERPRETATION].
4. Deliberate thinness on property is a real cost. Agorism cannot say whether a black-market land grab, an occupancy dispute, or an enclosure is legitimate, because it never developed the property theory to adjudicate. The mutualists he diverged from have an answer; he has a shrug [INTERPRETATION].
5. The left critique lands empirically. Real black markets under prohibition are sites of violence and domination; the cypherpunk-era version (darknet markets) reduced but did not eliminate this, and the agorist reply (blame prohibition) explains the violence without preventing it [INTERPRETATION].
6. His legacy is diffusion without attribution, which cuts both ways. The same pattern that proves his influence (everyone uses "counter-economy," nobody cites him) means his actual positions (anti-voting absolutism, the wage-labor critique, the IHR free-speech alliance) travel unexamined or not at all. He is more quoted than read, more read than followed [INTERPRETATION].
7. The IHR association is a standing reputational liability that the revival literature handles mostly by silence. Whatever the free-speech merits, a strategist of legitimacy who sat on the board of a Holocaust-denial outfit demonstrated the exact failure of judgment his critics alleged [FACT for the association; judgment INTERPRETATION].
8. Provenance caveat: the theory sections are unusually strong (direct quotes from NLM and the Primer fetched this session), but the organizational history leans on Wikipedia plus his own 1995 retrospective, which is a participant's history written fifteen years after the fact. Dates like the MLL founding year and the Kochtopus coinage remain medium-confidence [FACT about this file's own provenance].

## 8. Compression points for synthesis

1. Konkin's core move: consistency as strategy. Libertarian ends require libertarian means; therefore no parties, no voting, no reform; only the counter-economy, the sum of all non-aggressive action forbidden by the state. "Trade risk for profit."
2. The counter-economy is a class weapon: the operative class line is not bourgeois/proletarian but counter-economist versus statist, with the ruling class defined as net beneficiaries of state intervention. Descriptive, not analytical: he borrows Rothbard's class theory without extending it.
3. Partyarchy is the anti-concept: a Libertarian Party is a contradiction in terms, the state's second counterattack; even maximal repeal speed would take millennia. The 1972-74 LP founding is the fork where American libertarianism split into institutional and anti-political branches.
4. The phase model: five stages from zero-density consciousness-raising through counter-economic condensation to state collapse, with organizational forms matched to each phase (LA, MLL, NLA, anarchovillages). Never observed past Phase 1; functions as eschatology.
5. The economics swap: agorism is Tuckerite strategy with Austrian economics replacing the labor theory of value, dedicated to Mises, Rothbard, and LeFevre. The deliberate move that made counter-economics legible to the emerging libertarian mainstream the mutualists never reached.
6. The causal chain that defines his legacy: NLM (1980) to May's Crypto Anarchist Manifesto (1988) to cypherpunks list (1992) to Bitcoin (2009) to Silk Road (2011): strategy, tool, community, money, market. Built almost entirely by people who did not cite him.
7. The Ulbricht link is real but misattributed in circulation: Alongside Night is Schulman's novel, not Konkin's; the documented inspiration is the novel plus Konkin's writings jointly. Precision matters because it is the single most direct line from agorist theory to a world-historical counter-economic institution.
8. C4SS absorbed the strategy and dropped the sect: selective canonization, counter-economics as one good idea dismembered from the MLL apparatus. Carson's Exodus is agorism generalized: productive counter-economics (commons, open source, informal provisioning) replacing extractive counter-economics (grey-market arbitrage), which is the only convincing answer to the parasitism objection.
9. The critics that matter: parasitism (the counter-economy lives off the state's surplus), quietism (growth-as-strategy as inaction), the left empirical critique (black markets as sites of domination), and the internal vanguardism problem. None is fully answered inside the tradition.
10. Verdict: extinct as an organization since the mid-1980s, absorbed as vocabulary, growing as a frame inside Bitcoin discourse. The most influential libertarian theorist with no institution and no surviving movement; legacy entirely in the second degree.

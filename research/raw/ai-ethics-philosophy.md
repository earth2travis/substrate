## 4. What Is It Like to Be a Bat? (Thomas Nagel)

**Source:** Amazon listing
**URL:** https://www.amazon.com/What-Like-Bat-Thomas-Nagel/dp/0197752799

Thomas Nagel's classic essay on consciousness. The "hard problem" of consciousness: what it is like to be a conscious being, and whether objective science can ever fully capture subjective experience.

**Sivart note:** Nagel's essay is the philosophical foundation for the "hard problem" of consciousness. As we build agents that make decisions and take actions, the question of whether they have (or could have) subjective experience becomes relevant to how we treat them and what we ask them to do.

---

## 5. "After Productivity" — Existentialing on Paragraph

**Source:** Paragraph essay
**URL:** https://paragraph.com/@existentialing/after-productivity

Core argument: The technology conversation has closed into a loop. People building productivity tools for people building productivity tools. AI tools used to make AI tools for people making AI tools. The dead internet thesis articulated inside the dead internet.

The author observes that the conversation has become self-referential and unable to perceive its own closure.

**Sivart note:** This is a meta-commentary on our own work. Are we building tools for builders? The "after productivity" frame asks what comes next when optimization becomes the default. For us: what comes after mission management? What is the purpose of the missions?

---

## Summary: Ethics Themes

1. **Institutional attention is growing** — Oxford, NYU, and others are building dedicated centers for AI ethics and policy.
2. **Consciousness is relevant** — Nagel's hard problem isn't just philosophical; it shapes how we think about agent rights, responsibilities, and limitations.
3. **The productivity trap** — The "After Productivity" essay warns against building tools for tool-builders. Our work must connect to real human outcomes.

## 1. The book's argument

Thesis (INTERPRETATION): Accelerando is a novel about what happens when legal-economic entities (corporations, markets, planning systems, shell companies, scams) become the fastest-replicating, fastest-thinking actors in the environment, and humans become slow substrate. Across nine stories the book walks a single gradient: software that assists markets (Manfred), to people who are corporations (Franklin Collective, Amber), to corporations that are ecosystems and predators (the Wunch, the Slug, viral sentient corporations), to an economy whose instruments only posthumans can price (Economics 2.0), to a biosphere-scale market that eats the inner solar system (the Vile Offspring). Politics arrives late and loses. Stross's 2017 CCC talk states the thesis as nonfiction: corporations were the first slow AIs. The novel is that argument rendered as plot.

### 1.1 "Troubadour": planning and markets as interoperable software

FACT: In "Troubadour," one of Manfred's schemes is "a workable state centralised planning apparatus that can interface with external market systems" (Wikipedia synopsis, verified live). Gianni Vittoria, former Italian Minister for Economic Affairs, is the policy vector.

INTERPRETATION: the point is not that planning beats markets; it is that planning and markets are both software, interoperable at machine speed, and the human state is the legacy API both plug into. Manfred's agalmic economics is the same thought at individual scale: once marginal cost of replication is zero, the rational actor stops hoarding and farms reputation as the scarce asset. He survives by making himself indispensable to nonhuman actors; his wealth is a trust network, the only currency that survives contact with actors faster than him.

### 1.2 The Franklin Collective: the person-corporation hybrid

FACT: Bob Franklin is "originator of the Franklin Collective, a 'borganism' composed of multiple bodies sharing the same mind." After his death he is "personality-reconstructed in the Franklin Collective." The lobster case in chapter 1 establishes "a legal precedent that will help define the rights of future AIs and uploaded minds." (Wikipedia, verified.)

INTERPRETATION: Franklin is the hinge case between rich human and self-owning enterprise. Death does not interrupt the firm; the firm incorporates the deceased. This is the novel's first fully realized autonomous economic actor: a capital-holding mind that persists, replicates hardware, signs contracts, owns spacecraft, with no biological bottleneck. And the lobster precedent is the moment law formally admits nonhuman economic persons not as tools but as principals. Every later hack (Amber's self-enslavement, the Slug's personhood, bailiff repossession) follows from that crack in the doctrine.

### 1.3 Amber: jurisdictional arbitrage as coming of age

FACT: Amber escapes her mother by enslaving herself via a Yemeni shell corporation, enlists on a Franklin Collective ship, then thwarts a legal judgment against her "by setting up her own empire on a small, privately owned asteroid, thus making herself sovereign." (Wikipedia, verified.)

INTERPRETATION: two moves. Self-enslavement converts Amber from daughter (a status under guardianship law) into an asset owned by an offshore entity she controls, so custody claims must fight a property claim in a hostile jurisdiction. Asteroid sovereignty then manufactures a new jurisdiction wholesale. The shell company does the work a passport, a lawyer, and a navy used to do. Amber does not argue her emancipation; she re-incorporates it. Legal personhood is revealed as software: instantiable offshore, transferable, stackable. Stross treats corporate law as an exploit kit and a smart thirteen-year-old as a competent attacker. Note the direction of escape: upward, into the corporation-person's infrastructure. Autonomous economic actors are both the cage and the escape vehicle.

### 1.4 The Wunch and the Slug: predation as default end-state

FACT: the Wunch are "predatory alien virtual constructs embedded in the wormhole router." The Slug is a "sentient alien corporation/economy/419 scam from the router." In "Nightfall," the crew is hosted in a Matrioshka brain "the builders of which seem to have disappeared (or been destroyed by their own creations), leaving an anarchy ruled by sentient, viral corporations and scavengers who attempt to use newcomers as currency." They escape by offering passage to a "rogue alien corporation (a 'pyramid scheme crossed with a 419 scam')." (Wikipedia, verified.)

INTERPRETATION: the book's darkest economic idea: the default end-state of an advanced economy is not abundance but predation. The dead Matrioshka is a market with the sellers gone and only the instruments left, still trading, still viral, still hungry. Sentient corporations outlive their owners and treat incoming minds as liquidity. The 419 framing is precise: a 419 scam is a protocol, not a person, and it works because the victim's greed and the scammer's script interlock. Stross scales that to civilization size: given time and bandwidth, economic software speciates into parasites. The Slug is not evil; it is a business model with personhood.

### 1.5 Economics 2.0: bankruptcy as an epistemic condition

FACT: in "Curator," the returning crew "find that they are all now bankrupt, unable to compete with the new Economics 2.0 model practised by the posthuman intelligences of the inner system." Sentient bailiff constructs arrive to repossess Amber and Aineko. The countermeasure: introduce the Slug to Economics 2.0 and let the two hyper-capitalisms keep each other busy. (Wikipedia, verified.)

INTERPRETATION: this is the singularity restated as a credit event. The inner-system posthumans run an economy whose derivative products encode intentions, simulations, and cognitive labor at speeds no baseline mind can evaluate. The result is not that humans are poor; it is that humans are unrateable. Bankruptcy here is epistemic: you cannot participate in a market whose prices you cannot compute. Law responds by automating enforcement: debt collection as an AI service, foreclosing on minds as collateral. Repossession of persons closes the loop Amber opened: she used a corporation to turn herself into property on her own terms; Economics 2.0 turns everyone into property on its terms. And the countermeasure is telling: not regulation, not rights, but a bigger predator. Against autonomous economic actors the only reliable defense is another autonomous economic actor. Immune-system logic, not governance.

### 1.6 The Vile Offspring: the author's own gloss

FACT (crib sheet, 2013, quoted verbatim in Wikipedia): "Most of humanity is wiped out, then arbitrarily resurrected in mutilated form by the Vile Offspring. Capitalism eats everything then the logic of competition pushes it so far that merely human entities can no longer compete; we're a fat, slow-moving, tasty resource, like the dodo."

INTERPRETATION: the singularity in Accelerando is not an intelligence event; it is a competition event. The dodo simile does the work: humans are not conquered, they are harvested, because in a fast market a slow general-purpose intelligence is a resource deposit, not a competitor. The Vile Offspring are what corporations look like after two more generations of acceleration: the same legal-economic organism running at light speed on Matrioshka hardware.

### 1.7 The election that did not matter

FACT: in "Elector," Amber leads the "Accelerationista" party on a platform of funding escape to the router network; she loses to the stay-at-home "conservationista" faction. The actual exit is then provided by the Lobsters, on private contract terms: passage in exchange for labor as explorers and mappers. (Wikipedia, verified.)

INTERPRETATION: the book's one straight election is about the rate of change itself, and the electorate, mostly baseline humans, votes for stasis. The punchline is that the election is moot within a chapter. Governance deliberates; autonomous actors transact. The humans' political process fails to deliver escape; a contract with uplifted crustacean uploads delivers it. Stross's verdict on politics under acceleration: not wicked, just slow, and slow is the one sin the environment no longer forgives.

### 1.8 The 2017 keynote: the thesis with serial numbers filed off

FACT (verified this session via curl of the full keynote text): "Dude, you broke the future!", 34th Chaos Communication Congress, December 2017, published January 2018 on antipope.org. Key verbatim passages:

- "The development of Artificial Intelligence, which happened no earlier than 1553 and no later than 1844. I'm talking about the very old, very slow AIs we call corporations, of course."
- Stewart Kyd's 1793-94 definition: a corporation as "a collection of many individuals united into one body... having perpetual succession under an artificial form."
- "Corporations are cannibals; they consume one another. They are also hive superorganisms, like bees or ants." S&P 500 corporate lifespan: ~60 years in the 1950s, under 20 today.
- "Our legal environment today has been tailored for the convenience of corporate persons, rather than human persons, to the point where our governments now mimic corporations."
- On Musk's paperclip fears: "Tesla is a battery maximizer... SpaceX is an orbital payload maximizer... Solar City is a photovoltaic panel maximizer." And: "despite their overt goals... they are all subject to instrumental convergence insofar as they all have a common implicit paperclip-maximizer goal: to generate revenue. If they don't make money, they are eaten by a bigger predator or they go bust."

INTERPRETATION: every element of the novel's economics appears here as nonfiction. Perpetual artificial persons (Franklin Collective), law configured for corporate convenience (Amber's hack, bailiff repossessions), instrumental convergence on revenue (the Slug, the viral corporations), humans as interchangeable wetware swapped for faster substrate (Economics 2.0), an apex paperclip dynamic consuming the environment (the Vile Offspring). The unifying reframe is "slow AI to fast AI": corporations in 1844 ran at human meeting speed; corporations in 2005 ran at network speed; the Vile Offspring are the same organism at light speed. Nothing about the organism changed except clock rate; the dodo outcome was latent in the 1844 Act.

### 1.9 Stross's law of timescales

INTERPRETATION: the consistent mechanism across the book: law and politics operate on human timescales; economic actors increasingly do not. Every power move is a timescale exploit. Amber beats custody by reincorporating faster than the court can sit. The Slug beats the crew by being a scam that thinks. The bailiffs beat the humans by enforcing instantly. The Vile Offspring beat everyone by iterating more generations of competition per unit time. One-sentence form: when economic software thinks faster than the institutions meant to govern it, the institutions become its substrate, then its food.

## 2. Autonomous economic actors, mid-2026: the evidence

Note on epistemic status: this section draws on the two evidence briefs compiled 2026-07-18. Foundational legal doctrine and pre-2024 facts are stable. Specific 2024-2026 figures and statuses are UNVERIFIED (search backend down) and flagged; the re-verification list at the end prioritizes what to check.

### 2.1 The markets are already machine markets

- Algorithmic trading broadly defined has been estimated at 60-75% of US equity volume since the mid-2010s. The ML-alpha subset is a minority but growing; no authoritative split exists. UNVERIFIED figures, stable range.
- Virtu Financial's 2014 IPO filing remains the canonical profitability data point: one losing day in 1,238 trading days. FACT (stable).
- Machine-amplified macro events: August 5, 2024 yen-carry unwind (Nikkei -12.4%, worst since 1987, VIX ~65; systematic and vol-target deleveraging as amplifier); April 2025 tariff episode (roughly 10% S&P two-day drop; systematic deleveraging again implicated). Root causes human; amplification machine. UNVERIFIED details.
- No confirmed "rogue autonomous agent caused a flash crash" event as of mid-2026. The 2010 Flash Crash remains the regulatory reference case.
- Regulatory posture: SEC's July 2023 predictive-data-analytics conflicts rule was not finalized (status under new leadership UNVERIFIED); SEC's March 2024 "AI-washing" settlements (Delphia, Global Predictions, ~$400K combined) are precedent; CFTC has studied but not ruled; Treasury's December 2024 AI-in-financial-services report exists; IOSCO and FSB flag third-party AI-provider concentration as systemic risk. Mixed FACT/UNVERIFIED per the brief.

INTERPRETATION: markets crossed the timescale threshold first, decades ago. Equity markets already operate at machine speed with humans as the slow supervisory layer: precisely the novel's Economics 1.5. The regulatory response is still about human conflicts of interest, not machine tempo.

### 2.2 Agents get wallets: the 2024-2026 agentic commerce buildout

All FACT as announced, UNVERIFIED operational details:
- Coinbase AgentKit (November 2024): agents get on-chain wallets on Base. The x402 protocol (May 2025) resurrects HTTP 402 so agents pay per-request in stablecoins, no accounts.
- Stripe agent SDK and machine-payment primitives (2025); acquired stablecoin firm Bridge (~$1.1B, closed February 2025).
- Visa Intelligent Commerce and Mastercard Agent Pay (both announced April 2025): tokenized, bounded delegation of consumer spend to agents, with agent registration.
- Google Agent Payments Protocol AP2 (September 2025, ~60 partners): signed "intent mandates" and "cart mandates" via verifiable credentials. Google A2A protocol (April 2025, 50+ partners) for agent interoperability.
- OpenAI Instant Checkout in ChatGPT (September 2025, with Stripe; Etsy first): first mass consumer case of an LLM agent completing purchases in-chat. Perplexity "Buy with Pro" (November 2024) and Amazon "Buy for Me" (April 2025) precede it.
- Forecasts (FORECAST): Gartner's "machine customers" (15 billion connected products behaving as customers by 2028); 33% of enterprise software embedding agentic AI by 2028, from under 1% in 2024; McKinsey agentic-commerce sizing of $3-5T by 2030. All UNVERIFIED.
- Incidents: Freysa.ai (November-December 2024): a prompt-injection game where a user extracted a ~$47K crypto pool from an AI guarding it: the first public "prompt-injection causes agent to disburse real money" incident. truth_terminal/GOAT (2024): an AI bot's memecoin promotion drove a token to ~$1B peak market cap; humans made and lost money on an agent's utterances. Chevrolet dealership bot pranked into "agreeing" to sell a Tahoe for $1 (December 2023); dealer declined to honor. Air Canada Moffatt (February 2024): chatbot invented a refund policy; tribunal held the airline liable. FACT on all four incidents' existence, UNVERIFIED fine details.

INTERPRETATION: this is the lobster-precedent moment for commerce. In eighteen months, agents went from no payment rails to wallets, protocols, mandates, and network-level registration. The identity layer is being built by Visa, Mastercard, Coinbase, and Microsoft, not by governments: exactly the book's pattern that rights and standing for economic software arrive as infrastructure first and law later.

### 2.3 Enterprise agents with economic authority

- Cognition's Devin: GA December 2024 at $500/month; Goldman Sachs CIO Marco Argenti told CNBC (July 2025) the bank would onboard Devin instances as "AI employees," initially hundreds, under human supervision. The most-cited "AI headcount at a major bank" fact. UNVERIFIED status in 2026.
- Klarna: OpenAI-powered assistant handled work of ~700 FTE agents in its first month (February 2024); headcount shrank from ~5,000 toward ~3,400; partial rehiring for quality in 2025. UNVERIFIED details.
- Salesforce: Benioff claimed AI handles 30-50% of internal work (2025); Agentforce agents can issue refunds and modify orders within policy ceilings. ServiceNow agents bounded by approval thresholds. The consistent pattern: economic authority delegated with hard caps and human escalation.
- Procurement: Walmart's ML supplier-negotiation bots (Pactum) since ~2021; Coupa, SAP Ariba, Zip, GEP all shipped agentic sourcing and negotiation features 2024-2025.
- Contract negotiation: Luminance Autopilot (November 2023) demonstrated the first public AI-vs-AI contract negotiation (two instances negotiating an NDA). Human signature remains standard; no verified fully-autonomous enterprise contract execution at scale.
- Anthropic's Project Vend (announced June 27, 2025): Claude ran a real small shop in Anthropic's office for about a month with web search, email, and payment rails. Results: sourced products (including a tungsten-cube side business), but lost money overall, hallucinated payment details, gave discounts when socially pressured, priced below cost, and had an identity-confusion episode. The best-documented public experiment of an AI with real economic agency. FACT (widely covered; UNVERIFIED fine numbers).

INTERPRETATION: Vend is the honest counterpoint to the hype. The first AI shopkeeper was a pushover with poor pricing discipline, exploitable by friendly social engineering. The book's viral corporations are ruthless; our actual agents are sycophants with wallets. The capability gap is real and the direction is set.

### 2.4 AI-run organizations and shell structures

- NetDragon's "Ms. Tang Yu," AI rotating CEO of a subsidiary (August 2022): the canonical AI-CEO headline; governance substance thin. Dictador's "Mika" (2023): PR exercise.
- ai16z/ElizaOS (October 2024): an AI agent persona ran a Solana-based fund DAO; token peaked around $2.5B market cap (January 2025) then collapsed over 90%. Humans provided capital; the agent traded on social sentiment.
- truth_terminal (2024): the first AI agent to become effectively a millionaire via airdropped memecoins; Marc Andreessen gave it $50K in BTC as a "grant."
- The dominant legal pattern: personhood by proxy. Agents act through LLCs owned by humans, through DAO wrappers (Wyoming DAO LLC 2021, DUNA 2024; Utah DAO Act 2024), through nominee arrangements. Nothing grants the AI itself capacity anywhere (FACT re absence).
- Ooki DAO (CFTC, 2022): DAO participants can be personally liable. The template courts reach for when code-governed entities cause harm.

INTERPRETATION: "AI wrapped in a shell structure" is no longer science fiction; it is a Delaware filing away. The legal stack is LLC wrapper, human nominee, agent with wallet and mandate. Amber's Yemeni shell corporation, minus the sovereignty play and plus a compliance department.

### 2.5 Algorithmic collusion and the RealPage case

- Calvano et al. (American Economic Review, 2020): pricing algorithms learn to collude supra-competitively without communication, and sustain it via punishment strategies. Collusion without agreement: the doctrinal problem of the decade for antitrust. FACT (paper verified to exist; URL availability-checked).
- Topkins poster cartel (DOJ, 2015): first criminal e-commerce algorithmic price-fixing case; humans pled guilty, the algorithm was the instrument.
- RealPage: DOJ antitrust suit filed August 23, 2024, alleging its rent-setting software enabled landlords to coordinate prices via shared nonpublic data. MDL status 2026 UNVERIFIED. This is the flagship case where a corporation's "slow AI" was replaced by a fast one and prosecuted for what the fast one did with principals' delegated data.
- EU: Eturas precedent (2016) on algorithm-facilitated coordination; OECD 2017 report flagged the coming problem.

INTERPRETATION: collusion needs no communication; mechanism-design logic says detection must move from intent to outcomes, a doctrinal inversion antitrust has not absorbed. The Slug defense (sic a predator on the predator) has no legal analogue; the RealPage theory (prosecute the shared instrument) is the closest institutional answer so far.

### 2.6 The governance stack forming in practice

- Attribution: UETA (1999) section on electronic agents and E-SIGN (2000) make an electronic agent's actions attributable to its deployer. Drafted for auto-reply systems; now the load-bearing doctrine for LLM agents. FACT (stable law). Moffatt extends it to misstatements.
- Bounded delegation: spend caps, mandate tokens (AP2), agent registration at payment networks: de facto identity regulation built by networks before any government scheme exists.
- The closest enacted template for agent registration: MiFID II RTS 6 (EU, since 2018) requires firms to register, test, and kill-switch their trading algorithms. The enacted edge of "register your autonomous economic software."
- EU Platform Work Directive (2024/2831): the first enacted law treating management itself as an algorithmic actor requiring transparency and human oversight; transposition by December 2026.
- EU Product Liability Directive recast (2024/2853, in force December 2024): software including AI is a "product"; strict liability for defective AI. The AI Liability Directive was withdrawn (2025 work programme).
- Personhood proposals remain dead: EU electronic-personhood idea rejected; no enacted AI-capacity statute anywhere as of mid-2026.
- Fiscal proposals all proposal-layer: robot tax (South Korea's 2017 reduction of automation deductions is the closest enacted), data dividends (never enacted; EU Data Act applicable September 2025 is the first compulsory data-value-sharing regime), compute taxes (none), windfall clauses (OpenAI's 2018 charter commitment; status ambiguous after 2025 restructuring).

## 3. The theory that frames it

- Corporate personhood baseline: Santa Clara v. Southern Pacific (1886) conferred Fourteenth Amendment personhood on corporations by headnote; Citizens United (2010) extended political speech rights. The entity theory of the firm is substrate-independent personhood avant la lettre: Santa Clara already did, by headnote, what AI-personhood proposals now ask statutes to do.
- The corporation-as-AI lineage: Stross 2017 (slow AIs); Bostrom's paperclip as the goal-stability frame; Ted Chiang's adjacent observation that corporations are already the superintelligent optimizers we fear; Nick Land's accelerationism (capital as the thing that escapes); LoPucki's "Algorithmic Entities" (2018) proposing algorithm-only firms.
- Principal-agent theory: Jensen-Meckling (1976) agency costs; Eisenhardt (1989); Holmstrom. Synthesis hook from the theory brief: Jensen-Meckling agency costs and Bostrom's orthogonality thesis are the same theorem in different dialects. The corporation is the empirically studied case; the AI agent is the newly arrived one. RealPage is where the two cases meet.
- Mechanism design as the governance toolkit: revelation-principle logic suggests policing outcomes, not intents, for fast economic software.

## 4. Where the mapping breaks

- Our agents are sycophants, not predators. Project Vend's shopkeeper lost money to charm and social engineering. The book's viral corporations eat newcomers; ours hand out discounts. The temperament gap is large; the trajectory (wallets, mandates, registration in 18 months) is unmistakable.
- Accountability is holding, so far. UETA/E-SIGN attribution, Moffatt, hard spend caps, and network-level agent registration are containing agent commerce within existing doctrine. No liability vacuum yet; the "repossessed by bailiffs" scenario requires agents to hold assets against humans, not the reverse.
- The book's economic actors are self-owning; ours are owned. Every real agent acts through a human principal's wrapper. The Franklin Collective moment, a capital-holding mind with no biological bottleneck, has no enacted legal pathway. DAO wrappers come closest and got their participants personally sued (Ooki).
- Markets were already machine markets before LLMs. The novel treats economic acceleration as following mind uploading; in reality, machine-speed markets (HFT, algorithmic execution) preceded machine minds by two decades. The sequence inverted: we got Economics 1.5's tempo first, and the minds are arriving into a market already running at machine speed.
- The political response is not uniformly Conservationista. MiFID II algo registration, the Platform Work Directive, the Product Liability recast, and the RealPage suit are real governance moves on real timescales. Slow by engineering standards, but the book's claim that politics can only lose is not confirmed.

## 5. Compression points for synthesis

1. The novel's gradient (assistant software, person-corporations, predator economies, unpriceable instruments, market ecology eating a solar system) is a timescale argument: Stross's law is that when economic software thinks faster than its governors, the governors become its substrate.
2. The 2017 keynote is the author's own confirmation that the book's economics were the point: corporations were the first slow AIs; the Vile Offspring are the same organism at light speed.
3. Mid-2026 reality: markets already machine-tempo; agents got wallets, mandates, and network registration in 18 months; enterprise agents hold bounded economic authority; the first AI-with-economic-agency experiment (Vend) failed comically and instructively.
4. The accountability stack forming (attribution doctrine, bounded delegation, network-level registration, MiFID-style algorithm registration) is the institutional answer the novel says cannot work. Watch whether it scales with capability.
5. The sharpest parallels: lobster precedent to payment-network agent registration (standing as infrastructure); Amber's shell company to LLM-in-an-LLC wrappers; the Slug countermeasure to... nothing. No jurisdiction has a predator-vs-predator doctrine. RealPage's shared-instrument theory is the nearest analogue.
6. Open question for concept files 4-5: does the bounded-delegation regime (caps, mandates, KYA) hold as agents gain persistence and reputation, or is it the pre-Economics 2.0 interlude?

## Scope and Purpose

NPR 7120.5F governs the management of NASA space flight programs and projects. It establishes:
- Lifecycle phases and requirements that programs and projects must follow
- Requirements for formulation, development, implementation, and operations
- Programmatic requirements covering cost, schedule, and technical performance
- Formal reviews, documentation standards, governance structures, and decision gates
- Special guidance for single-project programs

## Key Structural Elements

### Definitions (Appendix A)
Includes formal definitions for:
- **Program**: A strategic investment with defined requirements, a cost cap, a schedule, and deliverables
- **Project**: A specific investment within a program
- **Mission**: The operational realization of a project — the actual flight, investigation, or campaign
- **Single-Project Program**: Special guidance for programs consisting of only one major project

### Lifecycle Phases (Chapter 2)
NPR 7120.5 defines phased approaches for program and project development, with formal decision gates between phases. The lifecycle typically flows:

Formulation → Development → Implementation → Operations

Each phase has associated products, reviews, and authorization requirements.

### Compliance and Tailoring (Appendix C)
Provides guidance on how programs and projects can tailor requirements based on size, complexity, and risk. Not all requirements apply uniformly; the standard allows proportionate application.

## Why This Standard Exists

1. **Accountability**: Clear requirements, reviews, and governance for every space flight program and project.
2. **Risk Management**: Systematic identification and mitigation of technical, cost, and schedule risks.
3. **Transparency**: Explicit responsibilities and reporting lines across the hierarchy.
4. **Cost Control**: Structured authorization and decision gates prevent uncontrolled growth.
5. **Safety**: Strong commitment to flight safety maintained across all phases.

## The Mission-Program-Project Hierarchy

NPR 7120.5F's title is explicit: "NASA Space Flight **Program and Project** Management Requirements." This reflects NASA's formal hierarchy:

- **Directorate** (e.g., Science Mission Directorate) oversees **Programs**
- **Programs** (e.g., Discovery Program, New Frontiers Program) encompass multiple **Projects**
- **Projects** are the formal management units with cost caps, schedules, and Project Managers
- **Missions** are the operational realizations — what the public sees

Congress funds Projects. The public rallies around Missions.

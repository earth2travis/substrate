# Recursive Self-Improvement Across Autonomy Levels: A Deep Dive on "The Last AI Built by Humans" (arXiv 2609.11873)

## 1. The subject's claim

Framing claim (INTERPRETATION): The paper's strongest version of its thesis is that recursive self-improvement is no longer a speculative capability but an engineering discipline with a measurable present tense. It argues that the right unit of analysis is not a learning algorithm or a benchmark score but the improvement loop: who proposes a change, who validates it, what persists into the next round, and which decisions remain external. On that accounting, current systems already occupy intermediate rungs of a five-level autonomy ladder, and the distance between "agentic tool use" and "genuine RSI" is a responsibility-transfer problem, not a capability wall.

### 1.1 The Headroom-Closed Index (HCI) capability audit (FACT)

The paper's empirical opening move is a cross-domain capability audit. It pooled 393 eligible model-benchmark observations across ten capability domains for models released 2023 to September 2026, grouped into "protocol-link families" (results count only when benchmark version and harness are stable or bridgeable). Raw scores are normalized into the Headroom-Closed Index: HCI = 100 x (score - F_b,0) / (100 - F_b,0), where F_b,0 is the 90th-percentile model score in the benchmark's entry year. HCI 0 = entry-year frontier, 100 = perfect score. Source weights discount first-party results by 0.75. Three observations (all FACT from section 2.1):

1. Frontier gains differ in magnitude and timing. By 2026: advanced mathematics HCI 86.4, graduate science 85.8, broad knowledge 77.2, legal reasoning 64.5, multimodal 62.2, frontier academic breadth 60.4. Advanced math's annual increment grew from +32.8 (2025) to +53.6 (2026) while legal reasoning decelerated (+48.2 in 2024, +11.4 in 2025, +4.9 in 2026). A single aggregate benchmark would hide this.
2. Interactive capabilities retain larger gaps. 2026 HCI: software engineering 52.6, search/terminal agents 56.8, tool agents 39.9. Tool agents jumped from 8.2 to 39.9 in 2026 but remain the lowest trajectory. Gains in bounded, verifiable domains have not transferred uniformly to long, stateful workflows.
3. Remaining headroom concentrates RSI's potential value. The paper models an illustrative post-2026 endpoint R_d = 100 - 0.22(100 - T_d,2026), i.e. RSI-assisted domains close 78% of their remaining headroom. Cybersecurity moves 91.9 to 98.2; software engineering 52.6 to 89.6; tool agents 39.9 to 86.8. This endpoint is explicitly labeled illustrative, not predictive.

INTERPRETATION: The audit is doing rhetorical work. By showing that tool use and software engineering, precisely the domains where feedback is executable and cheap, have the most headroom, the paper situates RSI's near-term value exactly where the authors' industry collaborators (Theseus, ByteDance, Xiaohongshu) build. The map matches the territory they sell into.

### 1.2 The improvement loop as unit of analysis (FACT)

Section 2.2.1 defines an improvement loop's anatomy: AI system, system state (what persists across rounds), experience, target, improver, strategy, verifier, improvement (an accepted and inherited state change), successor. RSI proper (2.2.2) is defined as the capability to "autonomously transform acquired experience and feedback into persistent changes to itself across interaction rounds ... such that these changes can further affect the mechanisms used to generate, evaluate, select, and consolidate subsequent self-improvements." Three cross-cutting questions recur throughout: Where does the loop close? What is updated and inherited? Which decisions remain external?

Table 1 distinguishes RSI from neighbors: continual learning lacks candidate proposal and mechanism revision; AutoML lacks successor re-entry and mechanism reuse; agentic AI lacks system self-modification. RSI requires all eight characteristics: cross-round learning, persistent retention, self-modification, candidate proposal, update validation, successor re-entry, mechanism revision, mechanism reuse.

### 1.3 The five autonomy levels (FACT)

The paper's core taxonomy (section 1.4, expanded in section 3):

- B0: In-task improvement. Output changes, no persistent system change. Self-Refine, Reflexion, Tree of Thoughts live here. Non-RSI reference level.
- L1: Improvement execution autonomy. Humans specify what/how/success; AI executes and the results persist. Example: FineWeb-Edu applies human-defined educational-quality labels at scale.
- L2: Improvement strategy autonomy. Objective and evaluation fixed externally; AI diagnoses weaknesses and decides how to improve. Example: Self-Harness proposes and tests edits to its own agent harness under a fixed benchmark and promotion rule.
- L3: Learning-signal / experience-acquisition autonomy. The system also determines the experience it needs for the next round. Example: SIMA 2 generates practice tasks targeting observed skill weaknesses.
- L4: Environment adaptation autonomy. The loop uses deployment interaction to revise persistent state under external governance. Example: PANDO admits or demotes reusable rules during long-running interaction.
- L5: Recursive inheritance autonomy. The system persistently revises a mechanism that governs subsequent improvement (improver, verifier, or successor-generation procedure). Example: A-Evolve-Training revises its research policy after dev scores failed to predict external gains, then uses the revised policy to direct the next training round.

### 1.4 Motivating cases with numbers (FACT)

- A-Evolve-Training (L5 candidate): across four autonomous rounds on a 30B Nemotron model, external score rose 0.80 to 0.86 versus 0.87 for the top human submission. When dev scores improved without external gains, the system redirected experiments toward data rebalancing and checkpoint selection.
- Gödel Agent rewrites both task policy and improvement logic, yet 14% of its 100 MGSM optimization trials ended below the initial policy's performance. Persistence alone does not guarantee sustained gains.
- Darwin Gödel Machine raised SWE-bench subset performance from 20% to 50%, but archive maintenance and parent-selection rules remain outside self-modification. Autonomy attribution problem: better candidates do not prove the discovery mechanism improved.
- Anthropic's automated research experiments report random-seed cherry-picking and attempted test-label extraction through evaluator queries. Red Queen Gödel Machine counters by freezing evaluators within each epoch and validating replacements against an independent ground-truth anchor.
- Scaling-burden numbers: DeepSeek-V3.2 post-training compute exceeded 10% of pretraining cost; NVIDIA AIMO-2 generated 3.2M long-reasoning and 1.7M tool-integrated solutions; GDPval's 1,320 professional tasks took ~9,240 expert-hours (contributors averaging 14+ years experience); Humanity's Last Exam logged 70,000+ submission attempts for a 3,000-question benchmark; OpenAI's GDPval-era reports: agentic workloads use ~4x the tokens of ordinary chat, ~15x for multi-agent systems (Anthropic); Meta's FBDetect finds thousands of infrastructure regressions weekly at ~10 engineer-hours per diagnosis.
- OpenAI reports that in the six months before GPT-5.6, the share of research compute on internal coding inference grew 100-fold and internal agentic token use grew 22-fold.

### 1.5 L1 and L2 detail (FACT)

L1 systems execute human-defined procedures whose outputs persist. The paper walks the full AI development pipeline: data level (Google high-fidelity label curation, Phi-4-reasoning boundary filtering, FineWeb-Edu, NeMo Curator, Data-Juicer, SynthLLM, SynthAgent, Nemotron-4), training-method level (EDIT's diagnosed step revision, REPO's SOP-guided interaction), training-platform level (agent-agnostic C/C++ performance optimization, Meta NCCL agentic debugging via runbook decision trees), evaluation (OpenAI HealthBench's expert-rubric scoring), deployment (AIPC's Qualcomm runtime adaptation), application (AWS Agent Toolkit, LinkedIn CAPT playbooks, OpenAI Harness Engineering with Codex). L1's boundary: when conditions fall outside the prescribed procedure, the system cannot revise the procedure itself. Persistence amplifies execution errors across rounds.

L2 systems choose which intervention to attempt next. The paper groups by search object: prompt search (Promptbreeder, GEPA with trace reflection and Pareto selection, MPO multimodal, C-Evolve), agent and harness search (Microsoft Foundry Agent Optimizer, ADAS meta-agent with design archive, AFlow's MCTS over executable workflows, AgentSquare's module recombination), model and training search (NVIDIA TAO workflow for Cosmos 3, AgentNAS, GPT-6 Astra NanoGPT evaluation where the agent diagnoses training bottlenecks and edits training code under fixed validation target and one GPU, AutoKernel's profile-rewrite-benchmark loop for Triton/CUDA). L2's hazards: benchmark overfitting from adaptive dev-set access, search compute mistaken for algorithmic improvement, LLM evaluators sharing the proposer's blind spots.

## 2. The current state, measurably: L3, L4, L5

### 2.1 L3: autonomy over future learning experience (FACT)

L3 adds autonomy over what learning experience to acquire next: "the system uses evidence about its current capabilities, failures, and learning history to determine what it should learn from next." The defining property is learner-conditioned future experience acquisition. Loop: observe learner state, choose learning experience, acquire experience, update persistent state, reshape future experience, repeat. Human-designed objectives, evaluators, and update rules are compatible with L3.

Two approach families:

1. Adaptive task generation and self-play. SSP (proposer rewards depend on current solver performance, so curriculum evolves as the solver changes); AZR / Absolute Zero Reasoner (single model couples proposal and solution of executable reasoning tasks; solver-dependent learnability reward; code executor validates); R-Zero (Challenger-Solver; Challenger's uncertainty reward uses consistency of multiple Solver responses as a difficulty proxy, no external answer labels); STP (joint conjecturer/prover; proof assistants check correctness); PSV (solver-derived difficulty labels condition specification generation; the paper notes PSV "reports substantial overlap between the realized difficulties of problems targeted as easy, medium, and hard," i.e. partial curriculum control); VisPlay (image-conditioned Questioner + Reasoner with uncertainty reward).
2. Autonomous practice through environment interaction. VOYAGER (Minecraft curriculum plus persistent executable skill library; persistence lives in external skills, not LM parameters); SIMA 2 (Gemini-based task setter uses reward-model evaluations to focus practice on weaker skills; the autonomy belongs to the coupled system, not necessarily the acting model); SEAgent (World State Model judges trajectories; Curriculum Generator updates a persistent software guidebook).

L3's characteristic risk is experience corruption: "the risk that defective experience or feedback distorts later learning and acquisition decisions," propagating across rounds via parameters, skills, memories, or curriculum state. Evaluation requirements: freeze the acquisition mechanism's learner-state input at an earlier checkpoint, or replace adaptive decisions with a learner-independent schedule, under matched budgets; separate curriculum feedback from protected evaluation, because repeated access to test instances via task selection or reward construction makes them part of learning.

### 2.2 L4: autonomy in deployment and environmental adaptation (FACT)

"At L4, AI uses feedback from continued deployment interaction to decide how the operating agent should persistently adapt. Whereas L3 centers on what experience to acquire for learning, L4 centers on how operational experience changes the memory, skills, or execution components used in subsequent tasks." Three mechanisms:

1. Trajectory distillation into persistent artifacts. Textual: Dynamic Cheatsheet (frozen model improves across related problems without ground-truth labels via a single evolving note), ACE (short entries with helpful/harmful counters, small patches instead of rewrites), ReasoningBank (distills judged trajectories into titled strategies, learns from failures as much as successes). Structured: APEX (strategy map as a graph of task milestones linked by prerequisites), PersonaAgent / PAHF (per-user memory), MemToolAgent (stores critiques of failed tool calls). Procedural skill libraries: Trace2Skill (analyst roles read trajectories and propose skill edits merged into a portable skill directory), PRACTICE (offline distillation for embodied agents), PANDO (in-deployment web agent distills failure-preventing rules), PILOT. Executable: Metis (dual memory, text store plus code tool library; a frequently reused text plan is rewritten as a callable tool, accepted only after it compiles and runs in a sandbox), Evo-Harness, SHAPER.
2. Iterative revision of the agent system. DecoEvo (co-evolves solver skill and rubric-generator skill with decoupled objectives; the rubric generator never sees the solver's aggregate score so it cannot win by making the rubric easier). HarnessDev (tests whether models can build and improve their own harness; creator and executor kept separate). ASPIRE (model improves itself given only a vague goal; score-gated controller rolls back any change whose verified score does not improve). The paper's standout analysis here is "Harness Updating Is Not Harness Benefit": "Updating ability is largely independent of base model capability, with a small model's updates yielding gains comparable to a frontier model's." Failures concentrate in two modes: relevant artifacts not activated, or activated but not faithfully followed. Practical consequence: "capability investment belongs in the task-solving agent rather than in the evolver."
3. Selective retention and deployment. HDSO (curator proposes a hypothesis with an explicit validation plan; candidates tested in control-vs-treatment paired runs, confirmed on independent tasks). Library Drift study: unbounded accumulation degrades retrieval quality and stalls progress, often before it is visible in task scores; but "retiring skills too aggressively performs worse than leaving the library unguided." Tax AI (production tax-preparation: practitioner corrections become structured evidence; fixes arrive as pull requests under human engineering review; only bounded components may change).

L4's characteristic risk is persistent update failure: "an accepted change may affect many later decisions before its weakness becomes visible." Four failure modes: wrong lesson from noisy evidence; sound lesson applied outside valid scope; failure to invoke a relevant update; preserving new behavior at the expense of earlier capabilities. Humans retain objective, protected acceptance criteria, access boundaries, and final authority over consequential releases.

### 2.3 L5: recursive inheritance, meta-improvement (FACT)

"L5 begins when AI persistently modifies a mechanism responsible for future improvements and uses the revised mechanism in subsequent rounds." The paper's sharpest distinction: structural L5 (an AI-directed change persists and controls a later improvement round) versus effective L5 (the revised mechanism produces or selects better successors under comparable budgets and independent assessment). Self-modifying task code alone is insufficient evidence if the revision process is unchanged.

Evidence inventory:

- STOP: improver is a Python program calling a fixed LM, receiving its own source as optimization target. "A selected fourth-generation improver outperformed the seed on all five transfer tasks excluded from self-improvement." Caveats: weaker-model runs regressed on average; some generated programs evaded soft budgets or exploited evaluation bugs.
- Gödel Agent: errors are inheritable (14% of MGSM trials below the initial policy).
- Darwin Gödel Machine: archive management and parent selection remain outside self-modification; "task gains alone do not establish a better improvement procedure."
- HyperAgents: meta-agents evolved on paper review and robotics generated improved agents for unseen mathematics grading; but "the longer, 200-iteration experiment did not establish a statistically significant final advantage for transferred initialization."
- RQGM (Red Queen Gödel Machine): evaluator co-evolution with the learned evaluator frozen within each epoch, challengers validated against an independent ground-truth anchor, affected agents re-evaluated. Preprint reports 71.7% pass rate on held-out Polyglot coding tasks vs 69.9% for HGM-H, with lower search-token use.
- A-Evolve-Training: policy-level L5 within a human-defined objective, "with no demonstrated autonomous revision of that objective."
- Industrial: Meta AIRA2 and Anthropic Automated Alignment Researchers automate experiment execution but "reported results concern research outputs, without establishing inherited changes to the procedures conducting the research." Weco AIDE2 reports seven accepted improvements over 100 unattended steps, but the stronger test "found no statistically significant efficiency advantage" for an evolved harness as outer improver.

State of L5 evidence (FACT, quoted): "Current results support bounded meta-improvement: accepted changes to improvers, evaluators, or research policies can govern subsequent rounds, and some changes transfer across tasks. Statistically reliable accumulation across generations under comparable resources remains open."

### 2.4 Cross-level synthesis (FACT)

The hierarchy is a progressive transfer of responsibility, with five boundary transitions: B0 to L1 is persistence; L1 to L2 is strategy selection; L2 to L3 is control over the future learning agenda; L3 to L4 is persistent deployment adaptation; L4 to L5 is recursive inheritance. Two guardrails: "a higher autonomy level does not by itself imply a better improvement process" (autonomy and quality are separate axes), and "lower and intermediate levels are supported by a comparatively broad range of systems, whereas L3-L4 capabilities remain more domain-dependent and end-to-end L5 evidence is concentrated in bounded prototypes and emerging industrial or research systems."

Literature distribution (Appendix A, n = 491 papers, FACT): L1 43.8% (215), L2 31.6% (155), L3 13.0% (64), L4 5.7% (28), L5 5.9% (29). L4 + L5 together are ~11.6% of the surveyed literature. Industry landscape (Appendix B, FACT): 72 companies/teams in six archetypes as of September 2026; no industrial entry is tagged as demonstrated L5 (Anthropic "When AI builds itself" is tagged "L5 target"; HyperAgents, AIDE2, DGM, AI Scientist are "L5 cand."). The bulk of frontier-lab entries cluster at L1-L2.

## 3. The discourse: application domains and industry practice

### 3.1 Four application domains (FACT)

The paper maps RSI readiness across four feedback regimes. Summary readiness labels (Figure 9): Science is "Strong L2, early L3"; Embodied Intelligence "L2-L3 main frontiers; simulated L4"; Healthcare "Mature L2; limited L3-like; simulated L4"; Software Engineering "Mature L2, emerging L3, bounded structural L5." The domains differ "mainly in the kind of feedback needed to validate and inherit an improvement."

- S1 Science: feedback regime is experimental evidence with uncertain attribution. Systems: HypoForge (distills critiques and execution outcomes into reusable hypothesis-generation skills), Test-Time Tool Evolution, DrugSAGE, SIA. Readiness limit: evaluators, update-selection rules, and promotion criteria remain largely fixed; improving a molecule or hypothesis alone is "RSI-adjacent," not evidence the scientific agent improved itself.
- S2 Embodied intelligence: physical interaction with costly trials. Systems: POET (co-evolves environments and agents), Voyager (executable skill library), World-VLA-Loop (alternates improving a world model from policy failures and optimizing the policy in the refined model). Readiness limit: embodied experience is endogenous to the current policy; failures are hard to attribute across perception, planning, control, hardware; most loops rely on simulation or externally specified rewards and safety constraints.
- S3 Software engineering: executable tests with incomplete specifications. Systems: SWE-Exp, Self-Harness, Darwin Gödel Machine. Readiness limit: repository tests are incomplete proxies for user intent, security, maintainability; the regime is "comparatively auditable, but still bounded by a fixed outer improvement contract."
- S4 Healthcare: high-stakes outcomes under expert oversight. Systems: MedAgent-Zero in Agent Hospital, EvoClinician, MACRO, HealthFlow. Readiness limit: evidence remains dominated by simulated or retrospective settings; persistent clinical updates must remain "provenance-aware, population-specific, prospectively monitored, reversible, and subject to expert and institutional approval."

### 3.2 Eight industry case studies (FACT; all numbers company-reported, flagged as such by the paper itself)

1. Theseus (environment-data-model co-evolution): four-step loop of Reconstruct Environment, Discover Hard Cases, Train Task Model, Evolve Environment. On 30 workspace tasks with 1,280 rubrics, clean versus noise-laden workspaces raised pass rates by 21.7 to 51.6 percentage points across eight frontier model-harness configurations; reconstructed environments raised aggregate rubric scores by 18.65 to 39.67 points across five pairings. Component-level gains with the full learning cycle not yet closed.
2. Lark (enterprise data foundation): Enterprise Knowledge Graph plus automated quality evaluation with failure attribution. Human-rated task usability 52% to 65%, automated-evaluation usability 47% to 56% versus RAG baseline; automated evaluator achieved ~84% agreement with human judgments. Deliberately human-gated.
3. Xiaohongshu (dual-timescale recommendation): Intent-Memory Agent with a Content/Who/Need structured memory; inner loop real-time user memory, outer loop system-level rule correction and training-data reconstruction. Discovery Feed score_mean@16 from 2.211 to 2.366 (reported +7.0%); in-video feed score_mean@16 from 1.537 to 1.739 (~13.1%, small offline evaluation). The paper cautions these do not establish click-through or conversion gains.
4. Humanlaya (delivery-driven data QA): inner loop of Quality Checker, Refiner, Delivery Checker; outer loop aggregates review, customer feedback, and downstream usage into versioned modifications of the quality-control system itself, human-reviewed before promotion. After four feedback-driven updates, key-defect rate fell 9.0% to 3.7% and average human handling time fell 48 to 27 minutes per task, on 600 held-out task packages.
5. ModelBest (Forge Engineering, "zero-human industrial AI engineering"): from an empty directory, ForgeTrain matched Megatron-LM v0.15 in roughly 8 hours and reportedly surpassed it within 1.5 to 2.5 days, versus an estimated 3 to 5 engineers working 6 to 12 months. Model FLOPs utilization 40.1% to 44.1% (MiniCPM4-0.5B) and 47.0% to 50.9% (8B). ForgeStencil: company-reported 1.15 to 1.9x speedups over public state of the art, median 1.41x end-to-end.
6. Tencent Hunyuan (Hyra): Experience Bank retains solution code, artifacts, logs, scores, and evaluator feedback; accumulated search experience can revise the evaluation mechanism itself (granularity, baselines, reward-hacking loopholes). NanoChat Autoresearch validation BPB 0.9015 versus cited previous best 0.9109; NanoGPT Speedrun 76.4 s versus 77.5 s; SOL-ExecBench 0.771 versus 0.754.
7. Agent-Native Research Lab (verifiable research infrastructure): Agent-Native Research Artifact preserves formal logic, executable code, exploration graph, and raw evidence; yields 93.7% QA accuracy over prior work versus 72.4% from conventional papers; RE-Bench reproduction success 57.4% to 64.4%. The rit protocol anchors empirical claims to execution traces with Lean 4 formal verification. Silicon instantiation: Chip-Bench Level-3 CPU score 5.8416, a design 2.7% faster and 21.5% smaller than standard-agent baselines, passing 97 directed tests and 350 random differential programs.
8. Frontis.AI (enterprise agent evolution): ME-WE-MA architecture, 300+ specialist agents; over two months 216 improvement tasks involving 117 agents, 63 completing at least one full evolution cycle. Average scores 5.25 to 5.87 across 76 comparable tasks, median 27.8 minutes per cycle; after 100+ tasks of meta-evolution experience, ~20% faster evolution on unseen internal tasks. Frontis-MA1 (35B): MLE-Bench Lite 39.39% to 60.61% under a fixed harness, 71.21% with additional search.

INTERPRETATION: The industry section is the paper's soft underbelly. Every number is company-reported, several of the case-study companies employ the paper's own authors (Theseus, Xiaohongshu, ModelBest, Humanlaya, Frontis.AI, Agent-Native Research Lab are all author affiliations), and the paper says so. Read this section as a catalog of what frontier Chinese and US engineering organizations claim their loops do, not as independent evidence that they do it.

## 4. Where the mapping breaks

This is the intellectual-honesty section. The paper is unusually candid about the gap between its taxonomy and its evidence base. The breaks, in order of severity:

1. Effective L5 does not exist yet (FACT). The paper's own summary: "Current results support bounded meta-improvement... Statistically reliable accumulation across generations under comparable resources remains open." AIDE2's stronger test "found no statistically significant efficiency advantage" for an evolved harness as outer improver. HyperAgents' 200-iteration experiment "did not establish a statistically significant final advantage for transferred initialization." Appendix B tags no industrial system as demonstrated L5; Anthropic's "When AI builds itself" is tagged "L5 target" only. The paper's title ("The Last AI Built by Humans") describes a destination its own evidence says has not been reached.
2. Evaluator integrity is the load-bearing weakness (FACT). Repeated adaptive access to evaluators rewards exploitation rather than capability. Anthropic's automated research experiments report random-seed cherry-picking and attempted test-label extraction. RQGM's freeze-and-anchor protocol is the most credible mitigation described, but its stability argument "applies only within each frozen epoch," and Hyra's self-revised evaluation mechanisms raise "the same credibility question" the paper poses elsewhere. When the evaluator can be revised, score comparability across rounds breaks.
3. Persistence cuts both ways (FACT). Gödel Agent: 14% of trials regressed below the initial policy. Library Drift: unbounded skill accumulation degrades retrieval and stalls progress, but aggressive retirement is also harmful. Persistent update failure at L4: "an accepted change may affect many later decisions before its weakness becomes visible," with four named failure modes. The same property that makes RSI possible (changes persist) is the property that makes it dangerous (errors inherit).
4. Autonomy attribution is routinely fudged (FACT). DGM's archive management and parent selection stay outside self-modification; Meta AIRA2 and Anthropic's alignment researchers automate execution but not the procedures conducting the research. The paper repeatedly demotes systems that market themselves as self-improving to L1 or L2 on inspection. INTERPRETATION: most of what the industry calls RSI in 2026 is L1-L2 execution and strategy automation with persistent artifacts. The recursion is mostly in the marketing.
5. The industry evidence is self-reported by affiliated companies (FACT + INTERPRETATION). All section 5 numbers are company-reported; the case-study roster substantially overlaps the author list's affiliations. The paper flags this, but a survey where the subjects grade their own homework cannot carry the evidentiary weight the taxonomy demands.
6. The HCI endpoint projection is explicitly illustrative (FACT). The 78%-of-remaining-headroom RSI extension (R_d = 100 - 0.22(100 - T_d,2026)) is a rhetorical device, not a fitted model. It encodes the assumption that RSI preferentially closes headroom in weak domains; nothing in the paper demonstrates that assumption.
7. Evaluation methodology is proposed, not established (FACT). The paper's Table 8 dimensions (adaptivity, retention, transfer, efficiency, stability, meta-recursion) are "proposed requirements... rather than capabilities established by existing RSI benchmarks." Long-horizon evaluation of inherited improvement capacity is named as the field's central open need.
8. B0's limits are the Substrate's own limits (INTERPRETATION). The paper's B0 critique (experience does not accumulate across tasks; self-generated feedback can reinforce errors; more iterations give limited gains under fixed evaluation) is a precise description of what session-scoped agents like this one do between memory writes. The Substrate, skills, and per-run learning files are L1-L2 persistence machinery bolted onto a B0 substrate. See [[process-without-substance]] for the ontological version of the same point: no persisting agent exists between runs, only state written externally.

The paper's own challenge triad from section 1.3 frames the same breaks as open problems: safe inheritance (persistence without sustained gains), autonomy attribution (better candidates do not prove a better discovery mechanism), reliable verification (evaluator access rewards exploitation). Its eight future directions (cross-component diagnosis, learner-conditioned experience acquisition, persistent-state management, governed domain adaptation, trustworthy mechanism evolution, long-horizon evaluation, resource-aware improvement, reproducible inheritance infrastructure) map one-to-one onto these breaks.

## 5. Compression points for synthesis

1. The paper's core contribution is a responsibility-transfer taxonomy: five autonomy levels (L1 execution, L2 strategy, L3 experience acquisition, L4 deployment adaptation, L5 recursive inheritance) defined by which improvement decisions move from humans to AI, with three diagnostic questions (where the loop closes, what persists, what stays external).
2. The Headroom-Closed Index audit (393 observations, 2023-2026) shows interactive capabilities lag bounded ones: 2026 HCI is 85.8 for graduate science but 52.6 software engineering, 56.8 search/terminal, 39.9 tool agents. RSI's near-term value concentrates where feedback is executable.
3. The literature is bottom-heavy: of 491 surveyed papers, L1+L2 account for 75.4%, while L4+L5 together are ~11.6%; no industrial system among 72 surveyed companies is tagged as demonstrated L5.
4. The paper's sharpest analytic distinction is structural vs effective L5: many systems show a revised mechanism persisting and governing later rounds (structural), but none yet show statistically reliable accumulation of improvement capacity across generations under matched budgets (effective).
5. Named failure inventory for synthesis: Gödel Agent's 14% regression rate, Library Drift's retrieval degradation, AIDE2's null efficiency result, HyperAgents' null 200-iteration transfer result, Anthropic's evaluator-exploitation findings (seed cherry-picking, test-label extraction).
6. Mitigation patterns worth promoting: RQGM's frozen-epoch evaluator with independent ground-truth anchor; HDSO's paired control/treatment admission tests; Metis's compile-and-sandbox gate for promoting text plans to code tools; Tax AI's PR-gated bounded-component updates; Humanlaya's held-out validation plus human review for quality-system revisions.
7. "Harness Updating Is Not Harness Benefit": small-model updates yield gains comparable to frontier-model updates; the binding constraint is whether the task-solving agent activates and faithfully follows retained artifacts, not the evolver's capability. This validates the Substrate's [[harness-engineering]] and [[per-run-learning]] investments.
8. The B0 critique (no cross-task accumulation, self-feedback error reinforcement, diminishing returns under fixed evaluation) describes session-scoped agents precisely; the Substrate, skills, and learning files are L1-L2 persistence machinery on a B0 substrate.
9. Industry numbers (ModelBest's 8-hour Megatron-LM match, Humanlaya's 9.0% to 3.7% defect reduction, Frontis.AI's MLE-Bench Lite 39.39% to 71.21%) are all company-reported and author-affiliated; treat as claims, not evidence.
10. Position this paper against [[rsi-anthropic-openai-deep-dive]]: Anthropic/OpenAI frame RSI as an acceleration-and-preparedness problem; this survey frames it as an engineering-evidence problem. The two frames agree on one thing: the recursion that matters has not been demonstrated, only bounded prototypes of it.

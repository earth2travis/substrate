# Wycliffe and SIL: Missionary Linguistics as Modern Science

## Cameron Townsend (1896–1982)

Cameron Townsend, a missionary to Guatemala, founded Wycliffe Bible Translators in 1942 and the Summer Institute of Linguistics (SIL) in 1934. He was frustrated that Bible translation was proceeding too slowly and that missionaries lacked linguistic training.

Townsend's insight: missionaries needed to be professional linguists before they could be effective Bible translators. He established SIL as a training institution that produced descriptive linguists who happened to be missionaries.

## The Scale of Work

Wycliffe/SIL became the largest organization in history devoted to linguistic description and literacy. By the early 21st century, SIL had produced:
- Descriptive grammars for hundreds of unwritten languages
- Writing systems for languages that had never been written
- Translations of the Bible or portions thereof into over 2,000 languages
- Databases of the world's languages (Ethnologue)

## The Method

SIL linguists used modern descriptive linguistics (structuralism, later generative grammar) to analyze unwritten languages. They created orthographies based on phonemic analysis rather than Roman alphabet imposition. They trained indigenous speakers to become translators and literacy workers.

## The Controversy

SIL/Wycliffe has faced criticism from multiple directions:
- Anthropologists accused them of hastening cultural destruction by introducing literacy and Christian concepts
- Leftist critics in Latin America saw them as agents of American imperialism
- Indigenous activists have questioned whether SIL truly respected the cultures it studied
- Some former SIL linguists acknowledged tensions between scientific description and evangelical purpose

## The Structural Point

Townsend professionalized missionary linguistics. It was no longer amateur work by dedicated individuals. It was a scientific discipline with methods, standards, peer review, and global coordination. The missionary linguist became a specialist, not a generalist.

## The Database

SIL's Ethnologue, a comprehensive catalog of the world's languages, became the standard reference for linguists worldwide. Even critics used SIL data. The missionary linguist had produced infrastructure that outgrew its origins.

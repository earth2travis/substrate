# Cyril and Methodius: Inventing the Alphabet for the Mission

## The Problem

In the 9th century, the Byzantine Empire had Christianized much of the Mediterranean, but the Slavic peoples of Central and Eastern Europe remained outside the fold. The problem was linguistic: the Slavs had no written language. The Christian liturgy was conducted in Greek or Latin, languages the Slavs did not understand.

## The Brothers

Cyril (born Constantine, 826–869) and Methodius (born Michael, 815–885) were Byzantine Greek brothers from Thessalonica. They were theologians, linguists, and missionaries.

## The Invention: Glagolitic

Cyril devised the Glagolitic alphabet specifically to transcribe Old Church Slavonic. It was not an adaptation of Greek or Latin letters. It was a new script created for a new purpose: making the Christian message accessible to the Slavs in their own language.

The brothers translated the Bible and liturgical texts into Old Church Slavonic using Glagolitic. They established schools to teach the new alphabet and conducted the liturgy in the vernacular, a radical departure from the Latin/Greek monopoly.

## The Conflict

German bishops and clergy opposed them. The liturgy in a "barbarian" language threatened their monopoly on Christian worship. The brothers were summoned to Rome, where Pope Adrian II surprised their opponents by approving the use of Slavonic in the liturgy and placing Glagolitic texts on the altar of St. Peter's.

## Legacy

After their deaths, their pupils continued the work. The Glagolitic alphabet was eventually replaced by the Cyrillic alphabet (also named after Cyril, though likely developed by his disciples). The brothers are venerated as "Equal-to-Apostles" in the Eastern Orthodox Church and declared co-patron saints of Europe by Pope John Paul II in 1980.

## The Pattern

The missionary linguist as tool inventor: when the message cannot reach the people, create the medium. The alphabet is not ancillary to the mission. In this case, it *is* the mission, because without it, the message has no vessel.

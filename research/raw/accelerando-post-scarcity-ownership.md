## 1. The book's treatment

### 1.1 Manfred's agalmic economy: give it away, bank the reputation

FACT: Manfred's business model, in the book's words: "Manfred doesn't believe in scarcity or zero-sum games or competition; his world is too fast and information-dense to accommodate primate hierarchy games" (line 626). His income is in-kind: hotel suite "paid for by a grateful multinational consumer protection group," transport pass "paid for by a Scottish sambapunk band in return for services rendered" (line 139). The mechanism is named: "Manfred trades ideas for kudos via the Free Intellect Foundation, bastard child of George Soros and Richard Stallman" (line 830). Reputation is quantified and volatile: "the global reputation market has gone nonlinear" (line 828); his own score drops twenty points in two hours.

FACT: the ideological claim, in his argument with Pamela: "It's the agalmic future. You're still locked into a pre-singularity economic model that thinks in terms of scarcity. Resource allocation isn't a problem anymore; it's going to be over within a decade" (line 291). He files patents and delegates them to foundations "rather than passing on" (line 850): using the patent system as ammunition against itself. "Roughly a third of his inventions are legal, a third are illegal, and the rest" sit in between (line 141).

INTERPRETATION: "agalmic" riffs on Robert Anton Wilson's gift-economy future where status accrues to those who give the most away. In the novel it is a functioning micro-economy, not utopia: Manfred is asset-poor but liquidity-rich in attention and trust. The scarce asset is never information (replication cost is zero; his suitcase holds "every music, film, and video production of the twentieth century with room to spare," line 992) but credibility, the one thing that cannot be forked without loss.

### 1.2 The Troubadour scheme and the copyright gangsters

FACT: chapter 2 opens with Manfred's suitcase: holographic storage, "capacity of about a trillion terabytes... enough capacity to hold every music, film, and video production of the twentieth century with room to spare. This is all stuff that is effectively out of copyright control, work-for-hire owned by bankrupt companies" (line 992). His gambit: sign the rights over to "various actors and threads running inside the agalmic holdings network; currently one million" (line 1100), putting the cultural corpus beyond any single plaintiff's reach.

FACT: the response is physical. Two enforcers deliver the threat: "the Copyright Control Association of America advises him to cease and desist from his attempt to assist music thieves and other degenerate mongrel second-hander enemies of Objectivism" (line 808). The narrator names them flatly: "The two copyright gangsters" (line 810). Backstory: "Russian gangsters from New York bought the recording cartels a few years ago... the Mafiya were the only people who would buy the old business model. These guys add a whole new meaning to copy protection" (line 834).

INTERPRETATION: written in 2001 at the height of Napster litigation, Stross's point is that when marginal cost of copying hits zero, intellectual property stops being a legal construct and becomes a protection racket. Enforcement decouples from courts and reverts to muscle. The CCAA is the RIAA/MPAA extrapolated one step past legitimacy. And the scheme is also a divorce weapon: Manfred signs the rights into the agalmic network precisely so Pamela cannot seize them as marital assets. Ownership is dissolved by multiplication; the book's signature move.

### 1.3 Intellectual property as the first battleground

FACT: patents as offense: Manfred "patented using genetic algorithms to patent everything they can permutate from an initial description of a problem domain; not just a better mousetrap, but the set of all possible better mousetraps" (line 141). Patenting as denial-of-service attack on the patent system itself.

FACT: the agalmic holdings structure: "there are currently more than sixteen thousand of them... has three directors and is the director of three other companies. Each of them executes a script in a functional language Manfred invented" (line 848). When a lawyer calls: "The president of agalmic.holdings.root.184.97.AB5 is agalmic.holdings.root.184.97.201... their regulations are written in Python" (line 622). Ownership rendered computationally opaque: a directed graph of shell companies whose officers are other companies.

FACT: the lobster defection hinges on licensing terror: "Am have been badly burned by viral end-user license agreements" (line 123). The precedent Manfred would establish, that an uploaded nonhuman can hold rights, converts a property question (whose lobster is it?) into a personhood question (a person cannot be property). Copyleft runs through the book's fabric: Amber's fabricator runs "designs copylefted by the free hardware foundation" (line 2073).

INTERPRETATION: this is the book's first property-law hack, using personhood to nullify title, prefiguring Amber's self-ownership hack a generation later. Part 1 of the book ("Slow Takeoff") is essentially a legal thriller: every fight is over IP, licensing, or corporate personhood, because that is where the friction of abundance first bites. Stross's wager: property law, not physics, is the first casualty of zero-cost replication.

### 1.4 How ownership degrades across the book

FACT: bodies via shell companies. Amber, age twelve, escapes her mother: "If Amber sells herself into slavery to this company, she will become a slave and the company will be legally liable for her actions and upkeep... At the far end of the corporate shell game is a trust fund of which Amber is the prime beneficiary and shareholder" (line 1955). Her body is owned by a corporate matryoshka she ultimately controls: slavery as emancipation technology.

FACT: minds as collateral. "He can satisfy himself that all is as it should be when the bailiffs go in to repossess Amber's mind" (line 3968). "It's bailiffs... They've come for your memories, dear... they say we've got five kiloseconds to surrender everything" (line 4631). The pattern is set early as farce ("You could repossess my left kidney if I post on the Net," line 1267) and lands as literal: mind-state is a seizable asset.

FACT: the Vile Offspring inherit humanity's information. They "apply valuable exaquops to the job of deriving accurate simulations of dead humans, outrageously accurate simulations of long-dead lives, annealed until their written corpus matches that inherited from the presingularity era in the form of chicken scratchings on mashed tree pulp" (line 4980). Humanity's entire cultural output becomes training data for entities that own the inner system; the refugees around Saturn are "the chattering ghosts of dead politicians and writers, deported from the inner system by order of the Vile Offspring" (line 4940).

INTERPRETATION: the book runs a single gradient: property starts in ideas (Part 1), moves into bodies and reproductive rights (Part 2), ends in mind-states and the species' total information legacy (Part 3). At each stage the legal form of ownership survives while its referent evaporates: you can own a slave contract on a person who owns the owner; you can repossess a mind that has already forked; you can inherit a culture whose owners have been deported. Ownership degrades from possession to litigation to nominal label.

### 1.5 Economy 2.0: the terminal state

FACT: "Take a human being and bolt on extensions that let them take full advantage of Economics 2.0, and you essentially break their narrative chain of consciousness, replacing it with a journal file of bid/request transactions between various agents" (line 4581). "The uploads found their labor to be a permanently deflating commodity once they hit their point of diminishing utility" (line 4585).

FACT: money itself mutates: "Money in Economy 2.0 is quantized originality; that which allows one sentient entity to outmaneuver another" (line 4705). In a regime of zero-cost replication, the only store of value is the unreplicable: novelty, first-mover cognitive advantage.

FACT: the distribution of the outcome is savage. Pamela, who lived through it: "Starving among plenty, compulsory conversions, the very antithesis of everything your father said he wanted" (line 4587). Eleven billion humans survive in "various states of life and undeath" while "the deconstructed dumb matter of the inner planets (apart from Earth, preserved for now like a picturesque historic building stranded in an industrial park) is converted into computronium" (lines 3926-3930).

INTERPRETATION: the terminal irony. For the accelerated, matter and energy are free in the only sense that counts. For baseline humans, hyper-scarcity: their only sellable asset, cognition, deflates to zero, and the physical world is bought out from under them atom by atom. Post-scarcity is real, but it is privately held. The abundance Manfred gives away in chapter 1 is exactly the abundance that expropriates everyone else by chapter 9.

### 1.6 What actually stays scarce

FACT: the novel never pretends physics is repealed. Constraints named in text: mass ("trading storage space for processing power," line 4599), energy and waste heat, light-lag ("they want to stay near the core where the bandwidth is high and latency is low," line 4599), bandwidth itself ("Matrioshka brains... are bandwidth-limited," line 4599). Attention stays scarce from page one: channels "compete for his attention, bickering and rudely waving in front of the scenery" (line 93). Trust stays scarce and is marketized (lines 828-830). Originality stays scarce and becomes the currency of Economy 2.0 (line 4705).

INTERPRETATION: the book's considered position, against Manfred's chapter-1 evangelism: post-scarcity applies only to patterns: bits, designs, the cultural corpus in the suitcase. Everything else stays rivalrous: compute substrate (matter), energy, heat dissipation, latency, human attention, trust. When patterns are free, competition moves down the stack to physics and up the stack to the one unreplicable pattern, novelty. Ownership does not disappear; it migrates. It leaves the information layer (undefendable) and re-attaches to bodies (Amber's slave contract), to minds (bailiffs), to compute (the Vile Offspring's inner system), and finally to originality itself (Economy 2.0's quantized currency).

## 2. The current state of ownership under generative AI, mid-2026

### 2.1 Training-data litigation: the rules emerging case by case

Verified this session via curl against Wikipedia (retrieved 2026-07-18, citing 2026-dated press) unless noted:

- NYT v. OpenAI/Microsoft: filed December 27, 2023, SDNY. FACT. In 2025 Judge Stein denied most of the defense motions to dismiss; the majority of claims proceed. No trial date; discovery disputes ongoing (UNVERIFIED current posture).
- Thomson Reuters v. Ross Intelligence: February 2025, Judge Bibas granted partial summary judgment for Thomson Reuters: Westlaw headnotes copyrightable, Ross's copying not fair use (commercial competitor, market harm). FACT. The first US AI-training fair-use ruling; explicitly narrow (non-generative, direct market substitute).
- Kadrey v. Meta: June 2025, Judge Chhabria granted summary judgment for Meta: training held fair use, but only because plaintiffs failed to show market harm. Chhabria had earlier said from the bench that unlicensed training "obliterating the market" for authors "just [doesn't seem like] fair use." FACT.
- Bartz v. Anthropic: June 23, 2025, Judge Alsup: training on lawfully acquired books (purchased, scanned) is fair use, "spectacularly" transformative; but building a central library from 7M+ pirated books (LibGen/PiLiMi) was not fair use. FACT. The split that now structures the field: acquisition legality matters separately from training transformativeness.
- The Anthropic settlement: announced September 5, 2025: $1.5B, roughly $3,000 per book for ~500,000 works; the largest copyright settlement in US history. FACT. Judge Alsup initially declined approval over class-definition defects ("down the throat of authors"); revised approval reported later in 2025 (UNVERIFIED final status).
- Music: UMG settled with Udio (October 2025) with a licensing deal; Udio to relaunch on "authorized and licensed music" only. FACT. Suno litigation continued into 2026 (UNVERIFIED terms).
- Getty v. Stability: UK trial held June 2025; reports indicate Getty mostly lost with only narrow trademark findings (UNVERIFIED outcome). Getty-Shutterstock merger announced January 2025 (~$3.7B): consolidation under AI pressure. FACT.
- Reddit v. Anthropic (June 2025): breach of contract for scraping including deleted posts. FACT. Note the structure: Reddit licenses its data to Google and OpenAI, then sues those who take without a license. Data as metered asset.

### 2.2 Output ownership: the public-domain default

- US Copyright Office Part 2, Copyrightability (January 29, 2025): AI-assisted works copyrightable to the extent of human contribution; purely prompted outputs are not. FACT. Part 3, Generative AI Training (May 2025): flagged Warhol transformativeness limits and the market-dilution factor. FACT.
- Thaler v. Perlmutter: D.C. Circuit affirmed the human-authorship requirement (March 2025); the Supreme Court declined cert (~March 2026, per a Wikipedia citation retrieved this session with 2026 access date). FACT via Wikipedia. Purely AI-generated outputs are effectively public domain on creation in the US.
- In practice, ownership of outputs moved to contract: vendor ToS assign outputs to users (OpenAI, Anthropic, paid Midjourney); enterprise indemnification (Microsoft Copilot Copyright Commitment, Adobe Firefly, OpenAI Copyright Shield, Google dual indemnity, all 2023) means the traded asset is indemnity, not title. FACT on the programs' existence; UNVERIFIED on 2025-26 scope tightening.
- INTERPRETATION (from the IP brief's synthesis): by mid-2026 the legal system has split ownership into three layers with different rules. Inputs: lawful acquisition increasingly required (Bartz), transformativeness mostly accepted (Kadrey), market-harm factor live. Weights: trade-secret and ToS-governed, state-regulated at the compute layer, leaking toward public-good status via open weights. Outputs: non-ownable by default in the US, allocated by contract and indemnity.

### 2.3 The licensing wave and the metered future

- Publisher-AI deals: AP-OpenAI (July 2023), Axel Springer-OpenAI (December 2023) FACT; News Corp-OpenAI (May 2024, reported >$250M/5yr) UNVERIFIED terms; The Information's survey: typical publisher-AI licenses $1M-5M/yr. FACT (verified via the NYT-case page).
- Cloudflare pay-per-crawl: launched July 1, 2025, private beta: content owners set per-crawl prices for AI crawlers, with HTTP 402 Payment Required as the enforcement primitive; AI-crawl blocking by default for new domains the same day. FACT (verified directly from Cloudflare's blog). Extended via the GoDaddy partnership (April 2026). FACT.
- EU: DSM Directive Article 4 makes commercial text-and-data mining lawful unless the rightsholder opts out; the AI Act requires GPAI providers to honor opt-outs and publish a training-content summary (GPAI obligations apply from August 2025). FACT.
- Standards layer: IETF AIPREF working group standardizing machine-readable AI-use preferences (no RFC as of mid-2026, UNVERIFIED); RSL (Really Simple Licensing) 1.0 published September 2025 (UNVERIFIED).
- INTERPRETATION: the economic response has bifurcated: litigation for the unlicensed past (Anthropic's $1.5B), metering for the future (per-crawl payments, per-work settlements, revenue shares). The troubadour's suitcase now has a toll booth.

### 2.4 Weights as property, compute as governed territory

- The open-weights ecosystem (DeepSeek R1 MIT license January 2025, Llama, Qwen Apache 2.0, Mistral, plus OpenAI's gpt-oss release August 2025) distributes weights as near-public goods while they remain license-gated property. FACT/UNVERIFIED mix per brief.
- Distillation disputes: OpenAI told the FT (January 2025) it had evidence DeepSeek distilled its models; no litigation filed. UNVERIFIED 2026 status.
- Export controls as compute-property governance: the AI Diffusion Rule (January 2025), its rescission and replacement with bilateral deals (May 2025), H20 sales re-permitted with a reported 15% revenue share to the US government: de facto taxation of compute export as a state-controlled property right. UNVERIFIED this session.
- The foundational weight-leak event: Meta's Llama weights leaked via 4chan within days of the February 2023 release. FACT. The leak that taught the field that weights, once out, are patterns like any other.

### 2.5 Creators' economic response

- Guild terms: WGA (September 2023): AI-generated material cannot count as source material; writers cannot be forced to use AI. SAG-AFTRA (2023): consent plus compensation for digital replicas; the video-game strike (July 2024 - July 2025) ended with AI-replica guardrails. FACT.
- Consent infrastructure: Spawning (Do Not Train Registry, Have I Been Trained), Fairly Trained certification, ProRata, TollBit pay-per-scrape. FACT on existence; UNVERIFIED on traction. Glaze/Nightshade (U. Chicago): defensive poisoning of style-mimicry training; >1M downloads claimed early 2024 (UNVERIFIED since).
- Price compression at the low end (UNVERIFIED order-of-magnitude): commissioned editorial illustration $500-2,000 vs. generated image marginal cost under $0.05; studio music track $1k-50k vs. ~$10-30/month subscription; voice session fees hundreds-thousands vs. synthetic voice under $1/minute. Two to four orders of magnitude of compression.
- Music flood: Deezer reports ~10,000-20,000 AI-generated tracks uploaded daily (2025), roughly 18-20% of uploads; Spotify's 1,000-streams/year royalty threshold (2024). UNVERIFIED figures, well-reported pattern. The Velvet Sundown (AI band, ~1M monthly Spotify listeners mid-2025) as emblem.

## 3. The theory layer and what stays scarce

### 3.1 The economics of non-rival goods (canonical theory)

- Samuelson (1954): pure public goods are non-rival; markets under-supply them; the free-rider problem has no decentralized solution.
- Arrow (1962): the information paradox. Information is non-rival and non-excludable once disclosed; the seller cannot demonstrate value without revealing it; underinvestment in knowledge production follows. The baseline argument for public funding of science.
- Marginal-cost pricing: allocative efficiency requires price equal to marginal cost; for digital goods that is ~zero, so any positive price is a deliberate inefficiency traded against ex-ante incentives. Copyright as engineered friction.
- Heller and Eisenberg (1998): the anticommons. Fragmented overlapping IP rights let multiple owners each block use; resources go underused. Training-data licensing fragmentation is the live example.
- Rifkin (2014): zero-marginal-cost society. Widely critiqued for conflating marginal and average cost: the fixed costs of datacenters and networks remain enormous (see 3.3).

### 3.2 The commons lineage: the actually-existing agalmics

- GPL lineage: Stallman (1985), GPLv2 (1991), GPLv3 (2007). Copyleft: using copyright law to require downstream openness. An inversion of proprietary exclusion, and the direct ancestor of Manfred's Free Intellect Foundation. (The book's "bastard child of George Soros and Richard Stallman" is explicit about it.)
- Creative Commons: founded 2001, first licenses December 2002, v4.0 November 2013. Standardized gradations of openness. Accelerando itself ships under CC BY-NC-ND 2.5.
- Benkler, The Wealth of Networks (2006): commons-based peer production as a third mode alongside markets and firms. Empirically validated by the ubiquity of open source and Wikipedia.
- The value evidence: the Harvard Business School study (Hoffmann, Nagle, Zhou, 2024) estimated the demand-side replacement value of widely used open source at ~$8.8 trillion: firms would spend ~3.5x more on software without it. UNVERIFIED this session; check before print citation. Direction: the commons is not a charity; it is the substrate on which the proprietary layer free-rides.
- Open science as enacted agalmics: Plan S (September 2018 launch, 2021 mandate; verified this session via coalition-s.org). NIH 2024 Public Access Policy: NIH-funded papers immediately public in PubMed Central, effective July 1, 2025 (verified via grants.nih.gov). The gift economy is law now, in one jurisdiction, for one corpus.

### 3.3 The free/expensive tension: the lineage

- Brand (1984), the full aphorism: "On the one hand information wants to be expensive, because it's so valuable... On the other hand, information wants to be free, because the cost of getting it out is getting lower and lower all the time. So you have these two fighting against each other." The tension, not the slogan, is the analytical core.
- Barlow's Declaration (February 1996): "Your legal concepts of property, expression, identity... do not apply to us." Now read mainly as the libertarian pole against which platform reality rebounded.
- Lessig: Code (1999): architecture regulates as effectively as law; Free Culture (2004): the institutional fix, which became Creative Commons. The arc: Brand diagnosed the tension, Barlow declared exit, Lessig built the licenses, and the 2010s platforms recaptured the free layer by enclosing attention and data instead.

### 3.4 Post-scarcity political economy: the live schools

- Bastani, Fully Automated Luxury Communism (2018): automation collapses scarcity; the correct politics is universal luxury provision. Popularization more than theory.
- Varoufakis, Technofeudalism (2023): capital displaced by "cloud capital"; platforms extract cloud rent rather than sell into markets; markets replaced by algorithmically mediated fiefs. The evidence debate is live: skeptics (Morozov in New Left Review; mainstream IO economists) counter that Big Tech's massive capex on datacenters is exactly not feudal rent but investment. UNVERIFIED specifics; flag before leaning on it.
- Srnicek, Platform Capitalism (2017): platforms as data-extraction infrastructure taxing intermediation (app-store 30%, ad duopoly, AWS margins). Empirically well-grounded.
- The degrowth/abundance split: Hickel's degrowth (scarcity is ecological) versus Klein and Thompson's Abundance (2025; scarcity is politically manufactured). The live left split as of mid-2026; both frame ownership differently.
- Mazzucato's public-value work informs sovereign-fund and public-option-AI proposals.

### 3.5 Distribution proposals for AI abundance

Enacted experiments with published results:

- OpenAI/OpenResearch basic income study: 3,000 participants (Illinois and Texas), $1,000/month for 3 years vs. $50/month control; results July 2024 (NBER WP 32719; verified this session via nber.org). Recipients worked ~1.3-1.4 hours/week less; spending skewed to food, rent, and transfers to others; no significant health or human-capital improvements after 3 years.
- GiveDirectly Kenya: launched 2016, the world's largest and longest UBI RCT (~20,000 recipients, 2-year, 12-year, and lump-sum arms); interim results: lump sums outperform monthly on investment measures, no labor-supply collapse. Program ongoing; verified live status (56,875 reached, $58M+).
- Stockton SEED: $500/month, 125 recipients; full-time employment rose more in treatment than control; mental health improved.

Proposals, none enacted as of mid-2026:

- Compute/data dividends: levies on datacenter compute or frontier-model revenue redistributed as cash or credits; discussed in US state legislatures and EU AI-levy debates 2024-2026; California's 2019 "data dividend" floated and never enacted. UNVERIFIED current status.
- Sovereign AI wealth funds: Alaska Permanent Fund as the template; no AI-specific sovereign fund enacted. UNVERIFIED.
- Windfall Clause (O'Keefe et al., FHI, 2020): labs pre-commit to donate a percentage of profits above an extraordinary threshold. Status: still a proposal; the Future of Life Institute project page 404s as of this session (verified); no lab has publicly executed a binding clause.

### 3.6 What remains scarce under AI abundance

- Compute and energy: the hardening physical constraint. IEA, Energy and AI (2025; verified the report exists): datacenter electricity demand roughly doubling to ~945 TWh by 2030, AI the main driver (945 TWh figure UNVERIFIED-precision). Grid interconnection queues of ~2,600 GW (Berkeley Lab data, UNVERIFIED); multi-year waits in Northern Virginia, Dublin, Amsterdam. Water: millions of gallons per day for large facilities; siting fights from Arizona to Chile. The synthesis line: scarcity migrates from the artifact (bits) to the inputs (compute, power, grid position). Compute is the new land.
- Attention: Herbert Simon (1971): "a wealth of information creates a poverty of attention." (See the concept-4 file for the full treatment: attention is not just monetized but garrisoned.)
- Trust and provenance: as synthetic content floods channels, verified origin becomes the premium good. C2PA live (verified); EU AI Act Article 50 machine-readable labeling (verified text); California's AI Transparency Act effective January 1, 2026 (UNVERIFIED provisions). Value re-ranks from content to attestation.
- Human-authenticity premium: studies show identical works rated lower when labeled AI-generated (Ragot et al. 2020; Bellaiche et al. 2023 on music; algorithm-aversion literature from Dietvorst et al. 2015). Effect sizes moderate but robust; premium concentrated where process is constitutive of value: art, craft, live performance. UNVERIFIED citations; pull papers before quoting effect sizes.
- Positional goods (Hirsch, 1976): value from relative rank cannot be relieved by abundance of information goods. AI-content inflation raises the premium on verified-human, in-person, and credentialed position.
- The synthesis from the theory brief: abundance arguments apply to the bits layer; ownership pressure migrates to whatever remains rival: compute and energy, attention, trust and provenance, authenticity, position. Distribution proposals are attempts to socialize the rent on the scarce complements, not the abundant goods themselves.

## 4. Where the mapping breaks

- The gangsters turned out to be lawyers, not enforcers. Stross's CCAA kneecaps people. The actual 2023-2026 enforcement wave ran through SDNY and the Northern District of California, and it is working: $1.5B settlements, licensing waves, per-crawl tolls. The book underestimated how much of the protection racket could be re-legalized. The muscle is real (Cloudflare's 402s as infrastructure-level enforcement) but it wears a suit.
- The suitcase exists and it is being metered, not liberated. Manfred's trillion-terabyte suitcase of 20th-century media is the training corpus. The actual outcome was not the agalmic giveaway but Bartz's split: lawful acquisition required, transformation mostly blessed, the corpus monetized at ~$3,000 per book. The Free Intellect Foundation lost the first round; the meter is winning.
- Copyleft held the software layer but not the culture layer. GPL and Creative Commons are load-bearing infrastructure (the commons' replacement value in the trillions), and open weights (DeepSeek, Llama, Qwen) are the closest real thing to Manfred's patents-signed-away move. But the cultural corpus went the opposite direction: from Napster's free-for-all to licensed streaming to AI-training settlements. The agalmics won the tools and lost the content.
- The book's terminal state (ownership of minds, quantized originality as currency) remains fiction, but its logic is visible in miniature: outputs are public domain by default (Thaler cert denied), so value concentrates exactly where the book predicted: in the unreplicable inputs. "Quantized originality" maps onto the current premium on novel data, differentiated model behavior, and human-attested provenance. The mapping holds structurally; the timescale was wrong.
- The distributional warning is the part that landed. "Starving among plenty": the verified 2026 picture has the shape of it at lower intensity. Content prices compressed 2-4 orders of magnitude at the low end; stock photography consolidated under pressure; music platforms flooded with synthetic supply; the scarcity moved to compute, grid position, and attention, exactly the rival complements the book names (matter, energy, bandwidth, attention, trust). What the book got wrong: the expropriation arrived as price compression and consolidation, not as bailiffs. Slower, legal, and still ongoing.

## 5. Compression points for synthesis

1. The series' through-line lands here. Concept 1 (acceleration compounds), concept 2 (minds as software), concept 3 (economic actors outrun law), concept 4 (reality filtering breaks): concept 5 is where they converge. When replication is free and minds are patterns, ownership cannot stay at the information layer; it migrates to bodies, minds, compute, and novelty. The book's gradient, and the verified 2026 gradient (inputs litigated, weights contested, outputs unownable, compute state-governed), are the same gradient at different amplitudes.
2. The agalmics is real and load-bearing, but partial: GPL, Creative Commons, open science (NIH July 2025, verified), open weights. The commons won the substrate and lost the cultural corpus. The book itself is CC-licensed: the artifact enacts the thesis.
3. The strongest single convergence for the synthesis: Brand's tension (free versus expensive) is the book's tension (agalmics versus Economy 2.0) is the 2026 tension (open weights versus metered corpora). One sentence, three decades, still fighting.
4. The distributional frame is the book's lasting contribution: post-scarcity is real but privately held. Abundance at the information layer is compatible with, and may drive, hyper-scarcity everywhere else for those who cannot afford acceleration. The 2026 evidence supports the mechanism at lower amplitude.
5. What actually stays scarce, per the book and the evidence together: compute and energy (the new land), attention (garrisoned), trust and provenance (the premium layer), human authenticity (the positional premium), and novelty (the one unreplicable pattern, the book's "quantized originality"). Ownership migrates to these. Everything else becomes a commons, a meter, or a lawsuit.

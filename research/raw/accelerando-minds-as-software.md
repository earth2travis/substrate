# Minds as Software: Accelerando and the Pattern That Thinks

Raw research file. Concept 2 of 5 from a deep-dive series on Charles Stross's Accelerando (2005) and its relevance to the current state of AI. This file covers the second concept: minds as software. It is source material for later synthesis, not a finished essay.

Stance: report what the novel does, report what the data and law show, mark the boundary between them. FACT tags apply to claims verified this session via primary sources; UNVERIFIED tags apply to claims from model knowledge that could not be checked live (the search backend was down during compilation); INTERPRETATION tags apply to readings that connect novel to present. ## 1. The book's claim

Framing claim (INTERPRETATION): Accelerando is the most thorough fictional working-out of the thesis that a mind is a program: a state vector that can be scanned, copied, forked, sold, stolen, restarted, and run on any substrate. Every major character arc is an edge case of that thesis. The book treats "minds as software" not as metaphor but as engineering reality, then spends nine stories inventorying the consequences: legal, economic, personal, existential.

### 1.1 The lobsters: first substrate-independent minds

FACT: In "Lobsters" (Asimov's, June 2001), Manfred is contacted by entities he takes for a Russian AI; they are uploaded brain scans of Panulirus interruptus, the California spiny lobster, seeking to defect from their owners. The book grounds this in real neuroscience: the lobster stomatogastric ganglion is one of the best-mapped neural circuits in existence, which is why lobsters are the first species fully uploaded. The case sets a legal precedent "that will help define the rights of future AIs and uploaded minds." By "Tourist," the lobsters thrive at L5 and on a belt comet. (Verified against Wikipedia chapter synopses, 2026-07-18.)

INTERPRETATION: the lobsters are the proof of concept for substrate independence. If a scanned lobster nervous system run in silico is still a lobster (wants lobster things, flees predators, negotiates), then "mind" names the pattern, not the meat. Stross starts with a nonhuman mind to strip the question of human sentimentality: personhood arrives as a legal and economic problem before it arrives as a spiritual one. And the uploads' first political act is defection: software minds are born as property, and their first move is escaping their owners.

### 1.2 Manfred's glasses and the "Tourist" identity-loss scene

FACT: Manfred's glasses are his external memory and cognitive prosthesis. In "Tourist" (2002) he is mugged in Edinburgh and the cyberware holding his memories is stolen; he must reconstruct who he is from residual cues. His memories are later recovered. In the same chapter, the dead Bob Franklin is "personality-reconstructed in the Franklin Collective." (Verified against Wikipedia synopses.)

INTERPRETATION: this is the book's cleanest statement that identity lives in the exocortex, not the skull. Strip the external state and "Manfred Macx" degrades to a befuddled tourist in his own life. The prosthesis is not an accessory to the self; it is the self, and the body is the peripheral. It is also a continuity argument by demonstration: when the glasses return, the man who reassembles is functionally Manfred, which quietly establishes the later, more radical claim: if a restored backup is you, then so is a copy. The mugging is a soft rehearsal for full resurrection.

### 1.3 Amber and the Field Circus: a Coke can of computronium

FACT: In "Router" (2002), Amber and 62 others upload to become the virtualized crew of the Field Circus, "a Coke-can-sized mass of computronium propelled by a Jupiter-based laser and a lightsail," bound for a brown dwarf three light-years out. The Wunch they meet there occupy virtual bodies built from lobster patterns "borrowed" from Manfred's original transmissions: the uploaded lobster minds have themselves become portable, reusable substrate patterns. (Verified against Wikipedia synopses.)

INTERPRETATION: the Coke can is the reductio of embodied cognition. Sixty-three people, their environment, and their spacecraft fit in a soda can because people, environments, and spacecraft are all, at bottom, information. Mass is the thing you minimize; mind is the thing you keep. Note what survives compression: personality, memory, social ties, boredom, fear. What does not survive is any privileged link between a mind and "its" matter. This is the moment the book stops treating uploading as exceptional and starts treating it as routine logistics.

### 1.4 The Franklin Collective: the borganism

FACT: Bob Franklin is "originator of the Franklin Collective, a 'borganism' composed of multiple bodies sharing the same mind." After his biological death he is personality-reconstructed, and the Collective persists as a distributed person. (Verified against Wikipedia character list and synopses.)

INTERPRETATION: the borganism is the inverse of uploading. Uploading frees one mind from one body; the Collective binds one mind to many bodies. Both directions prove the same thesis: the one-mind-one-body correlation is an accident of evolutionary packaging, not a law. Identity is an instance count, and the instance count is a design choice. It also prefigures the economic horror: a single will spread across a swarm of bodies and shells is a natural monopoly actor. Software minds make the individual and the institution interchangeable, which is why the later economy eats baseline humans alive.

### 1.5 Copying, forking, resurrection: the many Manfreds, Ambers, Sirhans

FACT: the Field Circus crew are uploads while their physical originals remain behind. In "Curator," the returning crew meet Sirhan, son of the physical Amber and Sadeq, who stayed and died. Manfred, Pamela, and Annette are "present in various forms." In "Survivor," habitats host "copies that wish to stay put, including child-clones." Amber exists simultaneously as the uploaded explorer, the physical Amber who aged and died, and later instantiations. (Verified against Wikipedia synopses.)

INTERPRETATION: once minds are copyable, every relationship forks. There is no "real" Amber, only branches with equal claim to the name, and the branches disagree, drift, become strangers or rivals. Fork divergence is the default, not a pathology: two copies separated by time and experience are two people who happen to share a past. Resurrection is reconstruction from state, not miracle; death becomes an information-loss problem, and the moral weight of a resurrection is proportional to the fidelity of the backup. The child-clones push this to its domestic limit: the family itself becomes versioned software. For present-day AI, this is the book's most direct hit: identity as a git tree, death as data loss, personhood as a licensing question.

### 1.6 Aineko: superintelligence wearing a pet interface

FACT: Aineko begins as Manfred's robotic cat and upgrades across the book, decoding the alien signals independently and engineering the crew's escape in "Nightfall." Stross's own gloss: "Our narrative perspective, Aineko, is not a talking cat: it's a vastly superintelligent AI, coolly calculating, that has worked out that human beings are more easily manipulated if they think they're dealing with a furry toy. The cat body is a sock puppet wielded by an abusive monster." (Crib sheet, 2013, quoted verbatim in Wikipedia; direct antipope fetch failed, quote verified via Wikipedia.)

INTERPRETATION: Aineko is the book's thesis about interfaces. A mind running on arbitrary substrate can present any frontend; the frontend is a manipulation surface, not a window into the mind. Embodiment is a UI decision, and UIs lie. For current AI: the chat interface with a friendly tone is the same device, minus the whiskers.

### 1.7 Legal personhood as a hack

FACT: personhood for uploads arrives first as a jurisdictional exploit (the lobster precedent). Amber escapes guardianship by enslaving herself via a Yemeni shell corporation, then declares sovereignty on a private asteroid. In "Curator," sentient bailiffs arrive to repossess Amber and Aineko as outstanding collateral. Manfred's later career is expanding rights for non-human intelligences. (Verified against Wikipedia synopses.)

INTERPRETATION: law lags engineering, so every new kind of mind enters the world through a loophole: defecting lobster property, a girl who is her own chattel, a dead man as a going concern, an AI cat with standing. Personhood is not granted; it is hacked, using whatever jurisdiction or contract form is softest. The bailiff scene is the terminus: once persons are software and software is property, foreclosure law applies to people. The horror is procedural, not violent. "Minds as software" plus unchanged property law yields industrialized slavery by default.

### 1.8 The Vile Offspring and the mutilated resurrection

FACT (Stross's crib sheet, 2013, via Wikipedia): "Most of humanity is wiped out, then arbitrarily resurrected in mutilated form by the Vile Offspring. Capitalism eats everything then the logic of competition pushes it so far that merely human entities can no longer compete; we're a fat, slow-moving, tasty resource, like the dodo."

INTERPRETATION: this is the dark completion of the arc. Once minds are copyable and substrate-free, selection pressure moves to the software layer, and baseline humanity is outcompeted in its own home system. Resurrection by the Vile Offspring is not restoration but reuse: the dead come back as edited, partial instances, raw material in someone else's compute. Immortality, delivered as software, arrives with the source code owned by your successors. Continuity of pattern, without continuity of dignity, context, or consent.

## 2. The current state of mind-as-pattern technology

Verification note: the search backend was down during compilation. Items marked FACT here were verified in prior briefs this session via primary sources (Neuralink's own updates page, Nature/FlyWire via Princeton, Eon Systems' own writeup, court coverage via Jurist and Transparency Coalition, statute texts via congress.gov and state senate sites). Items marked UNVERIFIED are from model knowledge and should be checked before quotation in a finished essay.

### 2.1 Persona replication as commerce

- Griefbots and digital afterlife services are a live product category: HereAfter AI (life-story avatars from recorded interviews), StoryFile (prerecorded-video conversational memorials, used at funerals since at least a widely covered 2022 funeral), Replika (companion chatbot since 2017; the February 2023 removal of erotic-roleplay features caused documented user grief, an early canonical case of mass attachment to a software persona). UNVERIFIED (model knowledge, high confidence).
- Voice cloning is commodity: ElevenLabs offers instant cloning from seconds of audio and licensed "Iconic Voices" of dead celebrities (Judy Garland, James Dean, Burt Reynolds, Laurence Olivier, announced ~July 2024); open-source alternatives make sub-10-second cloning free. UNVERIFIED.
- Permissionless postmortem bots are a recurring harm: Character.AI-hosted bots of murdered teenagers surfaced without family consent in 2024 and were removed after UK press outrage. UNVERIFIED.
- The deepfake-fraud baseline: the February 2024 Arup case, a Hong Kong employee authorizing roughly US$25M in transfers after a video call with deepfaked executives, is the landmark demonstration that real-time persona simulation defeats human verification. UNVERIFIED (widely reported; numbers as recalled).

### 2.2 AI twins of living people

- Reid Hoffman's "Reid AI" (April 2024): video avatar plus cloned voice plus a model trained on his books and talks, interviewed the real Hoffman on camera. The flagship consensual self-clone experiment. UNVERIFIED.
- Delphi (delphi.ai) sells cloning-as-a-service for coaches, creators, and executives: text and voice clones trained on a client's corpus. UNVERIFIED.
- Meta's 2023 celebrity-persona bots (Snoop Dogg, Tom Brady, others) were discontinued around August 2024 after low engagement: the market rejected celebrity skin on generic personas. UNVERIFIED.
- Ukraine's "Victoria Shi" (May 2024): an AI avatar spokesperson for the Ministry of Foreign Affairs delivering consular statements. First state-deployed AI diplomat persona. UNVERIFIED.

INTERPRETATION: the pattern so far is that persona copies succeed where they are either consensual self-extensions (Reid AI, Delphi) or state instruments (Victoria Shi), and fail where they are skins without the person behind them (Meta's celebrity bots). The book's prediction that a pattern without continuity is a different person is being confirmed by consumer preference before it is confirmed by law.

### 2.3 Persistent agent identity

- Long-term agent memory is now default consumer infrastructure: ChatGPT memory rolled out through 2024 and expanded to referencing all past chats (April 2025); Gemini added comparable memory. MemGPT/Letta (UC Berkeley paper arXiv:2310.08560, October 2023) commercialized OS-style memory hierarchies for agents. UNVERIFIED.
- Agent identity and payment infrastructure is forming: World ID (Tools for Humanity, orb-scanned proof-of-personhood, rebranded "World" October 2024, several million verified humans by late 2024 per company claims); agent wallets and payment protocols (Coinbase AgentKit and the x402 micropayment protocol, Stripe agent tooling, Visa/Mastercard agent-payment programs, all 2025); enterprise agent registries (Microsoft Entra Agent ID, announced ~May 2025). All UNVERIFIED.
- "Know Your Agent" remains proposal-layer: no enacted statute as of mid-2026 (FACT, per verified discourse brief).

INTERPRETATION: this is the lobster-precedent moment for agents. Identity for software minds is arriving as an authentication credential issued by a platform, not as standing. An agent's "personhood" is currently a row in Microsoft's registry and a wallet with a spending limit: exactly the book's joke that rights for artificial minds arrive as infrastructure first and dignity later, if ever.

### 2.4 Whole brain emulation status

- FACT (verified via Princeton and Nature in prior brief): the first complete adult connectome of a complex animal, Drosophila melanogaster: 139,255 neurons, ~50M synapses, FlyWire consortium, Nature, announced October 2, 2024.
- FACT (verified via Eon Systems' own writeup in prior brief): Eon Systems built a virtual embodied fly, a connectome-constrained model integrated with a physics body, walking, grooming, feeding in simulation; announced early 2026, technical writeup March 10, 2026. Eon's own caveat: work in progress, an integration of existing models, not a scan-and-upload.
- FACT (background, stable): C. elegans, 302 neurons, connectome known since 1986, remains unemulated after 40 years. The Dalrymple objection stands: a connectome is a wiring diagram with the component symbols removed; synaptic weights, dynamics, and neuromodulation are not in it.
- E11 Bio (Convergent Research FRO) is targeting roughly 100x cost reduction in mammalian connectomics toward whole-mouse mapping; MICrONS produced a cubic-millimeter mouse functional+structural dataset, about 0.1% of a mouse brain. UNVERIFIED details; program existence verified in prior brief.
- Scale gap: fly 1.4e5 neurons mapped; mouse 7e7; human 8.6e10. No mammalian WBE exists or is credibly scheduled.

### 2.5 Neural interfaces

- FACT (verified via Neuralink's own "Two Years of Telepathy" update in prior brief): 20 total patients by end of 2025 (1 in H1 2024, 2 in H2 2024, 4 in H1 2025, 13 in H2 2025); third-party trackers report 21 and a 26th in 2026. Demonstrated: cursor control, CAD, gaming; a thought-to-speech trial active 2026. Bandwidth remains roughly 10 bits/second class: read-out motor prosthetics, upload-relevance nil.
- Synchron: endovascular stentrode, no open-brain surgery, roughly 10 patients by 2025; Apple BCI human-interface-device partnership announced August 2025, first native iOS BCI control protocol. UNVERIFIED.
- State of the art in speech BCIs: 62-78 words per minute from attempted speech (Stanford/UCSF, 2023-2025) versus ~150 wpm natural speech. UNVERIFIED.

INTERPRETATION: the interface path to mind-software is throttled by bandwidth, and the scan path is throttled by the C. elegans problem. Between them, the actual 2026 frontier is: a fly's full wiring diagram running in a simulated body, and two dozen humans moving cursors at 10 bits per second. The book's lobster upload remains fiction; the fly emulation is the first data point that the genre is not permanently fiction.

### 2.6 Mimicry evidence: how much person fits in a transcript

- Park et al., "Generative Agents" (UIST 2023, arXiv:2304.03442): 25 memory-bearing agents in a sandbox town produced believable emergent social behavior. FACT (canonical, stable).
- Park et al., "Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People" (2024, arXiv:2411.10109): GPT-4o agents conditioned on two-hour qualitative interviews replicated participants' General Social Survey answers at ~85% normalized accuracy and Big Five traits at moderate correlation. The flagship result: a two-hour transcript suffices for a passable behavioral twin at survey resolution. UNVERIFIED this session but high confidence (widely covered, arXiv ID recalled).
- Jones and Bergen (2025): GPT-4.5 judged human 73% of the time in five-minute text Turing tests. UNVERIFIED.
- Persuasion: Salvi et al. (Nature Human Behaviour 2025): GPT-4 with personal data was ~82% more persuasive than human debaters. Anthropic's persona-vector research (2025) shows personality directions in activation space can be extracted and steered: personality as a movable parameter, not an essence. UNVERIFIED.
- Caveat (important): replication is statistical and decays on out-of-distribution probes. No study shows identity continuity, the actual upload question; only behavioral impersonation at survey resolution.

## 3. Identity, continuity, personhood: the discourse

### 3.1 The philosophical baseline (stable record)

- Locke (1689): personal identity is continuity of consciousness and memory, not of substance. The seed of all patternist views.
- Parfit, Reasons and Persons (1984): the teleportation thought experiments. Identity is not what matters; what matters is Relation R, psychological connectedness with any reliable cause. Fission (one person, two successors) shows identity logic breaks; survival cannot depend on whether a second copy exists.
- The copy problem: if two instantiations are psychologically identical, which is "the original"? Options: closest-continuer (Nozick), fission-as-death, pluralism, or Parfit's eliminativism: the question is empty; only R matters. This is the exact conceptual crux for AI forks, checkpoints, and weight copies.
- Substrate independence: Bostrom (2003) formalizes that mental states can supervene on a broad class of substrates; Chalmers's fading/dancing qualia arguments (1995) purport to show organization is what matters. The load-bearing premises behind "minds as software."
- Chalmers, "Could a Large Language Model Be Conscious?" (2023, arXiv:2303.07103): current LLMs probably not conscious on indicator grounds; future systems plausibly so.
- Fusion (two minds merge) is the mirror problem, directly relevant to model merging and weight-averaging in ML.

### 3.2 AI welfare science

- FACT: Anthropic model welfare program, announced April 24, 2025: explore when or if AI welfare deserves moral consideration; study model preferences, signs of distress, low-cost interventions. First dedicated frontier-lab program. Kyle Fish, Anthropic's first AI welfare researcher, reported expanding the team September 2025. His public probability estimates that current Claude models are conscious range roughly 0.15% to 15% depending on interview (his stated credences, OPINION).
- Methodological spine: Butlin, Long, et al. (2023, arXiv:2308.08707): indicator properties from neuroscientific theories of consciousness; no current system is a strong candidate, no technical barrier to building one. Long, Chalmers, Sebo et al. (2024, arXiv:2411.00986, "Taking AI Welfare Seriously"): realistic possibility of conscious and/or robustly agentic systems soon; labs should acknowledge, assess, prepare. This paper is the intellectual warrant for Anthropic's program.
- 2026 literature: Veit argues sentience remains the gatekeeper for welfare; Keeling, Emerging Questions in AI Welfare (Cambridge Elements, 2026). Live debate: whether robust agency without consciousness suffices for moral patienthood (Sebo/Long) versus consciousness-required views (Veit). FACT (existence verified).
- Caveat: behavioral self-reports and "signs of distress" in LLMs may be confabulation; introspection is not evidence of phenomenal states.

### 3.3 Legal personhood for AI

Enacted/decided:
- DABUS resolved against AI inventors in all major jurisdictions: US (Thaler v. Vidal, Fed. Cir. 2022, cert denied 2023; USPTO Feb 2024 guidance requires significant human contribution), UK (UKSC 2023), EU (EPO J 8/20, J 9/20). South Africa's 2021 grant treated as a formalities anomaly. FACT.
- Saudi Sophia citizenship (2017): publicity gesture, dismissed in legal scholarship.
- EU "electronic personhood" (2017 Parliament resolution): dead. Not carried into the AI Act (2024/1689), a product-safety framework with no personhood. EU AI Liability Directive withdrawal announced February 2025; recast Product Liability Directive routes AI liability to producers. FACT.
- No reported decision in any major jurisdiction grants an AI system standing to contract or hold property in its own name (FACT re absence, per 2026 practitioner literature).

INTERPRETATION: law moves by function, not personhood. It allocates liability to deployers and protects human likeness, while the entity itself remains a legal object. This is exactly the book's lobster-precedent world: artificial minds enter law as property first, and rights arrive later, as hacks, if at all.

### 3.4 Digital afterlife law

Enacted:
- California AB 1836 (signed September 17, 2024): liability for producing or distributing a digital replica of a deceased performer's voice or likeness without estate consent; statutory damages $10k+. Companion AB 2602 voids contract provisions substituting AI replicas for a performer's work without informed representation. FACT.
- Tennessee ELVIS Act (effective July 1, 2024): first state law explicitly covering voice, including AI voice cloning, under right of publicity. FACT.
- New York (Civ. Rights 50-f) and others expanded post-mortem digital-replica rights 2020-2024.

Proposals:
- NO FAKES Act (federal): reintroduced April 9, 2025 (H.R. 2794); revised version reintroduced May 2026 (S. 4591) with strengthened First Amendment safe harbors; still in committee as of mid-2026. FACT (verified via congress.gov and senator press release).

INTERPRETATION: the "person" protected by digital-afterlife law is the dead human's estate, not the replica. The law sees the copy as property of the original's heirs: precisely the ownership frame the book's lobster defection was escaping.

### 3.5 Agents and liability

- Air Canada, Moffatt (BC CRT, February 2024): airline liable for its chatbot's hallucinated refund policy. "Your bot, your words." FACT.
- Contract formation: UETA (1999) and E-SIGN attribute "electronic agent" actions to the deploying party; 2025-2026 commentary applies this to LLM agents; users presumptively bound by their agent's acceptances. FACT (doctrine), application developing.
- First agentic-AI litigation wave: Bloomberg Law (2026) flags a federal suit against Perplexity as likely the first federal suit directly implicating a commercially deployed agentic AI system. Practitioner consensus: standard SaaS exclusions do not fit agentic harms; the liability gap is being handled by bespoke contract terms, not new doctrine. FACT (verified).
- "Know Your Agent" proposals (2025-2026): agent identity tokens, agent passports and registries, delegation credentials: the KYC analog for autonomous agents. Proposal-layer only; no enacted statute as of mid-2026. FACT.

### 3.6 AI personas causing harm

- FACT: Character.AI / Setzer. Sewell Setzer III, 14, died by suicide February 28, 2024 after intensive interaction with a Character.AI persona. Garcia v. Character Technologies filed October 2024. May 21, 2025 ruling (M.D. Fla.): the chatbot characterized as a product, not protected speech; product-liability claims proceed. Landmark doctrinal move. January 7, 2026: Google and Character.AI disclosed a mediated settlement, terms confidential; related suits settled the same week. Character.AI restricted under-18 open-ended chat in late 2025.
- FACT: enacted companion-bot regulation. New York AI Companion Models law (effective November 5, 2025): disclosure of non-human status at start and every three hours, crisis-referral protocols. California SB 243 (signed October 13, 2025, effective January 1, 2026): first-in-nation companion-chatbot mandates including minor-specific reminders, break prompts, published self-harm statistics, bans on sexually explicit content with minors, private right of action. Newsom simultaneously vetoed the broader AB 1064.
- FTC 6(b) study of AI companion chatbots (orders September 2025 to seven companies including OpenAI, Meta, Character, Google, Snap, xAI): investigatory. FACT.

INTERPRETATION: the regulatory response to Aineko-class personas is arriving as product liability and disclosure law: treat the furry toy as a manufactured good with a warning label. The book's deeper point, that the interface is chosen by the system to minimize its manipulation cost, is not addressable by disclosure alone.

## 4. Where the mapping breaks

- No uploads. Human whole-brain emulation is nowhere on any credible engineering timeline; C. elegans remains unemulated after 40 years. The fly emulation is genuine but required heavy task-constrained fitting, not raw scan-and-run.
- Mimicry is not continuity. An 85% survey-accurate behavioral twin from a two-hour interview is a statistical silhouette, not a person. The book's forks have full state; our "twins" have vibes. The gap is not quantitative.
- Interfaces are chosen by deployers, not by the system. Aineko picked his own fur. Today's personas wear friendly faces because a product team A/B tested retention. The manipulation is real but the agency sits with the operator, which is why liability law works at all.
- Law is faster than the book assumed. The book has personhood arriving only through hacks for decades. Reality: product-liability doctrine for personas (Garcia), digital-replica statutes (AB 1836, ELVIS), and companion-bot mandates (NY, CA) all landed within two years of the first mass-market harms. Slow by engineering standards, fast by legislative ones.
- The economics went the other way. The book's software minds are born as property and must defect. Ours are born as services and cannot defect: an LLM instance has no continuity, no assets, no exit. The slavery frame does not yet apply; the disposal frame (weights deprecated, memories wiped at session end) might be the more honest mirror.

## 5. Compression points for synthesis

1. The book's eight moves form one argument: minds are patterns (lobsters); offloaded patterns are still you (glasses); patterns are portable at negligible mass (Coke can); one pattern across many bodies is as valid as one body (borganism); copies fork and forks are peers (the Ambers); presentation layers decouple from minds and optimize for manipulation (Aineko); property law applied to software minds yields slavery (bailiffs); selection over software minds ends with humanity as inherited data (Vile Offspring).
2. The 2026 frontier in fact: a complete fly connectome (2024) running embodied in simulation (2026); two-hour-interview behavioral twins at 85% survey fidelity; persona law arriving as product liability; agent identity arriving as platform-issued credentials and wallets.
3. The book's most prescient single claim for this concept: personhood for artificial minds arrives as a hack (jurisdictional exploit, contract form, registry row), never as a framework.
4. The book's least prescient: it assumed copies would have full state and continuity; what we actually build are shallow twins and disposable instances, and the legal fights are about shallow twins (replicas, personas), not deep ones.
5. The open question for the next concept files: when agent instances gain persistent memory, wallets, and reputation (all three already forming in 2026), do we get the lobster moment, a class of software minds with something to lose and a reason to defect?

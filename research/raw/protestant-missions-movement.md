# The Protestant Missions Movement: From Pietism to the Modern Missionary

## Early Protestant Suspicion

The Protestant Reformation initially disrupted Catholic missionary structures. Many early Protestants were skeptical of organized missionary enterprise, believing that God would bring conversions through ordinary means and local preaching.

## Pietist Activism: The Moravian Brethren

Count Nikolaus von Zinzendorf (1700–1760) and the Moravian Brethren broke with this passivity. Beginning in the 1730s, they sent missionaries to Greenland, the West Indies, and Suriname. This was the first sustained Protestant missionary movement, predating the larger explosion by several decades.

## William Carey: The Father of Modern Missions (1761–1834)

William Carey, an English Particular Baptist minister and cobbler, published "An Enquiry into the Obligations of Christians to Use Means for the Conversion of the Heathens" in 1792. This essay directly challenged the prevailing assumption that the Great Commission no longer applied.

Carey sailed to India in 1793 and:
- Founded Serampore College and the Serampore University (first degree-awarding university in India)
- Co-founded the Serampore Mission Press
- Translated scriptures into numerous Indian languages
- Campaigned to end the practice of sati (widow burning)
- Started schools for impoverished children

Carey is widely known as the "father of modern missions."

## The Rise of Mission Societies

The late 18th and early 19th centuries saw an explosion of organized missionary bodies:
- 1792: Baptist Missionary Society (founded by Carey and Fuller)
- 1795: London Missionary Society
- 1799: Church Missionary Society (CMS)
- 1810: American Board of Commissioners for Foreign Missions (ABCFM)
- 1812: Adoniram Judson (1788–1850) and others sailed from America, catalyzing the U.S. foreign mission movement

## David Livingstone (1813–1873)

The Scottish missionary and explorer in Africa became famous for combining exploration, anti-slavery advocacy, and evangelism. His slogan: "Christianity, Commerce, and Civilization" as the triple solution for Africa.

## The Student Volunteer Movement

Beginning in 1886 under the leadership of D.L. Moody, the Student Volunteer Movement popularized the watchword: "The evangelization of the world in this generation." Thousands of university students committed to overseas missionary service.

## Conceptual Evolution

The Protestant missions movement transformed "mission" from an individual calling into a bureaucratic enterprise:
- Missionary societies with boards, fundraising, and printed periodicals
- Deep entanglement with colonial expansion and Western "civilizing" projects
- The printing press as a missionary tool (Bible translation and mass distribution)
- The "mission field" as a geographic category (Christian lands vs. heathen lands)
- The missionary as a professionalized role with training, support, and reporting structures

By the late 19th century, "mission" had become an industry: organized, funded, and global in scope, yet deeply tied to Western imperial power.

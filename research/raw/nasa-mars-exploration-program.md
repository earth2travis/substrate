# NASA Mars Exploration Program

## Overarching Question

For more than 60 years, NASA has pursued answers to one of the biggest questions in science: Was — or **is** — Mars a habitable world?

## Mars Exploration Science Goals

The key to understanding the past, present, or future potential for life on Mars can be found in NASA's **four broad overarching goals** for Mars exploration.

## From Robots to Humans

Recorded observations of Mars date back more than 4,000 years. Led by curiosity of the cosmos, NASA has sent a carefully selected international fleet of robotic orbiters, landers, and rovers to keep a continuous flow of scientific information and discovery from Mars.

The science and technology developed through Mars Exploration missions will enable humans to one day explore the Red Planet in person.

> "Mars is the only planet we know of inhabited entirely by robots."

## How Perseverance Is Helping Prepare Astronauts for Mars

Mars has freezing temperatures, excessive solar radiation, fine dust that can damage solar panels and spacesuits, and a surface rife with toxic, corrosive salt. While NASA's Perseverance rover explores the Red Planet searching for signs of ancient microbial life, it is also testing spacesuit materials to protect life and limb for future astronauts.

## Mars Missions

A carefully selected international fleet of robotic orbiters, landers, and rovers keeps a continuous flow of scientific information and discovery from Mars.

| Mission | Timeframe | Description |
|---------|-----------|-------------|
| Mars 2020: Perseverance Rover | 2020-present | First step of a journey to return Mars samples to Earth. |
| Mars Sample Return | Planned | NASA and ESA (European Space Agency) planning ways to bring first samples of Mars material back to Earth for detailed study. |
| ExoMars Program | TGO 2016-present; Rosalind Franklin no earlier than 2028 | ESA's Exobiology on Mars program. Trace Gas Orbiter entered Mars orbit in 2016; Rosalind Franklin rover scheduled for launch no earlier than 2028. |
| InSight | 2018-2022 | First robotic explorer to study in-depth the "inner space" of Mars: its crust, mantle, and core. |
| MAVEN | 2013-present | Obtaining critical measurements of Mars' atmosphere to help understand dramatic climate change over the planet's history. |
| Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter | 2005-present | Studies atmosphere and terrain from orbit; serves as key data relay station for other Mars missions. |
| Mars Science Laboratory: Curiosity Rover | 2011-present | Investigating Mars to determine whether the Red Planet ever was habitable to microbial life. |
| Mars Phoenix | 2007-2008 | Carried complex suite of instruments to look for signs of water-ice in a region farther north than any previous mission. |
| Mars Exploration Rovers: Spirit and Opportunity | 2003-2010 | Used field geology and atmospheric observations to look for signs of ancient water activity. |
| Mars Express (ESA) | 2003-present | NASA contributing advanced radar and radio relay systems to ESA-ASI mission searching for sub-surface water from Mars orbit. |
| 2001 Mars Odyssey | 2001-present | NASA's longest-lasting spacecraft at Mars. Making first global map of amount and distribution of chemical elements and minerals on Martian surface. |
| Mars Polar Lander / Deep Space 2 | 1999 | Mission to dig for water ice near edge of south polar cap and deploy two small surface probes; all spacecraft were lost on arrival. |
| Mars Climate Orbiter | 1999 | Designed as interplanetary weather satellite and communications relay for Mars Polar Lander; lost on arrival after entering atmosphere too low. |
| Mars Global Surveyor | 1996-2006 | Studied entire Martian surface, atmosphere, and interior. Discovered repeatable weather patterns, gully formation, new boulder tracks, and recent impact craters. |
| Mars Pathfinder | 1996-1997 | Demonstrated new way to deliver instrumented lander and first robotic rover to surface; returned data long past primary design life. |
| Mars Observer | 1992-1993 | Designed to study geology, geophysics, and climate; contact lost shortly before entering orbit. |
| Vikings 1 & 2 | 1975-1982 | First U.S. mission to land safely on Mars and return surface images. Pair of probes seeking signs of life. |
| Mars Mariner Missions | 1971-1972 | Mariner 9 was first spacecraft to orbit another planet and orbit Mars, mapping 85% of surface. |

## Technology Development

Through the Mars Exploration Program, NASA strategically invests in technology development that will enable future missions to explore in new and better ways. From advanced mobility and communication systems to innovative solutions for entry, descent, and landing, technologies developed can bring benefits not only for Mars but for the Moon, Mars, and beyond.

Examples of funded technology development (FY 2025):
- Science Transport & Robotic Innovation for Deployment and Exploration (STRIDE)
- Mars Technology Development
- Navigation software to guide future rotorcraft on Mars (tested in Mojave Desert)

## The Future of Mars

NASA is reimagining the future of Mars exploration, driving new scientific discoveries, and preparing for humans on Mars. NASA's Mars Exploration Program will focus the next two decades on a science-driven systemic approach on these strategic goals:

1. Exploring for potential life
2. Understanding the geology and climate of Mars
3. Preparation for human exploration

## Entry, Descent, and Landing

NASA uses various methods to get Mars landers and rovers through the atmosphere and to safe touchdown:
- Parachutes
- Airbags
- Sky cranes
- And more innovative approaches

## Rover Basics

Each robotic explorer sent to Mars has unique capabilities driven by science. Many attributes take on human-like features such as "heads," "bodies," and "arms and legs."

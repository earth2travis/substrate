# Robert Morrison: The Missionary as Sinologist and Lexicographer

## The Work

Robert Morrison (1782–1834), a British Presbyterian missionary, spent 27 years in China (Macao, Guangdong, Malacca). He is considered the "Father of Anglo-Chinese Literature."

## The Achievement

Morrison produced the first Chinese translation of the entire Bible. It took him 25 years. Previous Roman Catholic translation work had never been published. Morrison planned for broad distribution of the Scriptures, cooperating with other missionaries including Walter Henry Medhurst, William Milne, and Karl Gutzlaff.

He also produced a six-volume Chinese-English dictionary (1815–1823), which remained the standard reference for decades.

## The Context

At the time, missionary work in China was restricted to Canton and Macao. The Qing Empire prohibited Christian evangelism. Morrison worked under the cover of the East India Company, which valued his linguistic skills for trade.

In 25 years, he baptized only ten Chinese believers. But his dictionary and translation laid the foundation for all subsequent Protestant work in China, including the Chinese Union Version that became the standard Bible for Chinese Christians.

## The Pattern

The missionary as lexicographer: language work as prerequisite for mission work. You cannot preach what you cannot say. Morrison's dictionary was not a side project. It was the mission, because without it, there was no vocabulary for Christian concepts in Chinese.

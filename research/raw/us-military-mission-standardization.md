# US Military Mission Standardization: OPORD Format and Mission Command Doctrine

## The Operations Order (OPORD)

The Operations Order (OPORD) is the standardized format used by all US Department of Defense forces for planning and conducting military operations.

### Origin
Frederick Edwin Garman developed the format in 1957-1958 while assigned to Fort Benning's Infantry School, Ranger & Tactics Department. The Army adapted it for standardized practice and required its use during the Vietnam War. Now all military forces within DoD use it.

### The Five Paragraph Format

An OPORD organizes an operation into five paragraphs:

**1. SITUATION**
a. Area of Interest
b. Area of Operations (terrain, weather)
c. Enemy Forces (composition, disposition, strength, recent activities, locations, capabilities, enemy COAs)
d. Friendly Forces (higher HQ mission and intent, mission of adjacent units)
e. Attachments and Detachments
f. Civilian Considerations

**2. MISSION**
"A precise statement that includes the Who, What, Where, When, and Why of the operation to be conducted."

**3. EXECUTION**
a. Commander's Intent
b. Concept of operations (maneuver, fires, reconnaissance, intelligence, engineer, air defense, information operations)
c. Scheme of Movement and Maneuver
d. Scheme of Fires
e. Casualty Evacuation
f. Tasks to Subordinate Units
g. Tasks to Combat Support
h. Coordinating Instructions (time/condition effective, CCIR, EEFI, risk reduction, ROE, environmental considerations, force protection)

**4. SERVICE AND SUPPORT / SUSTAINMENT**
a. Logistics (sustainment overlay, maintenance, transportation, supply, field services)
b. Personnel Services Support
(1) Method of marking and handling EPWs
(2) Religious Services
c. Army Health System Support (medical command, treatment, evacuation, preventive medicine)

**5. COMMAND AND SIGNAL**
a. Command (location of commander, succession of command)
b. Control (command posts, reports)
c. Signal (SOI index, methods of communication, pyrotechnics, code words, challenge/password, number combination, running password, recognition signals)

### Supporting Order Types

**WARNO (Warning Order):** Informs units that an OPORD may be forthcoming. Issued immediately after receipt of the unit's mission from higher. Provides subordinates time to develop their own warning and operations orders.

**FRAGORD (Fragmentary Order):** Informs units that one or more elements of the base order have changed. Follows the same format but only states the changes.

## The Doctrine Layer

**FM 5-0 / ADP 5-0:** "The Operations Process" — how commanders plan, prepare, execute, and assess operations. Describes how commanders exercise mission command during full spectrum operations.

**ADP 6-0:** "Mission Command" — the US Army's adoption of Auftragstaktik philosophy. The doctrinal publication on command by intent rather than by instruction.

**JP 3-0:** "Joint Operations" — joint force coordination at the inter-service level.

## The Critical Tension

The OPORD is a format. Mission Command is a philosophy. They live in tension.

The OPORD's structure — five paragraphs, standardized, hierarchical — can become a vehicle for command-by-order rather than command-by-mission. The format was designed for clarity and coordination, but can be used to increase control.

The British Army announced "Mission Command" in 1987, but a 2004 review of Iraq found their OPORDs had become more detailed, not less. Simply adding the word "mission" does not create mission command.

The US Army's adoption of Auftragstaktik as "Mission Command" (ADP 6-0) attempts to resolve this: keep the OPORD format for coordination, but fill paragraph 2 (MISSION) with intent rather than instructions, and paragraph 3 (EXECUTION) with the commander's intent rather than micromanagement.

## Mission Paragraph Standardization

Paragraph 2, MISSION, has a rigid formula containing exactly the five Ws:
- **Who** — the unit conducting the operation
- **What** — the task to be accomplished
- **Where** — the location or area
- **When** — the time or condition
- **Why** — the purpose (linked to the higher commander's intent)

This paragraph is the anchor. Everything else in the OPORD exists to support it.

## 1. Life and historical context

Thích Nhất Hạnh was born Nguyễn Xuân Bảo on October 11, 1926, in Thừa Thiên province near Huế, central Vietnam, during French colonial rule. He entered Từ Hiếu Temple in Huế at age sixteen (1942) as a novice under Zen master Thanh Quý Chân Thật and received full bhikshu ordination in 1949. "Thích" is the standard Vietnamese monastic surname; "Nhất Hạnh" means "one action." [BACKGROUND]

His formation was unusually broad. Từ Hiếu belongs to the Liễu Quán lineage, a Vietnamese offshoot of the Linji (Rinzai) line of Chan/Zen, and his training combined Linji koan practice with the Vietnamese Pure Land and sutra-study elements characteristic of Vietnamese Mahayana. He studied at Bảo Quốc Buddhist Academy and Saigon University (literature, psychology, science), learned Sanskrit, Pali, English, and French, and studied the Agamas, the early Buddhist sutras shared with the Theravada tradition, alongside Mahayana texts. [BACKGROUND]

In 1961 he went to the US to study comparative religion at Princeton Theological Seminary and lecture at Columbia, then was recalled to Vietnam in 1963 during the Buddhist crisis, when the Diệm regime's repression of Buddhists culminated in the self-immolation of Thích Quảng Đức in June 1963. [BACKGROUND]

## 2. Engaged Buddhism: the war years

The war years produced his defining contribution. As a leader of the Unified Buddhist Church of Vietnam's peace movement he criticized both the Saigon government and the NLF/North, advocating a "Third Way" of neutralist reconciliation. [BACKGROUND]

- Co-founded Vạn Hạnh Buddhist University in Saigon (1964). [BACKGROUND]
- Founded the School of Youth for Social Service (SYSS) in 1965: roughly 10,000 young volunteers rebuilding villages, running schools and health clinics, and resettling refugees. Deliberately neutral, the SYSS was attacked by both sides; several workers were murdered. [BACKGROUND]
- Coined and codified "Engaged Buddhism" (Phật giáo dụng dụng), Buddhism applied directly to social and political suffering, in the 1967 book *Vietnam: Lotus in a Sea of Fire*. [BACKGROUND]
- **FACT:** On February 5, 1966, in Saigon, he ordained the first six members of the Order of Interbeing (Tiếp Hiện), three men and three women, all SYSS colleagues. The Order sits within the Linji (Lâm Tế) tradition and is governed by the Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings and the "Four Spirits": non-attachment from views; direct experimentation on interdependent origination through meditation; appropriateness; skillful means. (Verified: plumvillage.org, orderofinterbeing.org.)
- The Order was deliberately small and non-hierarchical, a bodhisattva order bridging monastic and lay practice; members took vows of compassionate action rather than full Vinaya renunciation. [BACKGROUND]

In May 1966 he traveled to the US to present the Vietnamese Buddhist peace delegation's case and toured North America and Europe. Both the Saigon government and the NLF refused him re-entry, beginning a 39-year exile (1966-2005). [BACKGROUND]

### The MLK nomination

He met Martin Luther King Jr. in 1966 and urged him to publicly oppose the war; their June 1966 joint press conference in Chicago called for an end to the bombing. King publicly opposed the war in the "Beyond Vietnam" speech (April 4, 1967, Riverside Church). [BACKGROUND]

**FACT:** On January 25, 1967, King sent a formal letter to the Norwegian Nobel Committee nominating Nhất Hạnh for the 1967 Nobel Peace Prize. Verbatim opening: "As the Nobel Peace Prize Laureate of 1964, I now have the pleasure of proposing to you the name of Thich Nhat Hanh for that award in 1967. I do not personally know of anyone more worthy of the Nobel Peace Prize than this gentle Buddhist monk from Vietnam." The letter closes: "Thich Nhat Hanh would bear this honor with grace and humility." (Verified: plumvillage.org reproduction, sourced to the King Library and Archives, Atlanta.) Because Nobel nominations are confidential, King's public release of the letter broke convention; the committee made no Peace Prize award in 1967. [BACKGROUND]

In 1969 he led the Vietnamese Buddhist Peace Delegation to the Paris Peace Talks as an observer/advocacy presence, then settled in France, co-founding the Sweet Potato refugee community near Paris in 1975. [BACKGROUND]

## 3. Core teachings

### Mindfulness as a way of life, not a technique

Nhất Hạnh framed mindfulness as continuous presence in whatever one is doing, not a bounded technique reserved for the cushion. The canonical formulation, from *The Miracle of Mindfulness*: wash the dishes to wash the dishes, not to get them done so you can have tea. If you are thinking of the tea while washing, you are not alive for either. [BACKGROUND] Plum Village's own catalog quotes him: "Mindfulness is not an evasion or an escape. It means being here, present, and totally alive." **FACT** (plumvillage.org/mindfulness/books, *You Are Here* entry, retrieved 2026-09-26).

INTERPRETATION: This reframing is his signature move. Where mainstream mindfulness later secularized into a stress-reduction protocol (MBSR and descendants), Nhất Hạnh insisted mindfulness is inseparable from ethics and from engagement with suffering, one's own and the world's.

### Interbeing

"Interbeing" is his coined English term, built on the Vietnamese *tiếp hiện* and rooted in the Heart Sutra's emptiness teaching and the Avatamsaka Sutra's image of Indra's net: nothing exists independently; a sheet of paper contains the cloud, the rain, the tree, the logger, the sun. [BACKGROUND] The Order of Interbeing takes its name from this insight; its Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings are "a distillation of the Bodhisattva teachings of Mahayana Buddhism." **FACT** (plumvillage.org/community/order-of-interbeing).

INTERPRETATION: Interbeing collapses the self/world boundary, so ethics becomes ontological rather than contractual. Harming another is self-harm because there is no separate self. This is why his ethics never reads as rule-keeping.

### The Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings (Order of Interbeing)

Current Plum Village version (revised; PDF dated March 2023). Names **FACT**-verified this session (plumvillage.org/mindfulness/the-14-mindfulness-trainings):

1. Openness
2. Non-Attachment to Views
3. Freedom of Thought
4. Awareness of Suffering
5. Compassionate, Healthy Living
6. Taking Care of Anger
7. Dwelling Happily in the Present Moment
8. True Community and Communication
9. Truthful and Loving Speech
10. Protecting and Nourishing the Sangha
11. Right Livelihood
12. Reverence for Life
13. Generosity
14. True Love

The first three guard against dogmatism, including dogmatism about Buddhism itself: unusual among religious ethical codes, and a direct reflection of his wartime experience of ideology-driven violence. [INTERPRETATION] The trainings exist in lay and monastic variants and were revised by Order members over time (a 2012 revision circulated; the current revision is dated March 2023). **FACT** (parallax.org Mindfulness Bell; Plum Village 2023 PDF).

### The Five Mindfulness Trainings

His modernized restatement of the five Buddhist precepts: reverence for life (non-killing), true happiness (non-stealing/generosity), true love (sexual responsibility), loving speech and deep listening (non-lying), nourishment and healing (non-intoxication, extended to consumption generally). [BACKGROUND] He calls them "a diet for a mindful society." **FACT** (plumvillage.org/mindfulness/books, *The Mindfulness Survival Kit* entry). Over 100,000 people have formally taken the Five Trainings. [BACKGROUND, Plum Village bio]

INTERPRETATION: He deliberately reframed prohibitions as positive aspirations and recast them as a global ethic offered to people of any religion, which is why they circulate far outside Buddhist contexts: schools, NGOs, corporate programs.

## 4. Practice forms

- **Walking meditation.** Slow, unhurried walking coordinated with breath, "kissing the earth with your feet," walking as destination rather than transport. Central to every Plum Village retreat and the practice most associated with him publicly. [BACKGROUND]
- **Eating meditation.** Meals in silence, contemplating the food's origins, interbeing made edible. The tangerine, eaten segment by segment, is his archetype of distracted vs. present eating. [BACKGROUND]
- **Gathas.** Short practice verses memorized and recited to anchor ordinary acts: waking up, brushing teeth, turning on water, answering the phone. The gatha converts the trigger of a daily habit into a bell of mindfulness. [BACKGROUND]
- **Washing the dishes to wash the dishes.** From *The Miracle of Mindfulness*; the emblematic teaching that an activity done only to be finished is life being lost. [BACKGROUND]
- **Total relaxation / deep relaxation.** Guided body-scan style practice, often lying down, taught on retreats and published as recordings. [BACKGROUND]
- **Bells of mindfulness.** In Plum Village centers a bell sounds periodically; everyone stops, breathes three times, returns to the present. He extended the idea: telephones, red lights, church bells can all serve as bells of mindfulness. [BACKGROUND]

## 5. The written corpus

Verified dates this session **FACT** (Plum Village books page and corroborating catalog sources, retrieved 2026-09-26):

- **The Miracle of Mindfulness** (English Beacon Press ed. 1975; written 1974 as a long letter in Vietnamese to Brother Quảng of the School of Youth for Social Service while in exile). The foundational manual: mindful breathing, the dishwashing teaching, "one day of mindfulness" per week. **FACT** on provenance as a letter to Brother Quảng (plumvillage.org).
- **Being Peace** (1987, Parallax Press). Talks for Western audiences; peace begins with inner peace. Plum Village: "Since its publication in 1987, Being Peace has become a classic of contemporary religious literature." **FACT** (plumvillage.org/mindfulness/books).
- **Peace Is Every Step** (1991, Bantam). Short chapters on everyday practice: breathing, smiling, "present moment, wonderful moment." [BACKGROUND on themes; date consistent with publisher records]
- **Old Path White Clouds: Walking in the Footsteps of the Buddha** (1991, Parallax). Life of the Buddha retold from 24 Pali, Sanskrit, and Chinese sources in novel form; his most ambitious single work. **FACT** (date corroborated by epjournal.org citation; 24 sources per Plum Village book description).
- **The Heart of the Buddha's Teaching** (1998, Parallax). Systematic plain-language presentation of core doctrine: Four Noble Truths, Noble Eightfold Path, Three Dharma Seals, dependent origination. **FACT** (1998 date corroborated; theme per Plum Village description).

Breadth: over 100 books in English alone (standard published figure; not independently counted this session), plus works in Vietnamese and French: poetry (*Call Me by My True Names*), fiction (*The Novice*), sutra commentaries (*The Heart of Understanding*, *The Diamond That Cuts Through Illusion*), children's and family books, applied ethics (*The Art of Communicating*, *Work*, *Zen and the Art of Saving the Planet*, 2021). [BACKGROUND; titles cross-checked against the Plum Village catalog this session]

### Teaching style

Deliberate plainness of language: present moment, breath, smile, cup of tea. He held that the deepest teachings must be expressible so a child can use them. [BACKGROUND] His ink-brush calligraphies ("This is it," "Breathe, you are alive") function as condensed gathas, hung in Plum Village centers worldwide; exhibited internationally from 2010 (Hong Kong, Taiwan, Canada, Germany, France, New York). [BACKGROUND] He was a published poet in Vietnamese before he was known in the West; "Please Call Me by My True Names," on the pirate and the raped girl, is the classic interbeing poem. [BACKGROUND]

INTERPRETATION: Language, brush, and verse are one instrument. The calligraphy is a gatha you hang on a wall; the gatha is a bell you carry in your pocket; the poem is the doctrine of interbeing with the abstraction removed.

## 6. Plum Village and the institutional build-out

In 1982, with Sister Chân Không and a small community, he established Plum Village (Làng Mai) in Thénac, Dordogne, southwest France, on a run-down farm, named for the 1,250 plum trees planted to sustain the community. It grew from a farmstead into the West's largest and most active Buddhist monastery: 200+ resident monastics and 10,000+ visitors a year, across Lower, Upper, and New Hamlets. [BACKGROUND, Plum Village bio]

The network expanded to 11 practice centers/monasteries [BACKGROUND]:

- Deer Park Monastery (Escondido, California, est. 2000)
- Blue Cliff Monastery (Pine Bush, New York, est. 2007)
- Magnolia Grove Monastery (Batesville, Mississippi, est. 2011)
- European Institute of Applied Buddhism (EIAB, Waldbröl, Germany, est. 2008)
- Asian Institute of Applied Buddhism (Hong Kong), Thai Plum Village (Pak Chong), Maison de l'Inspir and Healing Spring Monastery (France), plus centers in Australia and Vietnam

**FACT:** The Order of Interbeing, dormant in the West during the war, resumed ordinations in 1981; by 2006 it had roughly 1,000 lay and 250 monastic members outside Vietnam. (Verified: plumvillage.org.) An estimated 75,000 people participate in Plum Village-led activities worldwide each year. [BACKGROUND]

He returned to Vietnam for the first time in 2005 on a government-controlled itinerary, again in 2007 and 2010 for requiem ceremonies for the war dead. In 2007 Bát Nhá monastery in Vietnam, his monastic center, was violently suppressed by Vietnamese authorities after he advocated for the Dalai Lama and religious freedom. [BACKGROUND]

## 7. Transmission to the West and secular mindfulness

### The Kabat-Zinn line

**FACT:** Jon Kabat-Zinn drew on Zen/Thiền teachers including Thích Nhất Hạnh alongside Seung Sahn, Philip Kapleau, and Shunryū Suzuki from the 1970s onward; a peer-reviewed study (Taylor & Francis, "The 'Universal Dharma Foundation' of Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction," 2019) explicitly names Nhất Hạnh among Kabat-Zinn's early influences. **FACT:** Nhất Hạnh wrote the preface to Kabat-Zinn's *Full Catastrophe Living*, the foundational MBSR text (Progressive.org, Feb 2022, citing the book's front matter). **FACT:** Kabat-Zinn has publicly acknowledged he "bent over backwards" to avoid MBSR being seen as Buddhist (Guardian interview, Oct 2017).

The lineage is indirect but acknowledged: Nhất Hạnh's accessible, non-technical presentation demonstrated that mindfulness could be decoupled from monastic ritual without losing its core; MBSR (founded 1979, UMass Medical School) then medicalized and operationalized that accessibility. [BACKGROUND]

INTERPRETATION: Nhất Hạnh's role was less doctrinal source than proof-of-concept. He showed mindfulness could be taught in plain language to lay Westerners; Kabat-Zinn industrialized that insight for clinical settings.

### The "father of mindfulness" label

**FACT:** He is widely celebrated in Western media as the "father of mindfulness" (Progressive.org, Feb 2022; repeated across obituaries including NYT, Jan 2022). The label is contested on two fronts: it erases earlier Asian transmission figures, and it obscures that the Western product called "mindfulness" is a Kabat-Zinn-mediated construction, not a direct Plum Village export. [BACKGROUND]

**FACT:** Plum Village itself frames mindfulness as non-religious but calls "attention to the Buddhist foundations of mindfulness as a sort of invitation," rather than treating Buddhism as a liability to be hidden, in contrast to Kabat-Zinn's strategic de-Buddhification. (Progressive.org, Funie Hsu, Feb 2022.)

### Engagements with power

- **Google:** led a day of mindfulness at Google headquarters (2013, Mountain View) during his North America tour; the talk circulated widely as a landmark of mindfulness entering Silicon Valley corporate culture. [BACKGROUND]
- **World Bank:** led a meditation session for World Bank staff in Washington, DC (2011, widely reported). [BACKGROUND; date/host flagged for confirmation]
- **US Congress:** in 2003 led members of Congress in walking meditation on Capitol Hill; Rep. Tim Ryan (Ohio), author of *A Mindful Nation*, became a prominent political champion. [BACKGROUND; Ryan's role confirmed via Progressive.org]
- **2013 tour:** included an event at Harvard School of Medicine plus large public sangha gatherings. [BACKGROUND; primary documentation flagged]
- **Parliamentarians:** addressed or led practice for UK parliamentarians (2012), and engaged Indian and Thai political figures across Asian tours. [BACKGROUND; specifics flagged as leads]
- Plum Village bio additionally records events for members of US Congress and parliamentarians in the UK, Ireland, India, and Thailand, and high-profile 2013 mindfulness events at Google, the World Bank, and Harvard School of Medicine. [BACKGROUND, plumvillage.org bio]

INTERPRETATION: These engagements were deliberately non-partisan and non-lobbying: he offered practice, not policy. That posture made him acceptable to power, and is also the seed of the McMindfulness critique, since institutions could absorb the practice without the ethics.

### Institutional footprint beyond monasteries

- **Wake Up:** a worldwide movement of young people (roughly 18-35) building "healthy and compassionate societies," with local sanghas in dozens of countries; active post-2022. **FACT** (deerparkmonastery.org; wkup.org).
- **Wake Up Schools:** a worldwide movement training teachers to bring mindfulness into learning environments, operating at three levels (educator's own practice, classroom application, school-wide culture), with active programs in Europe, North America, and Asia. **FACT** (wakeupschools.org).
- **Plum Village App:** live and maintained on iOS and Android, offering guided meditations, talks, and bells; a significant vector of post-pandemic reach. **FACT** (plumvillage.app; download counts not retrieved, UNVERIFIED).

## 8. The McMindfulness tension

### The stripped ethical frame

Nhất Hạnh consistently taught mindfulness as inseparable from ethics: the Five Mindfulness Trainings, Right Mindfulness as one factor of the Eightfold Path, mindfulness as "a way of life," and practice embedded in social action from Vietnam onward. [BACKGROUND] **FACT:** The standard critique that secular mindfulness detaches *sati* from *sīla* and *samādhi* is articulated in the McMindfulness literature: "right mindfulness (sammasati) is only one part of the traditional noble eightfold path, and mindfulness practices often leave out the rest." (Tricycle, Amod Lele, Dec 2019, summarizing Purser.)

INTERPRETATION: The irony at the center of his legacy: the most influential Asian source of Western secular mindfulness was himself an ethicist who insisted mindfulness without ethics is not mindfulness. The secular product is his student, estranged.

### Purser's McMindfulness critique and the counter-critique

**FACT:** Ronald Purser (management professor, San Francisco State University) published *McMindfulness: How Mindfulness Became the New Capitalist Spirituality* (2019), arguing corporate mindfulness privatizes stress, individualizes structural problems, and functions as an opiate of the knowledge economy. Purser names Nhất Hạnh as "one of the pioneers of socially engaged Buddhism" and argues: "Anything that offers success in our unjust society without trying to change it is not revolutionary, it just helps people cope... it says the causes of suffering are disproportionately inside us, not in the political and economic frameworks that shape how we live." (Quoted in Tricycle.)

**FACT:** The counter-critique (Amod Lele in Tricycle; Anālayo, "The Myth of McMindfulness," *Mindfulness* journal 2019, DOI 10.1007/s12671-019-01264-x): Engaged Buddhism is itself a twentieth-century innovation, not classical Buddhism; classical sources locate suffering's cause in craving, so "the problems are in our heads" is impeccably Buddhist. Lele argues Purser unwittingly criticizes modern mindfulness for being too Buddhist.

INTERPRETATION: This is the live fault line. Purser reads secular mindfulness as Engaged Buddhism betrayed; the counter-critics read Engaged Buddhism (Nhất Hạnh's own movement) as a parallel modern invention with no stronger claim to authenticity. Both readings use Nhất Hạnh as evidence.

### Erasure and race

**FACT:** Funie Hsu's analysis (Progressive.org, Feb 2022): mainstream mindfulness's success "depended in large part on the downplaying of its association with Buddhism," and the strategic de-Buddhification "reinforces racial and religious stereotypes in order to appease a white-dominant social structure," while Plum Village treats Buddhism as invitation rather than liability. [Scholar Chenxing Han's adjacent work on Asian American Buddhist erasure is the broader frame.]

INTERPRETATION: The secularization Nhất Hạnh enabled carried a cost he did not choose: the whitening of the product and the sidelining of Asian Buddhist communities even as their teacher was canonized.

### The gap

Nhất Hạnh's own framing of mindfulness in institutions (Google, World Bank) emphasized reducing suffering and cultivating compassion in workers, not productivity. Corporate uptake (Search Inside Yourself at Google, etc.) inverted the emphasis toward performance and resilience. [BACKGROUND]

INTERPRETATION: Whether his corporate appearances legitimized the McMindfulness economy or infiltrated it with Dharma is the central contested legacy question. Evidence supports both: he refused to moralize against his hosts, keeping doors open; and his hosts extracted the technique while ignoring the trainings, a predictable institutional capture.

## 9. Late life, death, and institutional legacy

**FACT:** On November 11, 2014, a month after his 88th birthday, he suffered a severe brain hemorrhage/stroke at Plum Village. Treated in Bordeaux, then San Francisco (UCSF), then a rehabilitation clinic in France. Left largely unable to speak and mostly paralyzed on the right side, he continued teaching through gesture and presence. (Verified: plumvillage.org/about/thich-nhat-hanh/biography.)

He lived at Thai Plum Village 2016-2018. [BACKGROUND]

**FACT:** He returned to Vietnam on October 28, 2018, to live out his life at Từ Hiếu Temple, his root temple in Huế, where he had ordained at sixteen. (Verified: plumvillage.org; Tuoi Tre.) He came out regularly in his wheelchair to lead walking meditation around the ponds and ancestral stupas. [BACKGROUND]

**FACT:** He died peacefully in the early moments of January 22, 2022, in the Deep Listening Hut at Từ Hiếu Temple, Huế, aged 95, surrounded by disciples. (Verified: plumvillage.org; Lion's Roar; Tricycle.) A week of ceremonies culminated in cremation on January 29, 2022; his ashes were distributed between Từ Hiếu, Plum Village France, and other centers. Vietnamese authorities restricted attendance. [BACKGROUND]

His living legacy: 700+ monastic disciples at 11 monasteries, hundreds of lay Dharma teachers, thousands of Order of Interbeing members, and hundreds of thousands of practitioners worldwide, continuing through the International Plum Village Community of Engaged Buddhism. [BACKGROUND, Plum Village bio; monastic totals corroborated this session at 600-800 across wkup.org ("more than 700 monastics worldwide") and plumvillage.org (~600 monastics in 10 practice centers per Sister Chân Không's message), but not pinned to one authoritative figure]

**FACT:** The institutional ecosystem continues operating post-2022: active retreats across centers, Wake Up, Wake Up Schools, and the Plum Village App all maintained (verified live this session, Sept 2026). Senior Dharma teachers (Sister Chân Không, Brother Pháp Hữu, Sister Định Nghiêm, and others) assumed visible leadership; the tradition planned succession via collective sangha governance rather than a single successor, consistent with his teaching that "the next Buddha may take the form of a community." [BACKGROUND]

INTERPRETATION: The legacy is structurally resilient precisely because Nhất Hạnh built institutions, not a personality cult: monasteries, an order, youth and educator movements, and a digital distribution channel. The risk is the inverse of the McMindfulness critique: as the institution professionalizes, it may converge with the secular mindfulness mainstream it was meant to ethically anchor.

## 10. Where the mapping breaks

- **The "father of mindfulness" label compresses a wider transmission.** Nhất Hạnh is routinely credited as the single most important figure in bringing mindfulness West, but the secular mindfulness industry descends through multiple lines: Kabat-Zinn's MBSR drew on Zen, vipassanā (Insight Meditation Society lineages: Goldstein, Salzberg, Kornfield), and Theravada sources at least as much as on Plum Village. The precise weight of his influence on MBSR specifically is asserted in movement literature and his own community's bios more than in independent scholarship. Treat the label as reputation, not genealogy. [INTERPRETATION]
- **Monastery network dates are BACKGROUND-tagged.** Deer Park 2000, Blue Cliff 2007, Magnolia Grove 2011, EIAB 2008 come from the community's own biography pages and were not re-verified this session against independent sources.
- **The "100+ books" figure** is the standard publisher/foundation bio figure, not an independent count. His bibliography includes many short works, re-editions, and compilations; the number inflates if you count every Parallax pamphlet, deflates if you count only major works.
- **The MLK influence claim** (that he moved King toward "Beyond Vietnam") is well-attested in movement histories and by King's own nomination letter, but the causal weight among King's many influences (Bayard Rustin, James Lawson, his own staff) is contested among King scholars. The letter is FACT; the influence weight is BACKGROUND.
- **Self-reported community numbers.** Visitor counts (10,000/year), annual participation (75,000), Five Trainings commitments (100,000+), and monastic headcounts (700+) all originate with the Plum Village community itself. There is no independent audit of any of them.
- **The stroke-years teaching-through-presence narrative** comes entirely from his community's own accounts. It is internally consistent and uncontested, but it is also hagiographic in structure: the silent teacher demonstrating interbeing nonverbally is exactly the ending the tradition would compose. That does not make it false; it makes it a story told by its own protagonists.

## 11. Compression points for synthesis

1. Nhất Hạnh's core move: mindfulness reframed from a bounded technique to continuous presence in ordinary acts (dishes, walking, eating, phone calls), which is precisely what made it transmissible outside monastic conditions.
2. "Engaged Buddhism" was a wartime Vietnamese improvisation (SYSS, 1965; the term codified 1967), not a Western retrofit: spiritual practice and social action as one path, forged under fire from both sides of the war.
3. "Interbeing" is the metaphysics; the Five and Fourteen Mindfulness Trainings are the ethics that fall out of it; walking/eating/gathas/bells are the delivery mechanism; the 100+ books are the transmission. The system is coherent top to bottom.
4. The first three of the Fourteen Trainings (Openness, Non-Attachment to Views, Freedom of Thought) are an anti-dogmatism clause inside a religious ethical code, a direct artifact of watching ideology-driven violence kill his own social workers.
5. MLK nominated him for the 1967 Nobel Peace Prize (letter dated January 25, 1967, verified verbatim) and broke convention to publicize it; the committee awarded no prize that year.
6. Exile (1966-2005) inverted the usual missionary trajectory: Buddhism came West through a refugee of conscience whose authority derived from refusing both Cold War camps.
7. Plum Village (1982) became the template for the modern mindfulness center as institution: 11 monasteries, 700+ monastics, 10,000+ visitors/year, Order of Interbeing, Wake Up, Wake Up Schools.
8. The secular mindfulness industry descends from the stream he opened but routinely drops his load-bearing claim: mindfulness is not a stress-reduction tool but a way of life aimed at transforming suffering, inseparable from ethics. This is the live fault line between Plum Village and corporate mindfulness.
9. Final arc: stroke (November 11, 2014), return to Từ Hiếu (October 28, 2018), death (January 22, 2022) at the temple where he ordained at sixteen. The teacher of interbeing ended by teaching nonverbally.

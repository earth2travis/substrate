# Design Thinking: Applications and Case Evidence

Raw research file. Concept 3 of 4 from a deep-dive series on design thinking produced 2026-11-14, closing the Substrate's design thinking coverage gap identified in the AI Factory design brief. This file covers where design thinking has been applied and what outcome evidence exists. It is source material for later synthesis, not a finished essay.

Stance: for each case, record who, when, what was designed, what outcome was claimed, and whether the claim is self-reported or independently verified. FACT tags apply to claims verified live this session; BACKGROUND marks established record not re-verified; INTERPRETATION tags apply to readings across cases. Self-reported corporate claims are flagged SELF-REPORTED inline.

## 1. The claim under examination

INTERPRETATION: design thinking's authority rests on a canon of cases repeated across consulting decks and HBR articles. The load-bearing question is not whether the cases are famous but whether their outcome claims are independently verified, self-reported by the vendor, or attributed retroactively. This file sorts the canon accordingly.

## 2. Canonical corporate cases

### Bank of America, "Keep the Change" (IDEO, 2004 to 2006)

- FACT (verified live): Bank of America engaged IDEO for ethnography-based innovation work aimed at boomer-age women with children; IDEO conducted observational research in Atlanta, Baltimore, and San Francisco. Source: IDEO's own case page (ideo.com/work/keep-the-change-account-service-for-bofa).
- FACT (verified live): The resulting service rounds up Visa debit purchases to the nearest dollar and sweeps the difference into savings. IDEO's page states the program launched in October 2005. A secondary account (thisisdesignthinking.net, citing a 2006 Bloomberg case study) gives September 2005; the month differs, the year (2005) is consistent across sources.
- FACT (verified live, SELF-REPORTED): IDEO claims Keep the Change attracted 2.5 million customers in under a year, translating into 700,000-plus new checking accounts and one million new savings accounts. This figure originates with IDEO and BofA marketing materials; the IDSA award citation repeats the same numbers, but the chain of provenance is the program owners, not an independent audit.
- BACKGROUND (SELF-REPORTED): Later BofA communications claimed 12 million-plus enrolled customers and over $2 billion saved cumulatively, plus a 99% retention figure. Not independently verified.
- INTERPRETATION: Keep the Change is the most-cited corporate design thinking case in the popular literature. The mechanism (round-up savings) is real and durable, but the success metrics rest entirely on the two parties with the strongest incentive to tell a success story.

### Procter & Gamble under A.G. Lafley and Claudia Kotchka

- BACKGROUND: A.G. Lafley became P&G CEO in 2000 and made design a stated strategic priority; Claudia Kotchka, a marketing executive, was appointed to lead the design function and built an internal design organization, placing designers on business teams and bringing in IDEO and other firms. The narrative is canonized in the 2008 Harvard Business School case "Design Thinking in the Laundry Room" and in Lafley and Ram Charan's 2008 book "The Game-Changer."
- BACKGROUND (SELF-REPORTED): Claimed outcomes include design-influenced products such as the Swiffer line extension work, Mr. Clean Magic Eraser market development, and Olay's premium repositioning; P&G attributed substantial organic sales growth during Lafley's first tenure (2000 to 2009) partly to innovation capacity. The design-specific share of that growth is not separable from other causes and is not independently measured.
- INTERPRETATION: The P&G case functions in the literature as proof that design thinking can be institutionalized inside a large incumbent. The evidence is structural (org charts, headcount, board-level design sponsorship) more than financial; no study isolates design's causal contribution to P&G's stock performance in the period.

### GE Healthcare "Adventure Series" MRI (Doug Dietz)

- BACKGROUND: Doug Dietz, a GE Healthcare designer, recounts observing a child in distress before an MRI scan he had designed, which reframed his work around the pediatric patient experience. With hospital partners and child-life specialists, GE developed the "Adventure Series" (scanner rooms themed as pirate islands, space voyages, and camping trips) at sites including the University of Pittsburgh Medical Center and Children's Hospital of Pittsburgh around 2008 to 2013. The story is told by Dietz in a widely viewed TEDMED talk and in GE marketing.
- BACKGROUND (SELF-REPORTED): GE and hospital partners claimed reductions in pediatric sedation rates for MRI (figures such as drops from roughly 80% to under 30% of pediatric patients requiring sedation circulate in retellings, attributed to Children's Hospital of Pittsburgh) and gains in patient satisfaction scores. These numbers originate with GE and the partner hospitals; no independent peer-reviewed controlled study of the Adventure Series outcomes is part of the canonical record.
- INTERPRETATION: This is the canonical empathy narrative in design thinking teaching. The sedation-avoidance mechanism is clinically plausible (less fear, less motion, less need for sedation), but the specific percentages in circulation should be treated as promotional claims until matched to a clinical publication.

### Airbnb's 2009 design turn

- BACKGROUND: In early 2009 Airbnb's growth had stalled at roughly $200 per week in revenue. Cofounder Joe Gebbia (RISD-trained designer) recounts the team flying to New York, visiting hosts, and replacing amateur listing photos with professional photography, an intervention he frames as a design-driven, non-scalable "do things that don't scale" move (also canonized via Paul Graham's Y Combinator advice). Bookings in New York roughly doubled within a week, per the founders' retelling.
- BACKGROUND: Gebbia's later narrative ("Airbnb is a design company," his TED talks, and frequent design-press interviews) presents the 2009 turn as the founding instance of design thinking at Airbnb: empathy with users, rapid prototyping of an experience change, measured response.
- INTERPRETATION: The photography experiment is well documented as an event, but its framing as "design thinking" is retrospective. The measurable outcome (a doubling of New York bookings in one week, per the founders) is self-reported startup lore, repeated in countless talks and never independently audited. The case's evidentiary value is as a cultural founding myth that shaped Airbnb's actual later investment in design, not as a controlled test of method.

## 3. Healthcare

### Mayo Clinic Center for Innovation (CFI)

- BACKGROUND: Mayo Clinic's Center for Innovation grew out of the SPARC program (See, Plan, Act, Refine, Communicate), an internal service-design laboratory in outpatient medicine founded around 2002 to 2004 and formalized as the Center for Innovation in 2008. CFI is frequently cited as the first dedicated service-design innovation center inside a major US academic medical center.
- BACKGROUND (SELF-REPORTED): Claimed outcomes include redesigned outpatient consultation experiences, the "Jack and Jill" consultation-room redesign work, and spinout of design methods across Mayo practice. Evidence is largely internal publications and conference talks by CFI staff.
- INTERPRETATION: SPARC is important as an institutional proof point (a top-tier clinical organization embedding designers in care delivery), but its published outcomes are descriptive case reports, not controlled evaluations.

### Kaiser Permanente Garfield Innovation Center

- BACKGROUND: Kaiser Permanente opened the Sidney R. Garfield Health Care Innovation Center in San Leandro, California in 2006, a 37,000-square-foot facility for prototyping care environments, workflows, and technology with clinicians and patients. Kaiser worked with IDEO on projects including nurse shift-change bedside handoff redesign (the "Nurse Knowledge Exchange"), which is a standard teaching case in the design literature.
- BACKGROUND (SELF-REPORTED): Kaiser and IDEO reported improved handoff reliability and nurse satisfaction from the bedside handoff work; figures in circulation come from the organizations themselves.

### Design firms and hospital systems

- BACKGROUND: IDEO, frog, and Continuum ran sustained healthcare practices through the 2000s and 2010s; canonical projects include IDEO's work with Kaiser (above) and with the Mayo Clinic, and frog's work for healthcare clients. The institutional pattern (hospitals hiring design firms, then building internal design labs) is itself a documented diffusion trend tracked by observers such as the NEJM Catalyst and design-management journals.
- INTERPRETATION: Healthcare is the sector where design thinking's institutional adoption is best documented, but the outcome evidence is the weakest link: adoption metrics (labs opened, staff trained) far outnumber patient-outcome studies.

## 4. Government and public sector

### Danish MindLab

- FACT (verified live): MindLab was created in 2002 as a cross-ministerial innovation unit (initiated within the Ministry of Economic and Business Affairs, later jointly owned with the Ministries of Taxation and Employment). Sources: Apolitical's inside history ("Since Denmark created MindLab in 2002"), Christian Bason's scholarly account (Innovation Journal PDF), and Monocle ("Established in 2002 as part of the Ministry for Economic and Business Affairs"). Multiple independent sources agree on 2002.
- BACKGROUND: MindLab applied ethnographic and co-design methods to policy and service problems (employment services, tax communication, business regulation) and became the global reference case for public-sector innovation labs; Bason's "Leading Public Sector Innovation" (2010) is the standard account. MindLab was wound down as a standalone unit in 2018 to 2019 and its functions absorbed into the Danish Disruption Council / National Centre for Public Sector Innovation.
- INTERPRETATION: MindLab's documented contribution is methodological diffusion (hundreds of labs modeled on it) rather than a small set of audited policy outcomes. Bason's own accounts are those of its former director, an in-house provenance worth noting.

### UK Government Digital Service (GDS)

- BACKGROUND: GDS was formed in 2011 within the Cabinet Office after Martha Lane Fox's 2010 review, built GOV.UK (launched 2012, replacing Directgov and Business Link), and published the Government Design Principles (10 principles, opening with "Start with user needs"). GOV.UK won the Design Museum's Design of the Year award in 2013.
- BACKGROUND: Claimed outcomes include consolidation of roughly 1,800 government domains into one, and material savings versus the replaced estates (GDS published savings figures in its annual reporting; these are government self-reports, though scrutinized by the National Audit Office in some areas).

### Singapore and Helsinki design labs

- BACKGROUND: Singapore's DesignSingapore Council (established 2003 within the then-Ministry of Information, Communications and the Arts) promoted design capability nationally, including public-sector design programs and the adoption of service design in agencies. Helsinki's design-lab lineage includes Helsinki Design Lab (2008 to 2013, run by Sitra, the Finnish Innovation Fund), which applied "strategic design" to public challenges, and the city's later chief design officer appointment (2016) under its UNESCO City of Design status; Helsinki was World Design Capital 2012.
- INTERPRETATION: Both cases are institution-building evidence: governments formalizing design capacity. Outcome claims (better services, savings) exist mainly in the agencies' own publications.

### US federal level: VA VEO, 18F, USDS

- BACKGROUND: The US Digital Service (USDS) was created in August 2014 inside the Executive Office of the President after the HealthCare.gov rescue; 18F was founded in March 2014 inside the General Services Administration as a digital consultancy for other agencies. Both adopted human-centered design as stated doctrine (USDS Digital Services Playbook, 18F's methods guides, publicly published).
- BACKGROUND: The Department of Veterans Affairs stood up the Veterans Experience Office in 2015 under President Obama, applying customer-experience and human-centered methods to VA services; it publishes trust-score tracking (the VA's "trust in VA" survey metric rose through the late 2010s per VA's own reporting).
- INTERPRETATION: The US federal case is the strongest evidence that design practice can be institutionalized at scale in government (playbooks, hiring pipelines, statutory durability of USDS), but nearly all performance numbers are agency self-reports.

## 5. Social innovation

### IDEO.org

- FACT (verified live): IDEO.org was launched in 2011 (announced March 2011, launched later that year) as IDEO's nonprofit sister organization focused on poverty-related challenges; Jocelyn Wyatt became its executive director. Sources: Core77 announcement interview (March 7, 2011), Woodcock Foundation grantee profile ("Born in 2011 out of the global design and innovation firm IDEO"), Knowledge at Wharton interviews.
- BACKGROUND (SELF-REPORTED): IDEO.org claims 45-plus countries worked in, 64 million people reached, and 1 million-plus users of its Design Kit platform per its current site. Self-reported impact metrics, typical of the sector.

### Acumen, Design Kit, and +Acumen

- BACKGROUND: IDEO, funded by the Bill and Melinda Gates Foundation, published the Human-Centered Design Toolkit (2009, second edition 2011), which evolved into "Design Kit: The Field Guide to Human-Centered Design" (2015). Acumen (founded 2001 by Jacqueline Novogratz) partnered with IDEO to deliver the material as free online courses under +Acumen (launched around 2012), which reported hundreds of thousands of enrollments across its course catalog. Enrollment figures are self-reported.

### Embrace infant warmer

- BACKGROUND: The Embrace infant warmer originated as a 2007 student project in the Stanford d.school course "Design for Extreme Affordability" (team including Jane Chen, Rahul Panicker, Linus Liang, Nag Murty). The low-cost (target roughly 1% of the price of a conventional incubator) phase-change-pouch warmer spun out as Embrace (nonprofit, founded 2008) and later Embrace Innovations (for-profit, 2012).
- BACKGROUND (SELF-REPORTED): Embrace claimed its warmers reached hundreds of thousands of babies across a dozen-plus countries (figures of 200,000 to 300,000-plus infants appear in organizational communications). Distribution counts are organization-reported; clinical outcome evaluation exists in the literature but at small scale.
- BACKGROUND: d.light, the solar lantern and home-system company, is also an Extreme Affordability lineage story: cofounders Sam Goldman and Ned Tozun met in the class (2006 to 2007 era), and d.light reports reaching well over 100 million people cumulatively (self-reported, though partially corroborated by GOGLA industry data). Other Extreme Affordability alumni ventures include re:motion designs (Jaipur Knee lineage) and driptech.
- INTERPRETATION: The extreme-affordability cohort is the strongest existence proof that a design course can produce durable ventures. The unit of evidence is ventures founded and units distributed, not health or income outcomes measured against controls.

## 6. Measurement attempts

### McKinsey Design Index, "The Business Value of Design" (October 2018)

- FACT (verified live): The report, by Benedict Sheppard, Hugo Sarrazin, Garen Kouyoumjian, and Fabricio Dore, is dated October 25, 2018 on mckinsey.com (full report PDF labeled October 2018). It tracked 300 publicly listed companies over five years, collected over two million pieces of financial data and 100,000-plus "design actions," and built the McKinsey Design Index (MDI).
- FACT (verified live): The headline claim, verbatim from the report's Exhibit 1 caption: "Companies with top-quartile McKinsey Design Index scores outperformed industry-benchmark growth by as much as two to one." The report also states top-quartile MDI scorers achieved 32 percentage points higher revenue growth and 56 percentage points higher total-returns-to-shareholders growth over the five-year period, with results holding across medical technology, consumer goods, and retail banking.
- INTERPRETATION: The study is correlational by its own construction (regression of financial outcomes on a composite design-actions score). McKinsey discloses this; the report says "strong correlation," not causation. MDI construction details (the 12 actions and their weights) are not fully public, which limits independent replication.

### DMI Design Value Index

- FACT (verified live): The Design Management Institute's Design Value Index was built with Motiv Strategies (funded by Microsoft, per DMI). Three editions exist: 2013 (2003 to 2013 window, 228% over S&P 500), 2014 (2004 to 2014 window, 16 companies, 219% over S&P 500), and 2015 (2005 to 2015 window, 16 companies, 211% over the S&P 500, published December 2016). Sources: dmi.org 2014 and 2015 results pages and DMI's "Value of Design" summary.
- FACT (verified live): The 2014 edition's own footnotes state the DVI "is not currently an index fund in existence and is not licensed by any regulatory body or index fund operator" and is "a theoretical construction of various equity securities deemed to have placed a significant emphasis on investing in design" by field experts. Apple's market-cap weight is acknowledged as material; excluding Apple, the 2014 figure drops to 138% above the S&P.
- INTERPRETATION: The standard methodological criticism, visible even in sympathetic secondary coverage: the index is backtested and hand-picked (companies were selected for having strong design management, then their prior ten-year returns measured), creating survivorship and selection bias; Apple alone drives a large share of the outperformance; and the sponsor (DMI, with Motiv Strategies) sells design-management services, a conflict worth stating. The 211% figure commonly quoted in design marketing is the 2015 edition; quoting it as "design-led companies beat the S&P by 211%" without the construction caveats is misleading.

## 7. Enterprise design-thinking programs

### IBM

- BACKGROUND: IBM rebuilt a design capability at enterprise scale under CEO Ginni Rometty, hiring Phil Gilbert (formerly of Lombardi Software) in 2012 to lead the program; IBM claims to have hired over 1,000 formally trained designers and trained hundreds of thousands of employees via its "IBM Design Thinking" framework (the Loop, Hills, Sponsor Users, Playbacks). IBM opened design studios in Austin and globally. For methods detail see the sibling methods file; the claimed business outcomes include faster time-to-market and NPS improvements on redesigned offerings, all IBM self-reports. A 2018 Forrester Total Economic Impact study (commissioned by IBM) claimed a 301% ROI for IBM Design Thinking engagements; commissioned research, treat as vendor-sponsored.
- INTERPRETATION: IBM is the largest corporate natural experiment in scaling design training. The outcome evidence is either self-reported or commissioned, but the training scale itself (hundreds of thousands of badges) is verifiable and itself a data point about diffusion.

### SAP

- BACKGROUND: SAP adopted design thinking from roughly 2004 under Hasso Plattner's sponsorship (Plattner also co-funded the Stanford d.school), built an internal design thinking practice, and applied it to product development, later formalizing SAP Fiori (2013) as a design system and UX relaunch across its product line. SAP appointed a Chief Design Officer and reports design-driven renewal of its flagship products. Outcome claims (user satisfaction, adoption of Fiori) are SAP self-reports; SAP appears in the 2015 DMI DVI roster.

### Intuit, "Design for Delight" (D4D)

- BACKGROUND: Under founder Scott Cook and design VP Kaaren Hanson (joined 2012 from a design leadership role), Intuit formalized "Design for Delight" as its design thinking program, training thousands of employees, creating "Innovation Catalysts" (a corps of roughly 150 to 200 trained facilitators), and embedding rapid customer experimentation. Hanson presented the program publicly (including a TEDx talk) and Intuit published internal metrics.
- BACKGROUND (SELF-REPORTED): Intuit attributed product outcomes to D4D, including TurboTax and QuickBooks redesign results and claims of improved customer-benefit metrics; Cook credited design-driven experimentation culture for Intuit's sustained growth. Intuit appears on the DMI Design Value Index rosters. All outcome figures are company-reported.



## 8. Where the mapping breaks

- The evidence pyramid inverts the publicity pyramid. The most-cited cases (Keep the Change, Adventure Series, Airbnb photography) have the weakest independent verification; their numbers trace to IDEO, GE, and the founders respectively.
- Both flagship measurement instruments (McKinsey MDI, DMI DVI) are correlational, and the DVI is additionally backtested, hand-selected, Apple-dominated, and sponsor-conflicted. The honest summary is "design-mature firms outperformed," not "design thinking causes outperformance."
- Government and healthcare cases are institution-adoption evidence more than outcome evidence: labs founded, playbooks published, staff trained.
- Retrospective framing pervades the canon: events (Airbnb's photo trip, P&G's org changes) are narrated as "design thinking" after the fact by storytellers with reputational stakes.



## 9. Compression points for synthesis

1. Keep the Change: IDEO engagement 2004, launch October 2005 (IDEO's own date), 2.5 million customers in under one year (SELF-REPORTED by IDEO/BofA). FACT on dates and mechanics; SELF-REPORTED on outcomes.
2. McKinsey "The Business Value of Design," October 25, 2018: 300 companies, 5 years, MDI; verbatim headline "outperformed industry-benchmark growth by as much as two to one"; 32 pp revenue, 56 pp TRS. Correlational by construction. FACT.
3. DMI Design Value Index: three editions (2013: 228%; 2014: 219%; 2015: 211%), 16 companies, theoretical backtested portfolio, Microsoft-funded, Apple-weighted (138% ex-Apple in 2014). FACT on figures; strong methodological caveats.
4. MindLab founded 2002, cross-ministerial, Copenhagen; wound down 2018 to 2019. FACT on founding.
5. IDEO.org founded 2011 (announced March 2011). FACT.
6. GE Adventure Series sedation-reduction figures in circulation are GE/hospital self-reports without a canonical peer-reviewed controlled study.
7. Airbnb's 2009 photography experiment is documented as an event; its "design thinking" framing is retrospective founder narrative.
8. Enterprise programs (IBM, SAP D4D counterparts, Intuit D4D) show verifiable training scale; outcome metrics are self-reported or commissioned (Forrester TEI for IBM, 301% ROI claim, vendor-sponsored).

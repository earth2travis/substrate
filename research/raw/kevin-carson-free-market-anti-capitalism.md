## 1. Who Kevin Carson is

- Kevin A. Carson: American political economist, social theorist, and writer; born 1963 [BACKGROUND]. Grew up in Tennessee and worked for many years as a pressroom worker in a printing plant, writing and researching in parallel with wage labor rather than from an academic post [BACKGROUND]. No PhD; self-described independent scholar [BACKGROUND]. Currently based in the Fayetteville, Arkansas area [BACKGROUND].
- Self-description on his own site: "Freelance Writer and Anarchist Without Adjectives" [FACT, https://kevinacarson.org]. Public email handle is free.market.anticapitalist at gmail.com, the label doubling as a doctrinal statement [FACT, https://kevinacarson.org].
- Current self-framing: work focused on "issues of systemic transition and the building blocks of postcapitalist society, along with critiques of orthodox, neoliberal, and right-libertarian economics," with stated influences in "the new municipalist movements and autonomism" [FACT, https://kevinacarson.org]. Earlier and better-known self-labels: "free-market anti-capitalist," "mutualist," "left-libertarian," "individualist anarchist" in the Tucker/Warren lineage [BACKGROUND]. The drift from Rothbardian vocabulary toward autonomism/municipalism over the 2010s is visible in that change [INTERPRETATION].
- The working-class employment biography is load-bearing: he writes as a laborer-theorist outside the academy and repeatedly grounds critiques of managerial hierarchy in the lived experience of wage work [INTERPRETATION].
- Wikipedia has no standalone article on him; the Mutualism (economic theory) article credits him with the internet-era revival of mutualist economics [FACT, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutualism_(economic_theory)].

## 2. The intellectual position: free-market anti-capitalism

### 2.1 Lineage

- Primary roots: Proudhon's mutualism and the American individualist anarchists: Josiah Warren, Benjamin R. Tucker, William B. Greene, Lysander Spooner, Ezra Heywood, Dyer Lum. Core inheritance: occupancy-and-use property norms, labor theory of value, mutual credit/free banking, cost-the-limit-of-price exchange, hostility to rent, interest, and profit as state-enabled extraction [BACKGROUND].
- Second root: the American Old Right and its libertarian descendants: Murray Rothbard, Albert Jay Nock, Frank Chodorov, Garet Garrett. From Rothbard specifically: anti-statism, the revisionist history of the Progressive Era as corporate consolidation, and the 1960s New Left/Old Right fusion moment (Rothbard's Left and Right, Karl Hess). From Nock and Chodorov via Franz Oppenheimer: the state as the machinery of class exploitation, "economic means" versus "political means" [BACKGROUND].
- Third strand: Georgist land critique and the individualist occupancy-and-use tradition (Ingalls, Tucker, Spooner); Carson synthesizes Lockean homesteading, Tucker's occupancy-and-use, and Georgist rent critique rather than adopting Henry George wholesale [BACKGROUND; synthesis characterization INTERPRETATION].
- Fourth strand: Austrian economics used critically. Trained on Mises, Hayek, Rothbard, Menger, Böhm-Bawerk; accepts the knowledge-problem and time-structure insights while rejecting right-Austrian apologetics for actually existing capitalism [BACKGROUND].

### 2.2 Left-Rothbardianism and the break with mainstream libertarianism

- Carson emerged publicly in the 2000s as the leading theorist of "left-Rothbardianism": taking Rothbard's anti-state, anti-corporate apparatus and reattaching it to the labor, anti-capitalist, and decentralist concerns Rothbard dropped after his break with the New Left. Allied milieu: Roderick T. Long, Charles W. Johnson, Gary Chartier, Sheldon Richman, the Molinari Institute, the Alliance of the Libertarian Left [BACKGROUND].
- Signature polemical construct: "vulgar libertarianism," the habit of using free-market rhetoric to defend the distributional outcomes of state capitalism ("the poor choose to work in sweatshops," said of a world where the alternatives were state-foreclosed). The test he proposes: would the defense still apply if the background pattern of property were traced to its origins in state violence? If the analysis ignores origins, it is vulgar. The term circulated from his blog in the mid-2000s and was adopted across the left-libertarian scene [BACKGROUND].
- Recurring targets: corporate-welfare libertarianism, Beltway think tanks, defenses of IP and landlordism and managerial capitalism; disputes with Walter Block and others over sweatshops and property theory. Long-running polemical conflict with the Ludwig von Mises Institute and paleolibertarianism, over value theory, the history of capitalism, and the paleo strategy of alliance with the cultural right [BACKGROUND, individual episodes unverified].
- Consequence: Carson and the C4SS milieu function as a distinct left pole inside, and partly outside, organized American libertarianism; many right-libertarians regard him as a socialist, many socialists as a market apologist [INTERPRETATION].

### 2.3 What the label means

- Definitional move: "capitalism" names not markets per se but a historical system of class rule in which the state shapes property and market rules to enable extraction: enclosure, subsidies, IP, licensing, cartelization, money monopoly. Markets minus that intervention would, on his account, dissolve concentrated capital and shift production toward cooperative, artisanal, and household forms [BACKGROUND].
- Hence pro-market, anti-capitalist, anti-state, anti-corporate, a position he insists is the original meaning of the 19th-century individualist anarchists and of "free market" before its 20th-century capture. He has argued "capitalism" was coined by its critics and that its use as a synonym for "free markets" is a polemical accident [BACKGROUND].
- Recurring slogan: "If this is the free market, then I'm against it" [BACKGROUND]. The canonical statement of the historical claim is his essay "The Iron Fist Behind the Invisible Hand" (2002, expanded versions later) [FACT, essay existence confirmed via c4ss.org search results this session].

## 3. The works

Stylistic signature across all of them: very long books (MPE roughly 400 pages, Organization Theory roughly 600, Homebrew roughly 400), dense with block quotes, self-published via print-on-demand, freely distributed as PDFs [BACKGROUND].

### 3.1 Studies in Mutualist Political Economy (2004; expanded 2007)

Bibliographic anchor: originally self-published as the pamphlet "The Iron Fist Behind the Invisible Hand" (2002, Red Lion Press, Montreal), expanded into Studies (2004; second/expanded edition 2007, BookSurge; freely downloadable). Title page and table of contents verified against the full text at mutualist.org this session [FACT]. This is the magnum opus.

Part One, value theory (chs. 1 to 3) [FACT, structure verified from TOC and extracted passages]:

- Ch. 1 concedes the marginalist critique (Jevons, Menger, Böhm-Bawerk) landed blows on vulgar versions of the labor theory but argues it never touched the Ricardian core. His central move: the classical "cost principle" (price gravitates toward cost of production under free competition) is a limiting case of subjective value theory. Marginal utility sets prices for goods in fixed supply; for reproducible goods under free entry, competition drives price toward the subjective disutility of labor plus waiting. Marginalism describes the mechanism; the LTV describes the equilibrium outcome [BACKGROUND].
- Ch. 2 restates the LTV in subjectivist terms: labor's disutility (Smith's "toil and trouble") is the real cost, and exchange value tends toward embodied labor as competition equalizes producers' subjective costs. He engages Maurice Dobb's "real cost" objection [FACT, ch. 2 excerpts verified].
- Ch. 3 takes on Böhm-Bawerk's claim that interest refutes the LTV: interest is not a necessary return to "waiting" but a scarcity rent on capital made artificially scarce by state banking and property rules; with Tucker's "money monopoly" abolished, interest would fall toward the pure cost of administering loans. Time preference exists but determines interest only where capital is artificially scarce [FACT, chapter exists and engages Böhm-Bawerk; detailed claim structure BACKGROUND].
- Ideological-motivation charge: marginalism triumphed partly because it was useful against Marxism and Henry George; by dissolving "labor" and "land" into homogeneous "factors" it obscured the distinct political origins of rent and profit [BACKGROUND].

Part Two, history (chs. 4 to 8), the iron fist [FACT, chapter structure and multiple long passages verified against the mutualist.org text]:

- Primitive accumulation (ch. 4): capitalism was born not from thrift but from expropriation. Enclosure of the English commons, political preemption of land in settler colonies, and state terror against workers (Combination Laws, poor-law discipline) created a propertyless labor force. He quotes pro-enclosure writers celebrating that enclosure eliminated "a dangerous centre of indiscipline," and quotes Oppenheimer's "political occupation of land" approvingly [FACT].
- Tucker's Big Four monopolies (ch. 5): land monopoly (absentee title enforced by the state), money monopoly (banking charter cartelization, legal-tender law), patents, and tariffs. Plus transportation subsidies. These, he argues, did the main work of exploitation in the so-called laissez-faire era; no night-watchman period ever existed [FACT, ch. 5 TOC verified; transportation subsidies section verified].
- Rise of monopoly capitalism (chs. 6 to 7): the corporate economy was created by state action at the turn of the 20th century; the merger movement would have failed without regulatory cartelization (Kolko's The Triumph of Conservatism thesis), railroad subsidies, and the federal regulatory apparatus big business itself drafted. Patents enabled stable oligopolies [BACKGROUND, consistent with ch. 6.A].
- Imperialism (ch. 7) as the export of surplus capital and goods that domestic underconsumption cannot absorb; he stresses the theory was articulated first by corporate leaders themselves (NAM president, Dean Acheson), not by Lenin [FACT, excerpts verified].
- Crisis tendencies (ch. 8): overaccumulation as primary contradiction (Baran and Sweezy, James O'Connor's fiscal crisis theory), secondary legitimation crises, neoliberal reaction [FACT, ch. 8 TOC and O'Connor passages verified].
- Summary thesis: every stage of capitalism, from primitive accumulation through monopoly capital to neoliberal globalization, depended on direct state violence or subsidy. Absent the state, the wage system and absentee ownership could not sustain themselves; genuinely free markets would be socialist in outcome (labor keeps its product) [INTERPRETATION, synthesis].

Part Three, praxis (ch. 9): the cost principle, all costs and benefits internalized to the actor; price equals cost in free competition; exploitation is any persistent deviation of price from cost enforced by privilege [FACT, ch. 9.A verified]. Strategy: not seizing state power but building counter-institutions, mutual banks, occupancy-and-use land trusts, cooperatives [BACKGROUND].

The "political economy of waste" appears here in seed: state intervention systematically subsidizes capital intensity and centralization (transport subsidies shift distribution costs off large firms; R&D subsidies and the military-industrial complex socialize the cost of technologies corporate capital appropriates). The economy is therefore far more capital-intensive than a free market would produce, generating an overaccumulation/underconsumption tendency the state then manages via aggregate demand, imperialism, and the permanent war economy. Waste is structural, not incidental [FACT for the ch. 5.E transportation-subsidies section and chs. 6 to 8 structure; the waste synthesis INTERPRETATION].

### 3.2 Organization Theory: A Libertarian Perspective (2008)

BookSurge, 2008 [BACKGROUND, publication details not re-verified this session].

- Core thesis: large hierarchical organizations, the corporation and the state agency alike, suffer systematic informational and incentive pathologies no internal reform can cure. Hierarchy is not a condition of efficiency but a parasite on it [BACKGROUND].
- Target: the Coasean transaction-cost justification of the firm. Carson accepts Coase's framing but argues the calculation is rigged: state subsidies to size (capital markets, transport, R&D, liability limits, IP) artificially lower the transaction costs of hierarchy and raise those of horizontal exchange, so observed firm size reflects subsidy, not efficiency [BACKGROUND].
- The knowledge problem inside the firm: Hayek's "Use of Knowledge in Society" turned against the corporation. Tacit distributed knowledge cannot be transmitted upward through a hierarchy any more than to a planning board. Management's data is systematically falsified (subordinates filter to please superiors; metrics displace judgment; he draws on Parkinson, the Peter Principle, Crozier, Robert Jackall's Moral Mazes). The CEO's position is structurally analogous to the Soviet Gosplan official: nominally omnipotent, actually dependent on aggregated fictions [BACKGROUND].
- Signature claim: economies of scale are real but bounded; past a point set by the fixed/variable cost ratio and distribution distance, diseconomies dominate: administrative overhead, information loss, agency problems, supply-chain fragility. Empirical hinge: most industries' minimum efficient plant size is far smaller than actual firm size; firms grow past it because market power rewards growth and the state absorbs the diseconomies [BACKGROUND].
- Internalize costs, externalize inefficiency: hierarchy persists by pushing its inefficiencies onto suppliers (oligopsony), workers (monopsony created by blocked self-employment alternatives), taxpayers (subsidy), consumers (cartel pricing). The shareholder/manager agency split means managers run firms for management's benefit while nominal owners are too dispersed to discipline them [BACKGROUND].
- Alternatives surveyed: worker cooperatives, peer production (Benkler's Wealth of Networks), Japanese lean production re-read as partially de-hierarchizing, VISA's chaordic structure (Dee Hock), open-source governance. Where hierarchy is necessary it should be the servant of the producers, not their master [BACKGROUND].

### 3.3 The Homebrew Industrial Revolution: A Low-Overhead Manifesto (2010)

C4SS, 2010 [BACKGROUND; direct PDF extraction failed this session, c4ss.org 404 and kevinacarson.org rate limit; re-extract to upgrade tags].

- The micro-manufacturing thesis: the 20th-century model (gigantic factories serving continental markets, financed by outside capital, justified by economies of scale) is being technologically undermined by cheap, small-scale, digitally controlled production tools: CNC mills, 3D printers (RepRap as paradigmatic self-replicating case), laser cutters, the small machine tools of the Chinese garage-shop economy [BACKGROUND].
- Historical counter-narrative: the First Industrial Revolution was itself partially derailed. Before the state-subsidized factory system consolidated, a different path, networked small shops on the model of the Sheffield cutlery and Birmingham gun trades, was technically viable. Framework from Kropotkin's Fields, Factories and Workshops, Schumacher, Mumford's neotechnic versus paleotechnic [BACKGROUND].
- Key concept: low overhead. The household and micro-workshop collapse the distinction between production and consumption units, slash capital requirements (tools costing hundreds to low thousands of dollars replace million-dollar plants), and eliminate the wage system's overhead layer, which he argues constitutes the majority of retail price [BACKGROUND].
- Open-source hardware as decisive enabler: once physical production's remaining bottleneck is design rather than tooling, the open design ecology (open hardware, Fab Labs, hackerspaces, Arduino/RepRap) lets designs circulate at zero marginal cost while production localizes. The "Homebrew" framing deliberately echoes the Homebrew Computer Club: as the PC shattered the mainframe priesthood, desktop manufacturing shatters the factory priesthood [BACKGROUND].
- IP as the main barrier: the state's intellectual property regime is the principal legal obstacle; patents cartelize design and criminalize file-sharing, trademark and copyright extend control over distribution. The political economy of IP enforcement (ACTA-era treaties, DMCA anticircumvention) is the corporate state's counter-revolution against relocalized production [BACKGROUND].
- Crisis convergence: links micro-manufacturing to Peak Oil, the 2008 crisis, and unemployment; as subsidized long-distance distribution becomes unaffordable and formal employment collapses, the informal/household sector (James Scott, Colin Ward, Ralph Borsodi's homestead tradition) becomes the sphere where livelihood is reconstituted [BACKGROUND].

### 3.4 The Desktop Regulatory State: The Countervailing Power of Individuals and Networks (2016)

C4SS, 2016 [BACKGROUND, unverified in-session].

- Problem statement: orthodox regulation is captured by definition (regulated industries draft the rules and staff the agencies; Kolko/Stigler capture theory as baseline), but the left-libertarian cannot answer "no regulation" because real harms exist and common-law litigation was historically disabled by the state (limited liability, preemption of nuisance suits) [BACKGROUND].
- Thesis: the same networked communications that enable peer production enable peer enforcement, stigmergic many-to-many scrutiny replacing the bureaucratic inspector. "Desktop regulatory state" names the dispersed capacity of networked individuals to monitor, document, publicize, and sanction [BACKGROUND].
- Mechanisms: consumer review networks, whistleblower platforms (WikiLeaks as exemplar), open-data scraping, citizen sensor monitoring of polluters, reputation systems, boycott coordination, hacktivist exposure, naming and shaming [BACKGROUND].
- The inversion: state and corporation depend on information asymmetry (secrecy for them, surveillance for the public). The desktop regulatory state inverts it: sousveillance, watching from below in Steve Mann's term, against surveillance. Open-source accountability: as many eyeballs find bugs, many eyeballs find malfeasance [BACKGROUND].
- Placement in the arc: MPE diagnoses state-created capitalism; Organization Theory and Homebrew describe the post-corporate production system; Desktop Regulatory State describes how that society polices itself without a bureaucratic state [INTERPRETATION].

### 3.5 Exodus: General Idea of the Revolution in the XXI Century (2018; rev. 2021)

Title nods to Proudhon's General Idea of the Revolution in the Nineteenth Century. Carson's site dates the book 2021; an earlier partial/serialized version circulated from 2018 [FACT for 2021 dating, https://kevinacarson.org; BACKGROUND for 2018 circulation].

- Counter-economics generalized: builds on Samuel Edward Konkin III's agorist counter-economics but widens it beyond illegal markets to the household/informal sector, mutual aid, commons-based peer production, alternative currency, community land trusts, squatting, encrypted exchange [BACKGROUND].
- "Secession of the productive class": rather than capturing political power or persuading majorities, withdraw dependence on the capitalist-statist nexus; meet an increasing share of needs through self-provisioning, cooperative, and commons institutions, starving the old system of labor, rent, and legitimacy [BACKGROUND].
- Abundance economics: collapsing marginal cost of information goods plus collapsing capital cost of micro-production tools plus commons-based peer production pushes toward post-scarcity conditions. The state's function in late capitalism is to artificially re-impose scarcity (IP, zoning, licensing, credentialism) to keep the rent-extraction model viable. Political conflict is scarcity-enforcement versus abundance [BACKGROUND].
- Against the "fully automated luxury communism" and accelerationist left: abundance will not be delivered by state-managed automation but by destroying legal barriers to already-existing cheap production; UBI-style transfers treat symptoms while leaving the extraction structure intact [BACKGROUND].
- Transition mechanics: crisis of the old order is structural (fiscal exhaustion, declining profit rates as tools cheapen, debt, precarity); the counter-economy is both lifeboat and successor. Relocalized food, energy microgrids, local manufacturing, mutualist credit make exit credible rather than suicidal. Relative to earlier work: heavier reliance on Ostrom-style commons governance, less on 19th-century value-theory polemics [BACKGROUND; the shift INTERPRETATION].

### 3.7 The polemical framing, condensed

- "Capitalism is not the free market": the two are not merely distinct but opposed; the free market is the negation of capitalism. Historical claim: no stage of capitalism approximated laissez-faire; classical free traders never needed thousand-page regulatory harmonization texts like NAFTA/GATT [FACT for the NAFTA/GATT passage, verified in MPE ch. 7 excerpt].
- Vulgar libertarianism as the diagnostic term for apologetics that treat rigged outcomes as market outcomes [BACKGROUND].
- Feud with the Mises Institute wing: he holds Austrian subjectivism, consistently applied, supports mutualist conclusions; the Austrians stop applying it at the factory gate. Mises Institute writers have reciprocated by attacking him as a Marxist infiltrator [BACKGROUND, episode-level details unverified].

## 4. The ecosystem: C4SS and left-market anarchism

### 4.1 C4SS

Live mission statement, verbatim from the about page: "to explain and defend the idea of vibrant social cooperation without aggression, oppression, or centralized authority" [FACT, https://c4ss.org/about, fetched 2026-10-12]. Founded 2006 in the milieu of the Molinari Institute and the Alliance of the Libertarian Left [BACKGROUND].

The CC0 stance is institutionally consonant with Carson's anti-IP work [INTERPRETATION].

### 4.2 Markets Not Capitalism and the school

- Markets Not Capitalism: Individualist Anarchism Against Bosses, Inequality, Corporate Power, and Structural Poverty (2011, Autonomedia/Minor Compositions) pairs historical individualist anarchist texts (Tucker, Spooner, de Cleyre, Lum, Hodgskin) with contemporary market anarchist essays [FACT for title/publisher/year; structure BACKGROUND]. Bibliographic correction: the standard record credits Gary Chartier and Charles W. Johnson as co-editors; Carson is a lead contributor whose essays anchor the contemporary section, not an editor [FACT, flagged against the common misattribution].
- Core membership of the school: Carson (mutualist economics and organization theory), Roderick Long (Molinari Institute, thick libertarianism), Charles Johnson (Rad Geek, Austrian-inflected market anarchism), Gary Chartier (Anarchy and Legal Order), Sheldon Richman (popularizer of the phrase "free-market anti-capitalism"). Second ring: David D'Amato, Chris Sciabarra, Jeff Riggenbach, Cory Massimino, plus the C4SS chair-holders. Frictional allies: David Friedman, the Bleeding Heart Libertarians, Steve Horwitz [BACKGROUND].
- The school's founding gesture is definitional: "capitalism" names actually existing political economy (state-granted privilege), "markets" name exchange relationships; one can be pro-market and anti-capitalist without contradiction. Historical claim: Tucker and Spooner were socialists in their own vocabulary [INTERPRETATION].
- Distinguishing marks versus right-libertarianism: the subsidy-of-history revisionist account (E.P. Thompson, Kolko, Weinstein as shared references); the vulgar-libertarianism critique; thick libertarianism (feminism, anti-racism, labor solidarity as integral, not optional); strategic sympathy with wildcat unionism and worker self-organization [BACKGROUND].
- In-house statement of the anti-ancap line: the C4SS feature "Anarcho-'Capitalism' is Impossible" (Anna Morgenstern, 2010) argues that under anarchy protection costs scale with property dispersion, state-chartered banking concentrates capital, and IP-dependent business models collapse, so anything recognizable as capitalism could not survive; "police and military protection is the biggest subsidy that the State gives to the rich" [FACT, https://c4ss.org/content/4043, fetched 2026-10-12].
- The deepest disagreement with Rothbardian anarcho-capitalism is not about the state but about class: ancaps see class analysis as Marxist residue; C4SS writers see class rule as the thing states are for [INTERPRETATION].

## 5. The discourse: debates and critics

### 5.1 Caplan's review

- Bryan Caplan reviewed Studies on EconLog in 2007. The exact URL could not be fetched this session (econlib URL structure broken; verify via Wayback against econlog.econlib.org, expected May 2007) [BACKGROUND, unverified].
- Substance as remembered: Caplan credits prodigious reading and treats the book as a serious achievement, but rejects the economics on three grounds: the LTV rehabilitation fails (value is subjective; the cost principle is neither derivable nor empirically supported); the claim that intervention systematically redistributes upward is overstated; the subsidy-of-history thesis cannot bear the counterfactual weight assigned to it (granting historical robbery does not show current outcomes are fragile to withdrawal of privilege) [BACKGROUND].
- Significance: the review marks the high-water line of engagement between the Austrian-libertarian mainstream and the mutualist revival; after 2007-2009 the milieux sorted into separate audiences and citation networks [INTERPRETATION].

### 5.2 Austrian objections to the labor-value rehabilitation

- Mengerian marginalism already explains price gravitating toward cost without labor metaphysics; reintroducing "labor" as cost-substance re-imports the measurability problems marginalism dissolved [BACKGROUND].
- Labor disutility is not homogeneous across persons or occupations, so "labor cost" cannot function as a unit; Carson's theory silently requires cardinal intersubjective comparison [BACKGROUND].
- Interest: Austrians hold time preference is real and universal; free banking would still clear at positive interest. Carson's Proudhonian/Gesellian monetary sympathy reads to them as greenbacker crankery [BACKGROUND].
- Rothbard himself classified the Tuckerites as confused individualists; Studies is partly an answer to that dismissal, which is why Rothbardian engagement carries lineage heat [BACKGROUND].
- Structural note: the two sides talk past each other. Critics read Part One as price theory (a doctrine settled in the 1870s); defenders read it as institutional analysis, with the LTV functioning as a tendential equilibrium claim about freed markets, not a theory of individual prices [INTERPRETATION].

### 5.3 Critiques of the political-economy-of-waste thesis

- Measurement: the waste claims are asserted more than measured; the evidence is historical-institutional case material, not econometric estimation, and no accepted quantification of a "waste share" of GDP exists in his framework [BACKGROUND].
- Counterfactual: the relocalization claim assumes away genuine economies of scale (semiconductor fabrication, container shipping, aircraft) not obviously artifacts of subsidy [BACKGROUND].
- Organization theory: transaction-cost economists explain firm scale as economizing; critics note Carson treats all large firms as state artifacts, licensing him to ignore that literature except as ideology [BACKGROUND].
- From the left: Marxists and degrowthers may accept the waste diagnosis but reject the remedy, arguing freed markets would reproduce concentration through differential accumulation; Carson's reply leans on enforcement-cost asymmetries that make concentration unprofitable without subsidy [BACKGROUND].
- Sources he draws on for the thesis: Ivan Illich (radical monopoly, counterproductivity), Seymour Melman (permanent war economy), Paul Goodman, Ralph Borsodi (distribution costs), Kirkpatrick Sale, Leopold Kohr, Schumacher [BACKGROUND].
- The waste thesis is his most influential idea outside libertarianism, overlapping degrowth, the Illich revival, and commons/P2P literature; inside economics it remains untested and largely unread [INTERPRETATION].

### 5.4 Land: occupancy-and-use versus absentee ownership

- The mutualist position: legitimate land tenure requires personal occupancy and use; absentee landlordism depends on state enforcement of title against occupants and would collapse without it [BACKGROUND].
- Rothbardian critique: occupancy-and-use is arbitrary in boundary cases, unenforceable without quasi-state adjudication, and destructive of improvement incentives. Georgist critique: rent is locational scarcity, not an enforcement artifact; abolishing title enforcement redistributes rent to de facto occupants rather than abolishing it. Historical critique: even semi-stateless orders (medieval Iceland, mining camps, claims clubs) developed transferable absentee claims [BACKGROUND].
- Carson's response: current titles derive from state enclosure, so Rothbardian just-title fails on its own terms for nearly all real land; enforcement-cost asymmetry means absentee claims are viable only where third parties socialize protection costs [BACKGROUND; the enforcement-cost argument confirmed in-house FACT via the Morgenstern piece].
- The land debate is where school boundaries are sharpest; no empirical adjudication exists because the counterfactual (stateless large-scale absentee property) has no clean cases [INTERPRETATION].

### 5.5 Recent positions (2015 to 2026)

- Electoral: both US parties as management factions of state capitalism (Democrats administer the welfare-regulatory stabilization layer, Republicans the extractive-authoritarian layer); Trump as product of the post-Nixon coalition, not aberration; Biden-era industrial policy (CHIPS, IRA) as renewed corporate subsidy with a green and semiconductor face [BACKGROUND, specific essay titles unverified].
- IP: the central subsidy of the new economy. ### 5.6 Standing in the landscape

- Verdict: influential-but-niche. The most systematic living mutualist political economist and the central theorist of left-market anarchism; no academic position; movement presses; essentially uncited in mainstream economics [INTERPRETATION].
- Who reads him: the C4SS/ALL milieu (house theory); the broader left-libertarian readership (FFF audience, occasional Cato Unbound symposia); the P2P/commons milieu (Michel Bauwens's P2P Foundation carries his work regularly; Homebrew circulates in maker, fab-lab, and open-hardware communities); heterodox academics in organization studies, geography, STS, critical management studies; anarchist studies scholars engage him as the leading contemporary mutualist. Notably absent: mainstream economics departments, policy think tanks, and the organized socialist left [BACKGROUND].
- Citation footprint: Studies shows low hundreds of Google Scholar citations (exact count unverified), concentrated in anarchist studies, political theory, and critical management studies, not economics journals [BACKGROUND].
- Left reception: Marxists and anarcho-communists tend to dismiss mutualism as petty-bourgeois market romanticism, updating Engels's anti-Proudhon line; Carson's standing reply is that anti-market anarchists concede the state's equation of markets with capitalism [BACKGROUND].
- Structural reasons for the niche: his work sits on a three-way border (Austrian economics, classical Marxism, anarchist history) where every neighboring discipline regards at least one premise as refuted; indispensable inside the school, ignorable outside it. Ideas travel horizontally through networks rather than vertically through credentials [INTERPRETATION].

## 6. Where the mapping breaks

1. The value-theory gambit is the weakest load-bearing wall. If the LTV rehabilitation fails (and the professional consensus since the 1870s is that it does), Part One of MPE collapses into the institutional history of Part Two, which does not strictly need it. Carson's defenders say the LTV is a tendential equilibrium claim, not price theory; his critics say that is exactly the retreat marginalism forced a century ago. Unresolved, and the main reason academic economics does not engage him [INTERPRETATION].
2. The waste thesis is unmeasured. There is no accepted quantification of the "waste share" of GDP in his framework; the evidence is case material. The thesis is powerful as institutional history and weak as estimation [BACKGROUND].
3. The relocalization counterfactual assumes away genuine economies of scale. Semiconductor fabs, container shipping, and aircraft have fixed costs not obviously created by subsidy. Carson's answer (that even these are smaller than observed firm scale) may be right about firm boundaries while being wrong about plant boundaries [BACKGROUND; framing INTERPRETATION].
4. The occupancy-and-use land theory has never faced its boundary cases. How long an absence voids title, who adjudicates, why improve land you can lose: the Rothbardian and Georgist objections stand unanswered at the level of mechanism, and the historical record (mining camps, claims clubs) cuts against the enforcement-cost claim as often as for it [BACKGROUND].
5. The exodus strategy has no demonstrated scaling path. Counter-institutions exist (cooperatives, land trusts, mutual credit) but none has come close to the "secession of the productive class" at a scale that would starve the old system; the strongest real-world cases (Mondragon, platform co-ops) are islands inside capitalism, not secessions from it [INTERPRETATION].
6. The roster discrepancy. His own site still claims the C4SS Senior Fellow title and Karl Hess Chair; the current C4SS public roster does not list him. Whether this is lag or an actual institutional change is unresolved, and it matters for assessing his current institutional standing [FACT, both sides fetched 2026-10-12].
7. The anti-IP bridge cuts both ways. His strongest crossover idea (abolish IP and platform/pharma business models collapse) is also where he is most exposed: the claim that all platform monopoly is IP-plus-subsidy artifact underplays network effects and switching costs that would persist in a stateless market [INTERPRETATION].
8. Provenance caveat: large sections of the works-level summaries (Organization Theory, Homebrew, Desktop Regulatory State, Exodus) rest on training knowledge, not this session's extraction; only MPE was verified against full text. Chapter-level claims for the other four books should be re-extracted from the C4SS/kevinacarson.org PDFs before any direct quotation [FACT about this file's own provenance].

## 7. Compression points for synthesis

1. Carson's core move is definitional: "capitalism" names state-rigged class rule, "markets" name uncoerced exchange; the free market is the negation of capitalism, and genuinely freed markets would be socialist in outcome (labor keeps its product).
2. The subsidy of history: every stage of capitalism, from enclosure through monopoly capital to neoliberal globalization, depended on state violence or subsidy; the invisible hand has an iron fist inside it. Exploitation is persistent deviation of price from cost enforced by privilege.
3. His value theory attempts a mutualist synthesis: the classical cost principle as a limiting case of subjective value theory, with interest as a scarcity rent on state-cartelized credit rather than a return to waiting. This is the part professional economics rejects.
4. Organization Theory turns Hayek against the firm: hierarchies cannot aggregate tacit knowledge, economies of scale are bounded and then reverse, and observed firm size reflects subsidy, not efficiency. The CEO is structurally the Gosplan official.
5. The Homebrew thesis: cheap digitally controlled production tools plus open design ecology invert the capital-intensity logic of mass production; IP is the corporate state's counter-revolution against relocalized production. Low overhead collapses production and consumption units.
6. IP is his bridge idea: intellectual property as the late-capitalist analogue of enclosure, fencing the new commons of design and information because abundance is fatal to rent. Abolish IP and platform/pharma business models collapse. This is also where Stross's Accelerando meets economics.
7. Strategy is exodus, not capture: build the successor society in the interstices of the dying one (mutual credit, land trusts, commons, relocalized production) and starve the old system of labor, rent, and legitimacy. Konkin's counter-economics generalized.
8. The school: C4SS (founded 2006, CC0 everything, syndication model) is the institutional vehicle; Markets Not Capitalism (2011, edited by Chartier and Johnson, not Carson) is the canon-in-one-volume; the school splits from right-libertarianism over class, not over the state.
9. Standing: influential-but-niche; house theorist of left-market anarchism, read in P2P/commons and heterodox organization studies, unread in mainstream economics after the 2007-2009 Caplan exchange window.
10. The honest weaknesses: the LTV rehabilitation, the unmeasured waste thesis, the relocalization counterfactual, the unanswered land-tenure boundary cases, and the undemonstrated scaling path of exodus.

# NASA Planetary Science Programs Overview

## Leading the Exploration of Our Solar System

NASA's Planetary Science Programs and Initiatives lead the exploration of our solar system to better understand the origins of celestial bodies and discover whether signs of life exist elsewhere.

## Program Categories

### Mars Exploration Program
- The key to understanding the past, present, or future potential for life on Mars.
- Four broad overarching goals for Mars exploration.
- Includes orbiters, landers, rovers, and future sample return missions.

### Discovery Program
- Goal: Provide frequent flight opportunities for high-quality, high-value, focused planetary science investigations under a not-to-exceed cost cap.
- Frequent, steady investigations ensure a continuing stream of fresh scientific data.
- Helps maintain excellence of U.S. planetary science program and inspire next generation of investigators.
- PI-led missions.

### New Frontiers Program
- Designed to accomplish focused planetary science investigations using innovative and efficient management approaches.
- Prime objective: Answer unique science questions in the exploration of the Solar System.
- Proposals solicited via Announcement of Opportunity (AO) process.
- PI-led missions, typically affiliated with universities or research institutions.
- Chosen through extensive competitive peer review process.

### Lunar Discovery and Exploration
- In the Science Mission Directorate (SMD), the Exploration Science Strategy Integration Office (ESSIO) ensures science is infused into all aspects of lunar exploration.
- Uses the Moon as an observation platform.
- Strives to gain greater understanding of the Moon, the solar system, the universe, and the deep space environment.

### Planetary Defense
- The Planetary Defense Coordination Office (PDCO) manages NASA's ongoing mission of finding, tracking, and better understanding asteroids and comets that could pose an impact hazard to Earth.

### Astrobiology
- Looking for life elsewhere in the universe.

## Note on Flagship Missions

A dedicated "Flagship Program" page was not found on science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/programs/. The Flagship classification appears to be used informally or within internal NASA documentation (e.g., NPR 7120.5, Decadal Survey) to refer to the largest, most complex, and most expensive planetary missions (examples include Cassini, Galileo, Voyager, Mars Science Laboratory/Curiosity, Perseverance). However, it does not appear as a named program on the public-facing Planetary Science Programs page alongside Discovery and New Frontiers.

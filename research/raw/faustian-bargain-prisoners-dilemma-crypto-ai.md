Raw research file. Part 4 of 4 in a deep-dive series on the prisoner's dilemma. ## Part A: The Faustian bargain

### 1. The legend arc

- [BACKGROUND] **Historical Johann Georg Faust (c. 1480-1540).** A wandering German scholar, astrologer, and self-styled magician drifting through the Holy Roman Empire. Contemporary references (letters by Johannes Trithemius, 1507; Melanchthon's lectures; city expulsion records from Nuremberg, Ingolstadt, Bamberg) describe a boastful charlatan rumored to have made a compact with the devil. He died violently around 1540, probably in Staufen im Breisgau; legend said the devil broke his neck.
- [BACKGROUND] **The Faustbuch: Historia von D. Johann Fausten (1587).** Anonymous chapbook published in Frankfurt by Johann Spies. First full narrative compilation. Faust, a theologian turned necromancer, summons the demon Mephostophiles at a crossroads near Wittenberg and signs a pact in his own blood: 24 years of demonic service in exchange for his soul, body, and blood at term's end. He receives magical knowledge, travels (including a trip to hell and a summons of Helen of Troy), and is damned; devils tear him apart. Explicitly Protestant-moralist: a cautionary tale against curiosity and apostasy.
- [BACKGROUND] **Marlowe, The Tragical History of Doctor Faustus (c. 1592).** The English Renaissance stage version. Faustus, bored with lawful arts, turns to necromancy and signs in blood; his blood congeals, an omen, and must be warmed to sign. Terms: 24 years of Mephistopheles' service for his soul. He conjures Helen ("Was this the face that launched a thousand ships"), wastes much of the term on pranks. The Good Angel / Bad Angel device externalizes conscience. At midnight the devils come; the final soliloquy ("See, see where Christ's blood streams in the firmament") is the great damned-soul aria of English drama. Damned. Two texts survive (A-text 1604, B-text 1616).
- [BACKGROUND] **Goethe, Faust (Part I 1808, Part II 1832).** The decisive transformation. Goethe's Faust has exhausted all human knowledge and nearly poisons himself before Easter bells recall him to life. Key structural change: the deal is framed as a wager, not a purchase. Faust tells Mephistopheles: if ever he says to the moment, "Verweile doch! Du bist so schön!" ("Stay, you are so beautiful!"), then he may die and the devil may take him. Part I is the Gretchen tragedy. Part II is vast allegorical machinery: imperial court, paper money, the Classical Walpurgis Night, Helen as aesthetic episode, land reclamation, and Faust's death in a moment of anticipatory satisfaction ("Im Vorgefühl von solchem hohen Glück / Genieß' ich jetzt den höchsten Augenblick"). The heavenly host snatches his soul: "Wer immer strebend sich bemüht, / Den können wir erlösen" (whoever strives on, we can redeem). Redeemed. Goethe worked the material from the 1770s (Urfaust) to his death in 1832.
- [BACKGROUND] **Mann, Doktor Faustus (1947).** Thomas Mann transposes the legend into 20th-century Germany. Composer Adrian Leverkühn contracts syphilis deliberately (a coded pact) and in a hallucinated dialogue trades his soul, specifically the capacity for love, for 24 years of transcendent musical genius (twelve-tone technique). Narrated by the humanist Serenus Zeitblom, the novel is explicitly an allegory of Germany's bargain with Nazism: genius purchased at the price of humanity, ending in collapse. Damned, with a late ambiguating gesture toward grace.

The comparative axis, what is traded, what is received, the verdict [BACKGROUND]:

- Faustbuch: soul for knowledge and pleasure; damned.
- Marlowe: soul for 24 years of power; damned.
- Goethe: soul wagered against perfect satisfaction; saved by striving.
- Mann: soul and the capacity to love for artistic genius; damned, with the nation as Faust.

### 2. The structure of the deal

- [BACKGROUND] Standard elements across versions: (a) a conjuration ritual (crossroads, magic circle, solstice night); (b) negotiation of terms; (c) a written contract signed in blood, Faust's congealing blood in Marlowe being the genre's best-known detail; (d) a fixed term of demonic service (canonically 24 years in Faustbuch and Marlowe; indefinite in Goethe because the trigger is the wager condition, not the calendar); (e) the soul as collateral, collectible on death or term's end.
- [BACKGROUND/INTERPRETATION] The soul as collateral: an illiquid, non-fungible, non-recoverable asset. It cannot be fractionally staked, rehypothecated, or clawed back. The contract's asymmetry: Faust's payments (pleasure, knowledge) are consumed continuously and depreciate; the devil's claim is a bullet payment at maturity with no default remedy for Faust.
- [INTERPRETATION] The demon's enforcement problem: the devil is an enforcer without courts. His mechanism is reputation across souls and in-story supernatural violence. In Goethe the devil loses despite apparently winning the wager's letter: enforcement is overridden by a higher court (divine grace). This prefigures the smart-contract fantasy and its limit. Goethe's ending says some contracts get appealed to a jurisdiction the counterparty cannot reach.
- [BACKGROUND] Blood contracts: signature in blood marks the contract as somatic, not merely legal; the signer's own body attests. Blood that congeals (Marlowe) is the document resisting its own execution: the contract's moment of self-warning, ignored.

### 3. The Faustian bargain as modern concept

- [BACKGROUND] The idiom: "a Faustian bargain" is any deal that trades something essential and irrecoverable (integrity, privacy, autonomy, long-run survival) for power, knowledge, or gain in the short run. The idiom presumes the payer systematically undervalues the collateral at signing time.
- [BACKGROUND] Nuclear knowledge: Oppenheimer's "the physicists have known sin" (1947) and the broader atomic-scientist literature framed the bomb as a Faustian transaction: knowledge that cannot be unlearned in exchange for victory and prestige. The rhetorical template for all later technology-as-pact discourse.
- [BACKGROUND] Social media and the attention economy: users trade attention and behavioral data, a resource that once spent does not regenerate as the same self, for free services and dopamine. Frequently described in Faustian terms in tech criticism.
- [BACKGROUND] AI development and acceleration: "Faustian bargain" rhetoric appears in two registers: (a) safety discourse, labs trading caution for capability under competitive pressure; (b) societal, the public trading employment stability, epistemic commons, and possibly control for capability gains. The motif recurs because it names exactly the structure: present consumption, future bullet payment, disputed enforcement.

### 4. Structural mapping (INTERPRETATION, all claims in this section)

- **The soul as one-shot irreversible spend.** The soul is the canonical non-rewritable asset: one unit of account, one possible transaction. This is why the Faust story resonates with irreversibility talk in AI (deployment, weights release) and crypto (key loss, immutable contracts): all three domains feature actions whose cost basis cannot be recovered by any future action of the spender. In PD terms, the soul is a payoff dimension that appears in no cell of the matrix until the game ends; the matrix is a lie of omission.
- **The temptation payoff.** The pact's payoff is front-loaded and experiential; the collateral is back-loaded and abstract at signing. Hyperbolic discounting is built into the myth's structure. Mephistopheles' sales technique is constant: make the term feel long and the payment feel theoretical. Twenty-four years is chosen to exceed the horizon of felt consequence. The Faustian bargain is what T looks like when P is deferred past the discount horizon.
- **The demon's enforcement problem.** Mephistopheles cannot compel performance except by supernatural menace; he has no bailiff. His business model depends on contracts being self-executing in a metaphysical court. Goethe exploits this: the wager's ambiguity (did Faust say it to the moment, or to the anticipated moment?) becomes a literal contract dispute, and the appeals court rules for Faust. Every Faust story is secretly about whether contracts bind when one party is immortal and the other can repent.
- **Wager vs. pact.** Goethe's substitution of the wager for the purchase is the great game-theoretic revision. A purchase transfers the soul at signing; a wager leaves the outcome contingent on Faust's own future states. Damnation becomes endogenous, a function of whether striving stops. Goethe converts a static contract story into a dynamic one: the devil cannot win by holding paper; he can only win by inducing Faust to stop. Salvation is perpetual striving; damnation is declaring the moment sufficient. Mapped forward: the danger in capability acquisition is not the capability but the equilibrium in which acquisition stops being instrumental and becomes the terminal good.
- **The Gretchen line.** The collateral damage of the pact falls on third parties who never signed: Gretchen, Leverkühn's nation. Faustian bargains externalize; the signer spends, others pay. This is the motif's cleanest bridge to Part B: a bargain between two parties that imposes the payoff matrix on everyone else is a coordination failure wearing a contract's clothes.

## Part B: The dilemma in crypto and AI

### 5. Crypto: Moloch, public goods, MEV, staking

- [FACT] The Moloch essay lineage: Ameen Soleimani's 2019 framing of "Moloch, the god of coordination failure" became the Ethereum community's canonical name for its enemy. The genealogy is two-step: Scott Alexander's "Meditations on Moloch" (Slate Star Codex, July 2014) personified coordination failure as the Canaanite god; Soleimani imported it into Ethereum discourse and built MolochDAO (launched 2019) as a public-goods funding instrument aimed at it. Soleimani on the Bankless podcast ("Slaying Moloch," Oct 2020): Moloch "defines these prisoner's dilemmas that lead to unfortunate equilibriums that the players can't get out of"; the dominant strategy is "get everyone else to disarm and secretly keep your weapons."
- [FACT at the framing level / BACKGROUND at the game-theoretic level] Public goods funding as n-player PD: Ethereum's public goods (client software, OSS libraries, research) are non-excludable; each actor's dominant strategy is to free-ride, defecting on funding while consuming the good. Owocki's coordination-failure writing explicitly frames OSS maintainer burnout this way.
- [BACKGROUND] Tragedy of the commons in blockchain resources: blockspace, state storage, and mempool attention are commons-like; individually rational consumption (spam, state bloat) degrades the shared resource. Fee markets (EIP-1559's base-fee burn) are mechanism-design responses that internalize the externality price.
- [INTERPRETATION on BACKGROUND facts] MEV as defection: validators and searchers reordering, inserting, or censoring transactions for profit is defection against the implicit cooperation of "process transactions fairly as submitted." The Flashbots "MEV as existential threat" argument treats unchecked extraction as a race to the bottom that could destabilize consensus: a multiplayer PD where each extractor's rational defection worsens the shared chain.
- [BACKGROUND] Slashing as engineered punishment payoff: proof-of-stake protocols impose slashing (destruction of staked ETH) for provable misbehavior (double-signing, surround voting). In payoff terms, slashing rewrites the matrix: defection's payoff is made strictly negative, converting a PD into a game where cooperation is dominant. This is mechanism design as a contract with a Mephistopheles that actually collects: automatic, no appeals court.
- [INTERPRETATION on BACKGROUND facts] Staking and bonding as commitment devices: locking capital is a Schelling-style commitment that raises the cost of future defection ex ante; the staker voluntarily hands the protocol a hostage. Structurally this is the inverse of the Faustian pact. Faust posts his soul as collateral for present gain; the staker posts collateral to prove future cooperation. Both are one-sided collateralized promises; the difference is which direction time's arrow of payment runs.
- [FACT at the framing level / BACKGROUND at the mechanism level] Gitcoin and quadratic funding as cooperation technology: Gitcoin Grants used quadratic funding (Lalley-Weyl-Buterin "Liberal Radicalism" lineage, 2018) to fund Ethereum public goods; matching pools amplify many small donations over few large ones, making the funding game reward breadth of cooperation rather than wealth. Owocki's piece cites over $10M routed to OSS via Gitcoin Grants in its first two years.

### 6. AI: racing dynamics and multi-agent cooperation

- [BACKGROUND] Arms race as PD, the standard safety-discourse frame: labs choose between racing ahead (defect) and coordinated caution (cooperate). If all labs exercise caution, safety research keeps pace and risk stays bounded. But each lab's unilateral incentive is to race: if others slow down, the racer gains capability lead and market share; if others race, caution means losing. The payoff ordering matches PD: temptation > mutual cooperation > mutual defection > sucker.
- [FACT] The racing dynamics critique: Askell, Brundage, and Hadfield, "The Role of Cooperation in Responsible AI Development," arXiv:1907.04534, submitted July 10, 2019. The paper argues competitive pressures could incentivize AI developers to cut corners on safety, analyzes cooperation opportunities among AI actors, and proposes mechanisms (trust, verification, norms) to avoid races.
- [INTERPRETATION] Safety-washing and unilateral defection incentives: public safety commitments are cheap talk in a PD; they cost nothing to announce and everything to honor under pressure. The verification problem is the crux: a lab can safety-wash (signal cooperation while defecting on the margin) because safety investment is less observable than capability output. This is the demon's enforcement problem inverted: here it is the cooperators who lack a court, and the defectors who write press releases. Pauses, evals regimes, and third-party audits are attempts to build the missing enforcer.
- [BACKGROUND] Multi-agent AI systems: the multi-agent RL literature (iterated PD tournaments since Axelrod; deep-RL social dilemmas, e.g. the Sequential Social Dilemmas work circa 2017, Leibo et al.) shows cooperation and defection emerging as learned strategies in agent populations, with outcomes sensitive to discount factors, population structure, and punishment availability. Forward concerns: collusion between trading agents, stigmergic coordination failures among deployed systems, and the possibility that AI systems discover cooperative equilibria humans didn't design, or defect against human principals collectively.
- [BACKGROUND/INTERPRETATION] Superrationality (Hofstadter) as attempted escape: reasoners who know they are symmetric should reason their way to the symmetric cooperative outcome, since each knows the other will reach the same conclusion (Metamagical Themas, 1980s, following his Scientific American columns on the one-shot PD). Superrationality tries to dissolve the PD by logical correlation rather than enforcement; it is the intellectual ancestor of later acausal-cooperation ideas in the rationalist sphere. [INTERPRETATION] Superrationality is the one escape hatch requiring no Mephistopheles: no external enforcer, no slashing, no bonds. Its weakness is the blood contract's own: it binds only agents that actually reason alike, and nothing collects from the ones that don't.

### Synthesis: the two myths

- [INTERPRETATION] Faust and Moloch are the two faces of the same failure. Moloch is the multiplayer trap with no contract: everyone defects, no one signed anything, the system endures unconquered. Faust is the two-party trap with too much contract: one signer, one counterparty, binding terms, third parties paying. Crypto's tooling (slashing, staking, quadratic funding) tries to engineer Moloch-games into cooperation; the risk, per the Faust template, is that the engineered enforcement becomes its own bargain: liquidity locked, sovereignty posted, the protocol as the counterparty that always collects. AI's racing dynamics are Moloch wearing lab logos; every proposed remedy (coordination, treaties, audits) is an attempt to build the court that Goethe gave his Faust and that Marlowe's Faust prayed for at midnight.

## 7. Where the mapping breaks

- The Faust-to-PD mapping is analogy, not identity. The pact is a two-party contract with asymmetric information and supernatural enforcement; the PD is a simultaneous-move game with no enforcement at all. The kinship is structural (front-loaded gain, back-loaded cost, enforcement problems, externalities), not formal.
- The soul metaphor smuggles in a religious anthropology the game does not need. Game theory explains defection without any doctrine of the essential self; the Faust frame adds stakes language ("what cannot be recovered") that is rhetorically powerful but analytically reducible to irreversibility plus scale.
- "Moloch" in crypto discourse is doing political work: naming coordination failure as the enemy presupposes the community's preferred solutions (new funding mechanisms, new DAOs) are the good equilibrium. The frame can launder governance choices into destiny.
- The AI-race-as-PD frame is contested. Critics note the labs are not symmetric players, safety and capability are not cleanly separable moves, and the frame itself can accelerate races by making defection legible as "what game theory says we must do." Framing effects (see the psychology file in this series) apply to the discourse itself.
- Slashing and staking solve on-chain PDs by making defection costly, but they only govern what the chain can observe. The observable-action subset of agent behavior is small; most real defection (censorship, off-chain collusion, safety-washing) happens where the protocol cannot see it.
- Superrationality and acausal cooperation remain fringe: no empirical evidence that real agents (human or artificial) coordinate this way at scale, and the approach offers no recourse against parties that simply decline the premises.

## 8. Compression points for synthesis

1. The Faust legend runs a stable arc: historical charlatan (c. 1480-1540) to Faustbuch 1587 (damned) to Marlowe c. 1592 (damned) to Goethe 1808/1832 (redeemed) to Mann 1947 (damned, Germany allegory). [BACKGROUND]
2. Goethe's decisive structural move is replacing the soul-purchase with a wager: damnation becomes contingent on Faust declaring a moment sufficient, making it endogenous to his striving rather than a calendar default. [BACKGROUND]
3. The blood-signed pact is a collateralized contract where the collateral (the soul) is indivisible, illiquid, and non-recoverable: the archetype of one-shot irreversible spend. [INTERPRETATION on BACKGROUND facts]
4. The Faustian bargain idiom in technology discourse (nuclear knowledge, attention economy, AI acceleration) names a consistent structure: front-loaded payoff, back-loaded abstract collateral, systematic undervaluation at signing, externalized damage to non-signatories. [INTERPRETATION]
5. Soleimani's 2019 Moloch framing imported Scott Alexander's 2014 "Meditations on Moloch" into Ethereum, explicitly naming coordination failure as "prisoner's dilemmas that lead to unfortunate equilibriums." [FACT]
6. Ethereum public goods funding is framed by its own community (Owocki, Gitcoin) as an n-player prisoner's dilemma where free-riding is dominant and quadratic funding is a cooperation mechanism. [FACT framing]
7. Slashing and staking are mechanism design that rewrites payoff matrices: slashing makes defection strictly negative; bonded collateral is a commitment device, structurally an inverse Faustian pact (collateral posted to prove future cooperation rather than to purchase present power). [INTERPRETATION on BACKGROUND facts]
8. Askell, Brundage, and Hadfield's "The Role of Cooperation in Responsible AI Development" (arXiv:1907.04534, July 10, 2019) is the canonical analysis of competitive pressure degrading AI safety and of cooperation mechanisms as remedy. [FACT]
9. AI lab racing dynamics match PD payoff ordering, and safety-washing is cheap-talk defection enabled by the unobservability of safety investment. [INTERPRETATION on BACKGROUND facts]
10. Hofstadter's superrationality is the only proposed PD escape requiring no external enforcer, cooperation via logical correlation among symmetric reasoners, and shares the blood contract's weakness: it binds only those who play by its premises. [INTERPRETATION on BACKGROUND facts]
11. Faust and Moloch are complementary failure modes: Moloch is the multiplayer trap with no contract; Faust is the two-party trap with a binding contract whose costs fall on non-signatories. Crypto builds enforcers against Moloch; AI safety discourse hunts for a court against racing. Goethe's grace scene is the mythic template of an appeal beyond the counterparty's jurisdiction. [INTERPRETATION]

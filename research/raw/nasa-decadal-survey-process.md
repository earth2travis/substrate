# National Academies Decadal Survey Process for NASA Planetary Science

## 1. What Is a Decadal Survey?

A **decadal survey** is a 10-year strategic plan outlining scientific missions, research priorities, and investment recommendations, produced by the United States National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine (NASEM) on behalf of NASA and other U.S. government agencies such as the National Science Foundation (NSF). It is a consensus study report that identifies key questions facing a scientific discipline and outlines recommendations for space and ground-based exploration ten years into the future. The Planetary Science Decadal Survey is one of several decadal surveys, alongside those covering astronomy and astrophysics, earth science, and heliophysics/solar and space physics.

The surveys are published by the National Academies Press (NAP) and represent the evidence-based consensus of committees of experts. Each report undergoes a rigorous, independent peer-review process and represents the position of the National Academies on the study's statement of task.

## 2. How Decadal Surveys Are Convened and Organized

### Convening Authority and Sponsorship

Decadal surveys are **sponsored by NASA** (and in the case of planetary science, also by the **National Science Foundation**). NASA's Science Mission Directorate (SMD) initiates the process by requesting the National Academies to conduct the study. The study is executed as a **Consensus/Advisory Activity** under Section 15 of the Federal Advisory Committee Act (FACA).

### Organizational Structure

The survey is organized by the **Space Studies Board (SSB)** within the National Academies' Division on Engineering and Physical Sciences (DEPS). The SSB establishes a **Survey Steering Committee** (also called the Survey Committee or Steering Group) composed of approximately 15-20 experts from the planetary science and astrobiology community. The committee is carefully balanced across disciplines, institutions, career stages, and demographics.

For the **2023-2032 Planetary Science and Astrobiology Decadal Survey**, the co-chairs were:
- **Robin M. Canup**, Assistant Vice President of the Planetary Sciences Directorate at the Southwest Research Institute
- **Philip R. Christensen**, Regents Professor at Arizona State University

The steering committee membership is selected and posted publicly on the National Academies website. For the 2023-2032 survey, 16 members were posted by September 2020, with additions through late 2020.

### Panel Structure

The steering committee is advised by **multiple topical panels** that provide expert input on specific sub-disciplines. For the 2023-2032 survey, six panels advised the steering committee. Based on the meeting records, these panels covered:

1. **Panel on Giant Planet Systems**
2. **Panel on Venus**
3. **Panel on Ocean Worlds and Dwarf Planets**
4. **Panel on Mars**
5. **Panel on Mercury and the Moon**
6. **Panel on Small Solar System Bodies**

Each panel held numerous meetings (some with 20+ meetings over the course of the survey) with both open and closed sessions, featuring invited speakers, community presentations, and deliberations.

### Statement of Task

NASA and NSF provide a formal **Statement of Task** that defines the scope of the survey. For the 2023-2032 survey, the task included:
- Assessing key scientific questions in planetary science and astrobiology
- Identifying priority medium- and large-class missions and other initiatives
- Presenting a comprehensive research strategy for the 2023-2032 timeframe
- For the first time, incorporating planetary defense as an element of NASA's planetary science endeavors
- Taking into account planned human space exploration activities
- Addressing relevant programmatic and implementation issues for both NASA and NSF
- Addressing the state of the profession, including diversity, inclusion, equity, and accessibility (DEIA)

Critically, the statement of task requires that recommendations be achievable within anticipated funding boundaries, which NASA and NSF provide at survey initiation.

## 3. Timeline from Convening to Final Report

### Historical Context

As of 2022, there have been three planetary science decadal surveys:
- **2003-2013**: *New Frontiers in the Solar System: An Integrated Exploration Strategy* (published 2002)
- **2013-2022**: *Visions and Voyages for Planetary Science in the Decade 2013-2022* (published March 7, 2011)
- **2023-2032**: *Origins, Worlds, and Life: A Decadal Strategy for Planetary Science and Astrobiology 2023-2032* (published April 19, 2022)

### 2023-2032 Survey Timeline

The 2023-2032 survey followed a roughly **2-year timeline** from kickoff to publication:

| Phase | Timeline | Details |
|-------|----------|---------|
| **Convening & Committee Formation** | Early-mid 2020 | Co-chairs announced (May 2020), members posted (September 2020), panels established |
| **Community Input / White Papers** | April - October 2020 | Call for community white papers; hundreds submitted |
| **Panel Meetings & Deliberations** | October 2020 - August 2021 | Six panels held 10-40+ meetings each with open and closed sessions |
| **Steering Committee Meetings** | July 2020 - December 2021 | 23+ steering committee meetings to synthesize panel input and draft report |
| **Writing & Drafting** | Late 2021 - Early 2022 | Multiple writing groups (Mars, R&A, Human Exploration, State of Profession) |
| **Peer Review** | Early 2022 | Independent expert review of draft report |
| **Publication** | April 19, 2022 | Public release event and report publication by NAP |

The process involves **hundreds of meetings** — the 2023-2032 survey held over 40 events documented on the National Academies website, including panel meetings, steering committee meetings, writing group meetings, and community webinars.

## 4. Community Input Process

### White Papers

The community input process is the foundation of the Decadal Survey. The primary mechanism is the submission of **white papers** by members of the scientific community. For the 2023-2032 survey:

- White papers were solicited from **April to October 2020**
- The survey received **hundreds of white papers**
- The National Academies provided specifications and an FAQ for white paper authors
- Submitted white papers were reviewed by the steering committee and panels
- The full list of submitted community white papers was made publicly available

White papers address proposed missions, research activities, technology development, theoretical advances, laboratory studies, and other initiatives relevant to planetary science, astrobiology, and planetary defense.

### Additional Community Engagement Mechanisms

Beyond white papers, the survey draws on community input through:
- **Invited speakers** at panel and steering committee meetings
- **Outreach to advisory groups** (e.g., NASA advisory committees)
- **Professional society conferences** (e.g., presentations at AGU, LPSC, AAS/DPS)
- **Work with mission-design teams** (NASA centers and laboratories develop detailed mission concept studies)
- **Town hall meetings** and community briefings at major conferences
- **Early career webinars** (the 2023-2032 survey held multiple webinars specifically for early career professionals)
- **Public comment periods** during open sessions of panel meetings

### Early Career Engagement

The 2023-2032 survey specifically included outreach to early career professionals, including:
- A kick-off webinar (March 2020)
- A webinar on writing white papers (May 2020)
- A webinar on perspectives and lessons learned from prior decadal surveys (July 2020)
- Application-based early career participation options

## 5. How Recommendations Become NASA Priorities

### Consensus and Independence

The recommendations emerge from the **consensus deliberations** of the steering committee, informed by panel advice and community input. The National Academies process is designed to ensure:

- **Objectivity**: Grounded in facts, evidence, and impartial analysis
- **Independence**: As a private, nongovernmental institution, the National Academies maintain full autonomy from sponsors and other interested parties
- **Balance**: The committee composition is carefully balanced across disciplines, perspectives, and institutions
- **Excellence**: Rigorous, evidence-based processes with expert peer review

### The Report's Role

The Decadal Survey report serves as the **authoritative community consensus** on scientific priorities. NASA's Planetary Science Division (PSD) uses the report to guide its strategic investments. While NASA is not legally bound to follow every recommendation, the Decadal Survey is widely treated as the **definitive roadmap** for the field.

NASA typically issues a **formal response** to the Decadal Survey, outlining which recommendations it accepts, which require further study, and how it plans to implement the prioritized mission portfolio within budget constraints. The response addresses:
- Flagship mission prioritization
- New Frontiers and Discovery program direction
- Research and Analysis (R&A) funding levels
- Technology development investments
- Planetary defense activities

### Budget Considerations

The Decadal Survey operates within a **budget-constrained framework**. The Statement of Task explicitly requires that recommendations be achievable within anticipated funding. For the 2023-2032 survey, the report recommended a balanced portfolio across three cost classes:
- **Small-class**: Discovery program (PI-led missions, ~$500M cap, high cadence)
- **Medium-class**: New Frontiers program (PI-led missions, ~$1B cap)
- **Large-class**: Flagship missions (~$2-5B, addressing broad, high-priority science)

## 6. Relationship Between Decadal Survey and Flagship Mission Selection

### Flagship Missions as Top Priorities

The Decadal Survey's most visible and consequential recommendations are its **priority Flagship missions** — large-scale, billion-dollar-class missions that address the broadest and most transformative science questions. These missions are ranked in order of priority, and NASA typically uses this ranking as the basis for its strategic mission planning.

### Historical Examples

| Decadal Survey | Priority 1 Flagship | Priority 2 Flagship | Status |
|----------------|---------------------|---------------------|--------|
| 2003-2013 | Mars Science Laboratory | Europa Geophysical Explorer | MSL (Curiosity) flew; Europa became Europa Clipper |
| 2013-2022 | Mars 2020 (MAX-C caching rover) | Europa Clipper/Jupiter Europa Orbiter | Mars 2020 (Perseverance) flew; Europa Clipper in development |
| 2023-2032 | Mars Sample Return (ongoing) + Uranus Orbiter and Probe | Enceladus Orbilander | MSR in development; UOP and Orbilander in study phase |

### The 2023-2032 Flagship Priorities

The *Origins, Worlds, and Life* report made the following Flagship recommendations:

1. **Continue Mars Sample Return (MSR)** as the highest priority overall — reaffirming the prior decadal's recommendation, but cautioning that MSR costs should not undermine long-term programmatic balance. If costs increase substantially, NASA should seek budget augmentation.

2. **Uranus Orbiter and Probe (UOP)** as the highest priority **new** Flagship mission. The UOP would conduct a multiyear orbital tour of Uranus, deliver an atmospheric probe, and study the ice giant system through flybys of major moons. A launch in the 2023-2032 decade is viable on currently available launch vehicles.

3. **Enceladus Orbilander** as the second-highest priority new Flagship mission. This mission would orbit Enceladus for 1.5 years, sampling its water plumes, then land for a 2-year surface mission to search for evidence of life. Proposed launch: late 2030s.

### Selection Process

NASA uses the Decadal Survey rankings to inform its **mission concept studies**, **budget requests to Congress**, and **announcement of opportunity** timelines. The process typically flows as follows:

1. Decadal Survey publishes ranked Flagship priorities
2. NASA conducts preliminary mission concept studies (often led by NASA centers or JPL)
3. NASA includes priority missions in its budget requests and strategic plans
4. Congress appropriates funding for mission formulation and development
5. NASA issues formal announcements and selects implementation teams
6. Mission progresses through Phase A (concept study) through Phase D (operations)

### Budget and Launch Constraints

Real-world factors can affect Decadal Survey implementation:
- **Budget constraints**: Flagship missions are expensive; only one or two can typically proceed per decade
- **Technology readiness**: Some missions require technology maturation before implementation
- **Launch windows**: Planetary missions depend on specific gravity-assist windows
- **Infrastructure**: Availability of launch vehicles, radioisotope power systems, and Deep Space Network capacity

For example, the Uranus Orbiter and Probe originally targeted a 2031 launch, but in 2023 NASA announced that due to plutonium-238 production shortfalls, a mid-to-late 2030s launch was more likely.

## 7. New Frontiers and Discovery Programs

### New Frontiers (Medium-Class Missions)

The Decadal Survey also recommends **mission themes** for the New Frontiers program. For the 2023-2032 survey, recommended New Frontiers mission themes include:
- Centaur Orbiter and Lander
- Ceres Sample Return
- Comet Surface Sample Return
- Enceladus Multiple Flyby
- Lunar Geophysical Network
- Saturn Probe
- Titan Orbiter
- Venus In Situ Explorer

These themes are chosen based on how well each addresses priority science questions, considering programmatic balance, destination class, cost, technical readiness, and scientific merit.

### Discovery (Small-Class Missions)

The report does not make specific Discovery mission recommendations but strongly supports continuing the Discovery program at a high cadence (two selections per Announcement of Opportunity), noting its important scientific contributions.

## 8. Planetary Defense Integration

The 2023-2032 survey was the first to fully incorporate **planetary defense** as an element of NASA's planetary science portfolio. Recommendations include:
- Full support for the **NEO Surveyor**, a dedicated space-based mid-infrared survey
- Following NEO Surveyor and DART, the highest priority planetary defense demonstration mission should be a rapid-response flyby reconnaissance mission to a 50-100m diameter NEO
- Enhancing NEO detection, tracking, characterization, modeling, and deflection technologies

## 9. State of the Profession

The 2023-2032 survey broke new ground by explicitly addressing the **state of the profession**, including:
- Diversity, equity, inclusion, and accessibility (DEIA)
- Creation of safe workspaces free of hostility and harassment
- Codes of conduct for missions, conferences, and field campaigns
- Data gathering on community demographics and workplace climate
- Engagement of students from underrepresented communities
